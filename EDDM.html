Canonical Definition

The Event Determination Decision Module (EDDM) governs how the
results of Determination Readiness and Determination Conditions
governance are converted into a formal Tax-Relevant Event
Determination Decision.

EDDM determines the governed Event status that follows from the
available readiness, condition, qualification, coherence,
uncertainty, and conflict information at the point of decision.

Its principal transition is:

Governed Determination Condition State

↓

Determination Decision
Governance

↓

Governed Event Determination Decision

EDDM does not determine legal taxability.

It does not determine tax jurisdiction, taxpayer status, taxable
value, Tax Base, Tax Rate, legal tax point, Tax Liability,
exemption, deduction, filing obligation, collection, enforcement,
or any other jurisdiction-specific tax consequence.

Its foundational distinction is:

Event Determination Decision ≠ Tax Decision.

Operational Role

EDDM is the formal decision layer of TREDS.

EDRDM determines whether the Event is ready for formal
Determination.

EDCM determines whether the applicable Determination Conditions
are satisfied.

EDDM then answers:

What formal TREGA™ Determination state should this Event receive?

The Decision may be: Positive; Conditional; Deferred; Rejected;
Ambiguous; Conflicted; or otherwise qualified.

EDDM transforms prior governance into an explicit and traceable
Determination outcome.

Operational Scope

EDDM may govern Event Determination Decisions arising from:

The decision logic remains technology-neutral and

Decision Object

The Decision Object is the governed Event represented through:
the EDRDM Readiness State; the EDCM Condition State; relevant
upstream governance references; material qualifications;
uncertainty indicators; and conflict indicators.

EDDM decides the TREGA™ status of this governed Event.

It does not decide the legal tax status.

Decision Initiation

Decision governance begins when EDCM establishes a sufficiently
governed Determination Condition State.

EDDM shall not begin a formal Decision process where required
condition governance remains absent.

Decision Initiation ≠ Positive Determination.

Decision Eligibility

An Event is decision-eligible where the relevant Readiness and
Condition governance provides enough basis for EDDM to issue a
formal status.

Decision eligibility does not require that the result will be
positive.

A Decision may still be: Deferred; Rejected; Conflicted; or
Conditional.

Decision Eligible ≠ Determination Positive.

Decision Basis

Every Determination Decision shall be linked to its governance
basis.

The Decision basis may include: Readiness State; Condition State;
material qualifications; unresolved issues; conflict state;
uncertainty state; upstream referrals; governance traceability.

A Decision without a reconstructable basis shall not qualify as a
governed Decision.

Decision Output Without Decision Basis ≠ Governed Decision.

Positive Determination

A Positive Determination means that the Event is sufficiently
governed to be formally recognized within TREGA™ as a
Tax-Relevant Event.

It means:

The Event is eligible for downstream jurisdiction-specific
taxation analysis.

It does not mean:

Tax is legally due.

Therefore:

Positive Event Determination ≠ Positive Tax Determination.

Conditional Determination

A Conditional Determination may be issued where the Event is
sufficiently governed for controlled downstream use but one or
more bounded qualifications remain.

These qualifications shall be explicit.

Conditional Determination ≠ Unqualified Determination.

The downstream system shall be able to identify the conditions
attached to the Decision.

Deferred Determination

A Deferred Determination means that the Event cannot yet receive
a final Determination state because materially necessary
governance remains unresolved.

Deferral may result from: unresolved condition; material
uncertainty; material conflict; unstable Event structure;
unresolved upstream referral; or pending structural
reconsideration.

Deferred Determination ≠ Rejected Determination.

Rejected Determination

A Rejected Determination means that the governed Event structure
does not support establishment of a Tax-Relevant Event under
TREGA™ at that time.

Rejection may occur where the apparent Event fails a foundational
governance requirement.

Examples may include: invalid Event Formation; non-establishable
Event Boundary; duplicate technical artifact; purely technical
operation incorrectly treated as an Event; or materially
incoherent Event structure.

Rejected Determination ≠ Legal Non-Taxability.

Ambiguous Determination

A Determination may remain Ambiguous where more than one
materially plausible Event interpretation remains and the
available governance does not support a single stable Decision.

Ambiguity shall remain visible.

Ambiguity ≠ Permission to Choose the Convenient Decision.

Conflicted Determination

A Determination may be Conflicted where materially authoritative
governance inputs support incompatible outcomes.

The Decision shall preserve the conflict rather than collapse it
artificially.

Decision Conflict ≠ Decision Discretion without Governance Basis.

Decision Qualification

A Decision may contain a qualification.

Qualification may relate to: bounded uncertainty; incomplete
geographic precision; limited participant certainty; partial
Value Association; conditional Relationship structure;
provisional source availability; or another materially relevant
limitation.

The qualification shall not disappear when the Event moves
downstream.

Decision Finality

EDDM may establish operational finality sufficient for downstream
processing.

This does not mean absolute or legal finality.

A later correction or reconsideration may change the Decision.

Operational Decision Finality ≠ Legal Finality.

Decision Consistency

EDDM shall apply consistent decision logic across materially
comparable Events.

Consistency does not require identical outcomes where Event
circumstances differ.

Decision Consistency ≠ Mechanical Uniformity.

The governing principle is methodological consistency.

Decision Rationale

A formal Decision shall be accompanied by sufficient rationale to
reconstruct why the Decision state was selected.

The rationale should identify: the Event under review; the
Readiness State; the relevant Condition State; material satisfied
conditions; material unsatisfied or qualified conditions;
material uncertainty; material conflict; and the reason for the
resulting Decision.

Decision Provenance

The Decision shall preserve provenance to the governance
information from which it arose.

This may include references to: upstream Parent Standards; EDRDM
readiness evaluation; EDCM condition evaluation; relevant system
or governance-source identifiers; previous Decision states.

Decision Provenance ≠ Identity Provenance.

OBIDENITY® remains authoritative for broader identity and origin
governance.

Decision Traceability

The Determination Decision shall remain reconstructable.

A reviewer should be able to identify: what was decided; why; on
what governance basis; under which qualifications; with what
uncertainty; and whether the Decision later changed.

EDDM owns Decision traceability.

ADIT® may govern broader evidential or audit requirements.

Decision Authority

The Decision process may be: automated; human; hybrid; delegated;
rules-based; AI-assisted.

EDDM requires the Decision authority or process reference to
remain identifiable.

It does not assign organizational accountability.

AGA™ remains authoritative for accountability governance.

Decision Timing

The time at which the Determination Decision was made should be
captured separately from the Event Timing itself.

Decision Time ≠ Event Time.

and:

Decision Time ≠ Legal Tax Point.

Positive Decision Threshold

A Positive Determination should require that: the Event is
sufficiently ready; materially required conditions are
sufficiently satisfied; remaining qualifications are compatible
with downstream use; no unresolved issue fundamentally undermines
Event Identity or structure; and the Decision basis is
reconstructable.

The threshold is methodological sufficiency.

It is not a jurisdiction-specific tax threshold.

Conditional Decision Threshold

A Conditional Determination may be appropriate where: Event
identity is stable; material Event structure is sufficient;
remaining limitations are bounded; downstream interpretation
remains possible; the qualification is explicit.

Conditional Determination shall not be used to hide a
fundamentally unresolved Event.

Deferred Decision Threshold

Deferral is appropriate where material issues remain unresolved
but the Event may become determinable later.

Deferral should preserve: reason; required follow-up; affected
governance layer; and current Decision state.

Rejected Decision Threshold

Rejection should require a sufficiently governed basis showing
that the apparent Event does not qualify as a governed
Tax-Relevant Event.

Rejection shall not be based merely on: low tax value; no
expected Tax Liability; jurisdictional exemption; or reporting
convenience.

Low Tax Relevance under Law ≠ TREGA™ Rejection Criterion.

Governance Logic

IF EDCM has not established a sufficient Governed Determination
Condition State, THEN EDDM shall not issue a Positive
Determination.

IF materially required conditions are sufficiently satisfied and
remaining qualifications do not undermine Event Identity or
downstream interpretability, THEN EDDM may issue a Positive
Determination.

IF the Event is sufficiently governed but bounded qualifications
remain, THEN EDDM may issue a Conditional Determination.

IF materially necessary governance remains unresolved but may
become resolvable, THEN EDDM shall issue a Deferred Determination
rather than a Rejected Determination.

IF the governed structure does not support existence of a valid
Tax-Relevant Event, THEN EDDM may issue a Rejected Determination.

IF materially plausible competing Decision outcomes remain
unresolved, THEN the Decision shall remain Ambiguous.

IF materially authoritative governance inputs support
incompatible outcomes, THEN the Decision shall remain Conflicted
or be escalated.

IF the apparent basis for Decision is a technical status,
accounting classification, invoice state, payment state,
blockchain finality, or model output alone, THEN that basis shall
not be sufficient for a governed EDDM Decision.

IF a Merge or Split has materially changed Event structure, THEN
the resulting Event shall undergo appropriate Decision governance
rather than inherit prior Determination automatically.

IF a Positive Determination is issued, THEN it shall mean only
that the Event is sufficiently governed for downstream tax
evaluation.

IF later information materially affects the Decision basis, THEN
the Decision may be flagged for reconsideration under EDREM.

IF the Decision is sufficiently governed, THEN EDDM shall
establish the Governed Event Determination Decision and hand it
to EDUM where uncertainty governance is required.

Enterprise Application

EDDM provides the formal decision point at which a sufficiently
governed Event receives its TREGA™ Determination status.

A typical operational path is:

EDRDM — Governed Determination Readiness
State

→ EDCM — Governed Determination Condition State

→ EDDM — Governed Event Determination Decision

→ EDUM — Event Determination Uncertainty Governance

→ EDREM — Event Determination Reconsideration

→ Determined Tax-Relevant Event

This gives enterprises a clear, reviewable answer to whether a
governed Event can formally enter downstream tax infrastructure
without allowing the Event layer itself to become a tax engine.
Use Case 1 — Cross-Border Marketplace Event with Conditional
Determination

A marketplace Event has completed Readiness governance and its
materially necessary Determination Conditions are satisfied.

However, one bounded digital Location qualification remains.

The uncertainty does not undermine Event Identity, Formation,
Boundary, Participation, Value Association, or Event
Relationships.

EDDM issues a Conditional Determination and preserves the
Location qualification for downstream processing.

The Event becomes eligible for jurisdiction-specific analysis.

EDDM does not determine which jurisdiction has taxing authority.
Use Case 2 — Machine-Generated Record Rejected as a Tax-Relevant
Event

An autonomous industrial system generates a technical record
every time a machine changes operating state.

One record enters TREGA™ because a downstream accounting system
incorrectly treats each machine state change as a separate
economic event.

Upstream governance establishes that the state change is merely
part of an already-governed service Event and does not possess
independent Event Formation or Boundary.

EDDM therefore issues a Rejected Determination for the apparent
standalone Event.

The rejection does not mean the broader service is non-taxable.

It means only that the technical machine-state record does not
qualify as an independent Tax-Relevant Event.

Canonical Boundary

EDDM owns the governance of Decision initiation, Decision
eligibility, Decision basis, Positive Determination, Conditional
Determination, Deferred Determination, Rejected Determination,
Ambiguous Determination, Conflicted Determination, Decision
qualification, Decision rationale, Decision consistency, Decision
provenance, Decision authority reference, Decision timing,
Decision traceability, Decision correction, Decision escalation,
and establishment of the Governed Event Determination Decision.

EDDM does not perform Determination Readiness governance,
Determination Condition governance, full Determination
Uncertainty governance, or Determination Reconsideration.

It does not perform Event Context, Recognition, Formation,
Boundary, Timing, Location, Participation, Value Association, or
Event Relationship governance.

It does not determine legal taxability, Tax Jurisdiction,
taxpayer status, legal tax point, Tax Period, Tax Base, Tax Rate,
Tax Liability, exemption, deduction, filing obligation, payment,
collection, enforcement, or any other jurisdiction-specific legal
tax consequence.

Its governance begins when EDCM establishes a Governed
Determination Condition State suitable for Decision.

Its governance ends when a formal Governed Event Determination
Decision has been established for handoff to EDUM and the
remaining TREDS process.

Canonical Closing Statement

EDDM converts governed readiness and explicit determination
conditions into a formal Event Determination Decision without
allowing that decision to become a legal tax conclusion.

The decision establishes whether TREGA™ recognizes a sufficiently
governed Tax-Relevant Event. It does not decide what any
jurisdiction must tax.

Event Determination Decision ≠ Tax Decision.

Positive Event Determination ≠ Positive Tax Determination.

TREGA™ governs the event. The jurisdiction governs the tax.
