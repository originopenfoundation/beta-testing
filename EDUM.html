Canonical Definition

The Event Determination Uncertainty Module (EDUM) governs how
uncertainty affecting a Governed Event Determination Decision is
identified, classified, bounded, assessed for materiality,
represented, qualified, escalated, monitored, and resolved or
preserved before the Event proceeds to final Determination
completion or reconsideration.

Its principal transition is:

Governed Event Determination Decision + Determination Uncertainty
Indicators

Governed
Determination Uncertainty State

EDUM does not recreate Event Readiness, Determination Conditions,
or the Determination Decision itself.

It governs what uncertainty means for that Decision.

Its foundational distinction is:

Determination Uncertainty ≠ Determination Failure.

Operational Role

A Determination Decision may be methodologically valid even where
some uncertainty remains.

The critical governance question is not whether uncertainty
exists.

It is:

Does the uncertainty materially undermine the Event
Determination, and if so, how must that uncertainty affect the
governed Decision state?

EDUM prevents two opposite failures: treating every uncertainty
as fatal; ignoring material uncertainty merely to produce a
definitive result.

Its role is to preserve a usable but honest Determination state.

Operational Scope

EDUM may govern uncertainty affecting Events arising from:
commerce; marketplaces; banking; payments; ERP environments;
digital platforms; blockchain systems; smart contracts; AI
agents; autonomous systems; industrial systems; energy
infrastructure; distributed systems; machine-to-machine
environments; cross-border activity; multi-party economic
structures; and future machine economies.

The uncertainty-governance logic remains technology-neutral and
jurisdiction-neutral.

Determination Uncertainty

Determination Uncertainty is uncertainty that may affect the
reliability, qualification, interpretation, or continued validity
of the Event Determination Decision.

It may arise from: incomplete upstream information; conflicting
sources; bounded measurement limitations; unstable Event
structure; ambiguous Participation; uncertain Value Association;
uncertain Event Relationships; incomplete Location precision;
timing ambiguity; or subsequent information.

Uncertainty Indicator ≠ Material Determination Uncertainty.

Uncertainty Identification

EDUM shall identify uncertainty that remains materially relevant
after EDDM has issued or proposed a Determination Decision.

Uncertainty shall be distinguishable from: missing data;
conflict; error; unresolved governance; condition failure; or
mere lack of precision.

These states may overlap, but they are not identical.

Uncertainty Source

The source of uncertainty should be identifiable where possible.

Sources may include: Event Context; Event Formation; Event
Boundary; Event Timing; Event Location; Event Participation;
Value Association; Event Relationships; system interoperability;
incomplete records; distributed execution; machine inference;
model estimation; or contradictory operational sources.

EDUM governs the uncertainty impact.

It does not seize ownership of the underlying governed space.

Uncertainty Type

EDUM may classify uncertainty as:

Factual Uncertainty Uncertainty over what happened.

Structural Uncertainty Uncertainty over the constitution or
structure of the Event.

Identity Uncertainty Uncertainty over which governed Event is
under consideration.

Boundary Uncertainty Uncertainty over Event scope.

Temporal Uncertainty Uncertainty over Event timing.

Location Uncertainty Uncertainty over Event location.

Participation Uncertainty Uncertainty over actor participation.

Value Uncertainty Uncertainty over Event–Value Association.

Relationship Uncertainty Uncertainty over relationships among
Events.

Decision Uncertainty Uncertainty affecting the validity or
qualification of the Determination Decision itself.

Uncertainty Scope

Uncertainty shall be bounded wherever possible.

EDUM should identify whether uncertainty affects: one attribute;
one participant; one relationship; one Event segment; one Value
Association; the entire Event; or the Determination Decision as a
whole.

Local Uncertainty ≠ Global Event Uncertainty by default.

Uncertainty Materiality

The central EDUM question is whether uncertainty is materially
significant to Event Determination.

Uncertainty is material where it could materially change: Event
Identity; Event Formation; Event Boundary; Determination status;
Event count; participant structure; Value Association;
relationship structure; or downstream interpretability.

Uncertainty Presence ≠ Material Uncertainty.

Immaterial Uncertainty

Immaterial uncertainty may remain without invalidating the
Decision.

For example, minor imprecision in a non-material technical
attribute may not affect Event Determination.

EDUM shall avoid transforming trivial imperfection into
unnecessary governance failure.

Imprecision ≠ Material Uncertainty by default.

Bounded Uncertainty

Bounded Uncertainty exists where uncertainty remains but its
scope and potential impact are sufficiently understood.

A bounded uncertainty may be compatible with a Positive or
Conditional Determination.

Bounded Uncertainty ≠ Ungoverned Uncertainty.

Residual Uncertainty

Residual Uncertainty is uncertainty that remains after reasonable
governance has been completed.

Its existence may be acceptable where: it is known; bounded;
materially limited; traceable; and does not invalidate the Event
structure.

Residual Uncertainty ≠ Governance Failure.

Material Uncertainty

Material Uncertainty exists where the uncertainty could
materially alter the meaning or validity of the Event
Determination.

This may require: Decision qualification; Decision deferral;
escalation; upstream referral; or reconsideration.

Critical Uncertainty

Critical Uncertainty exists where Event Determination cannot be
responsibly relied upon without further governance.

Examples may include uncertainty over: whether the Event exists;
whether one or several Events exist; which Event Boundary
applies; whether the principal participant is correctly
associated; or whether the Value Association belongs to another
Event.

Critical Uncertainty ≠ Permission to Maintain an Unqualified
Decision.

Uncertainty Severity

Moderate

High

Severity shall reflect governance impact rather than arbitrary
scoring.

Higher Score ≠ Greater Materiality by default.

Uncertainty Confidence

Confidence may be recorded as supporting information.

However:

Confidence ≠ Certainty.

and:

Confidence Score ≠ Determination Validity.

A high-confidence model output may still be methodologically
unsupported.

Uncertainty Qualification

Where uncertainty does not invalidate the Decision but materially
limits its interpretation, EDUM may attach an explicit
qualification.

Qualification may specify: affected governance dimension;
uncertainty scope; known limitation; potential Decision impact;
required downstream caution; reassessment condition.

Qualification ≠ Concealment of Uncertainty.

Uncertainty Interaction

Multiple small uncertainties may interact and create material
combined impact.

For example: moderate Location uncertainty; moderate
Participation uncertainty; moderate Value uncertainty

may together undermine downstream interpretability.

EDUM shall therefore consider cumulative impact where material.

Several Immaterial Uncertainties ≠ Automatically Immaterial in
Combination.

Uncertainty Accumulation

Uncertainty may accumulate across Event governance layers.

EDUM shall identify whether cumulative uncertainty has reached a
threshold where the Determination Decision requires qualification
or reconsideration.

Uncertainty and Conflict

Uncertainty and conflict shall remain distinct.

Uncertainty means the correct state is not fully known.

Conflict means materially relevant sources support incompatible
states.

Uncertainty ≠ Conflict.

A Determination may contain both.

Conflicted Uncertainty

Where uncertainty exists because authoritative inputs conflict,
EDUM may establish a Conflicted Uncertainty state.

It shall preserve: competing positions; source references;
material effect; unresolved status.

Conflict Resolution by Convenience ≠ Governed Uncertainty
Resolution.

Uncertainty Tolerance

A governed system may define tolerance for bounded uncertainty.

Tolerance shall be based on Event-governance materiality.

It shall not be determined solely by: commercial pressure;
reporting deadlines; tax advantage; processing cost; system
limitations.

Operational Pressure ≠ Uncertainty Tolerance Criterion.

Uncertainty Threshold

EDUM may establish thresholds determining when uncertainty
requires: qualification; escalation; Decision deferral; upstream
referral; or reconsideration.

Thresholds shall remain methodological rather than
jurisdiction-specific tax thresholds.

Uncertainty Escalation

Escalation may be required where: uncertainty is critical;
uncertainty cannot be bounded; material conflict remains
unresolved; automated evaluation cannot reliably classify impact;
or downstream reliance would be unsafe.

Escalation does not itself resolve the uncertainty.

Uncertainty and Readiness

EDRDM may already identify uncertainty affecting Readiness.

EDUM does not repeat that function.

At EDUM level, the question is whether uncertainty affects an
existing Determination Decision.

Readiness Uncertainty ≠ Determination Uncertainty.

Uncertainty and Conditions

EDCM may identify uncertainty attached to individual
Determination Conditions.

EDUM evaluates the consolidated impact on the Determination
Decision.

Condition Uncertainty ≠ Determination-Level Uncertainty by
default.

Uncertainty and Decision

EDDM establishes the Decision.

EDUM governs how uncertainty qualifies, constrains, destabilizes,
or preserves that Decision.

Uncertainty Governance ≠ Decision Issuance.

Uncertainty and Reconsideration

EDUM may conclude that uncertainty is significant enough to
trigger reconsideration.

EDREM owns the reconsideration process.

Reconsideration Trigger ≠ Reconsideration Decision.

Uncertainty and Correction

An uncertainty is not automatically an error.

Correction is appropriate where a governed state is known to be
wrong.

Uncertainty exists where the correct state remains insufficiently
established.

Uncertainty ≠ Error.

Uncertainty Persistence

Some uncertainty may persist legitimately over time.

Persistent uncertainty shall remain governed, visible, and
traceable.

Persistent Uncertainty ≠ Permission to Ignore Uncertainty.

Uncertainty Resolution

Uncertainty may be resolved through: additional governance;
better source information; corrected Event structure; participant
clarification; Value reassociation; Event Relationship
correction; reconciliation of conflicting sources; or later
evidence.

Resolution shall preserve prior uncertainty history where
material.

Uncertainty Reassessment

EDUM shall support reassessment when: new information appears;
upstream governance changes; the Event is corrected; Merge or
Split occurs; a conflict is resolved; or downstream reliance
reveals material inconsistency.

Uncertainty Reassessment ≠ Historical Erasure.

Uncertainty State Supersession

A later uncertainty state may supersede an earlier one.

Historical states shall remain traceable.

Resolved Uncertainty ≠ Never-Existing Uncertainty.

Machine-Generated Uncertainty

Machine-generated Events may carry uncertainty resulting from:
probabilistic classification; incomplete sensor data; inferred
participation; distributed execution; asynchronous systems; model
estimation.

Machine processing does not reduce the requirement for explicit
uncertainty governance.

Machine Confidence ≠ Governed Certainty.

AI-Generated Uncertainty

AI systems may propose: Event classification; Event association;
participant mapping; relationship interpretation; Value mapping.

Where such conclusions are probabilistic, EDUM shall preserve
relevant uncertainty rather than convert probability into
certainty automatically.

AI Probability ≠ Governed Determination Certainty.

Blockchain Uncertainty

Blockchain infrastructure may provide strong certainty about
ledger state while leaving uncertainty about: off-chain Event
Formation; participant role; Value meaning; legal or operational
context; Event Relationship.

Therefore:

Ledger Certainty ≠ Event Determination Certainty.

Distributed-System Uncertainty

Distributed systems may contain: asynchronous timestamps;
replicated records; delayed updates; differing system states;
partial visibility.

EDUM shall evaluate their effect on the governed Decision rather
than assuming technical inconsistency equals Event failure.

Cross-System Uncertainty

The same Event may appear differently across systems.

EDUM may classify whether those differences are: harmless
representation differences; bounded uncertainty; material
uncertainty; or conflict.

Representation Difference ≠ Event Uncertainty by default.

Cross-Organization Uncertainty

Different organizations may possess incomplete or inconsistent
views of the same Event.

EDUM may govern the resulting Determination uncertainty without
assigning accountability.

Cross-Border Uncertainty

Cross-border Events may contain uncertain operational location,
participant roles, or distributed execution.

These uncertainties may affect Event governance.

They do not authorize EDUM to determine Tax Jurisdiction.

Cross-Border Uncertainty ≠ Tax Jurisdiction Determination.

Multi-Jurisdiction Neutrality

EDUM shall preserve uncertainty in jurisdiction-neutral Event
terms.

A jurisdiction may later interpret the same uncertainty
differently under law.

Determination Uncertainty ≠ Jurisdictional Tax Uncertainty.

Uncertainty and Taxability

EDUM does not govern uncertainty over legal tax interpretation.

It governs uncertainty over the Tax-Relevant Event Determination.

Event Determination Uncertainty ≠ Taxability Uncertainty.

Uncertainty and Taxpayer

Participant uncertainty may affect Event Determination.

EDUM does not determine taxpayer status.

Participant Uncertainty ≠ Taxpayer Determination.

Uncertainty and Tax Base

Value uncertainty may affect Event Determination.

EDUM does not calculate Tax Base.

Value Uncertainty ≠ Tax Base Determination.

Uncertainty and Legal Tax Point

Timing uncertainty may affect Event representation.

EDUM does not determine legal tax timing.

Temporal Uncertainty ≠ Legal Tax Point Determination.

Established Governance Requirements

EDUM implementations shall ensure that: uncertainty remains
distinct from Determination failure; uncertainty indicators
remain distinct from material uncertainty; uncertainty source is
identifiable where possible; uncertainty type is distinguishable;
uncertainty scope can be bounded where possible; materiality is
explicitly assessed; immaterial uncertainty does not
automatically invalidate the Decision; bounded uncertainty may be
preserved without forcing false certainty; residual uncertainty
remains visible; critical uncertainty cannot support an
unqualified Decision; uncertainty severity reflects governance
impact; confidence scores do not replace uncertainty governance;
qualifications remain visible downstream; interacting
uncertainties are evaluated where materially relevant; cumulative
uncertainty can be represented; uncertainty remains
distinguishable from conflict; conflicted uncertainty preserves
competing governance states; operational pressure does not
determine uncertainty tolerance; escalation remains available;
upstream issues are referred to the authoritative governance
layer; uncertainty does not recreate Readiness, Conditions, or
Decision governance; reconsideration triggers remain distinct
from reconsideration itself; uncertainty remains distinct from
known error; persistent uncertainty remains governed; resolution
preserves historical traceability; AI or machine probability does
not become certainty automatically; blockchain finality does not
eliminate off-chain Event uncertainty; cross-border uncertainty
does not determine Tax Jurisdiction; and the resulting
uncertainty state is suitable for EDREM or final TREDS
completion.

Governance Logic

IF an uncertainty indicator exists, THEN EDUM shall evaluate its
scope and materiality before modifying the Determination state.

IF uncertainty is immaterial to Event Identity, structure, or
downstream interpretability, THEN the Determination Decision may
remain unchanged.

IF uncertainty is bounded and materially limited, THEN the
Decision may remain valid with an explicit uncertainty
qualification.

IF several individually limited uncertainties combine into a
material Determination risk, THEN EDUM shall govern their
cumulative effect rather than evaluate them only in isolation.

IF uncertainty materially undermines Event Identity, Formation,
Boundary, Participation, Value Association, or Event
Relationships, THEN the Decision shall be qualified, escalated,
deferred, or referred for reconsideration as appropriate.

IF uncertainty results from conflicting materially relevant
sources, THEN EDUM shall preserve the conflict rather than select
the most convenient representation.

IF the underlying uncertainty belongs to an upstream TREGA™
governed space, THEN EDUM shall preserve Determination impact and
refer the substantive issue to the authoritative layer.

IF uncertainty cannot be sufficiently bounded and downstream
reliance would be materially unsafe, THEN Reconsideration
Required shall be established.

IF new information resolves the uncertainty, THEN the new
uncertainty state shall supersede the prior state without erasing
historical uncertainty.

IF the Determination Decision remains sufficiently reliable after
uncertainty governance, THEN EDUM shall establish the Governed
Determination Uncertainty State for downstream TREDS processing.

Enterprise Application

EDUM provides the uncertainty-control layer between formal Event
Determination and later reconsideration or final downstream use.

A canonical enterprise path is:

EDCM — Governed Determination Condition
State

→ EDDM — Governed Event Determination Decision

→ EDUM — Governed Determination Uncertainty State

→ EDREM — Event Determination Reconsideration Governance

→ Determined Tax-Relevant Event

This enables systems to preserve usable Determination results
while exposing the exact uncertainty that remains rather than
forcing every Event into a misleading binary state. Use Case 1 —
Distributed Digital Service with Bounded Location Uncertainty

A distributed digital service has stable Formation, Boundary,
Participation, Value Association, and Event Relationship
governance.

EDDM issues a Positive Determination.

However, infrastructure execution occurred across several
geographically distributed systems and the exact operational
Location of one component cannot be established with full
precision.

EDUM determines that the Location uncertainty is bounded and does
not alter Event Identity or prevent downstream
jurisdiction-specific analysis.

The Determination remains valid with an explicit Bounded Location
Uncertainty qualification.

EDUM does not determine which jurisdiction may tax the Event. Use
Case 2 — Autonomous Settlement with Material Participation
Uncertainty

An autonomous settlement Event receives a provisional
Determination Decision.

Two materially authoritative systems disagree over which
autonomous participant executed the decisive settlement function.

The uncertainty could materially change the governed
Participation structure and downstream interpretation.

EDUM classifies the uncertainty as Material and Conflicted,
preserves both participant representations, refers the
substantive Participation issue to TREPS, and establishes
Reconsideration Required.

The module does not select the participant that produces the
preferred tax result.

Canonical Boundary

EDUM owns the governance of Determination-level uncertainty
identification, source, classification, scope, materiality,
severity, bounded uncertainty, residual uncertainty, cumulative
uncertainty, conflict interaction, qualification, tolerance,
escalation, persistence, reassessment, resolution, traceability,
and Determination-level reconsideration triggers.

EDUM does not perform Event Readiness governance, Determination
Condition governance, Determination Decision issuance, or
Determination Reconsideration itself.

It does not recreate Event Context, Recognition, Formation,
Boundary, Timing, Location, Participation, Value Association, or
Event Relationships.

It does not determine legal taxability, Tax Jurisdiction,
taxpayer status, legal tax point, Tax Period, Tax Base, Tax Rate,
Tax Liability, exemption, deduction, filing obligation, or any
other jurisdiction-specific tax consequence.

Its governance begins when a Governed Event Determination
Decision contains or encounters materially relevant uncertainty.

Its governance ends when uncertainty has been sufficiently
governed for continued reliance, qualification, escalation, or
handoff to EDREM.

Canonical Closing Statement

EDUM ensures that uncertainty is governed according to its
material impact rather than hidden, exaggerated, or converted
into false certainty.

A Determination may remain valid while uncertainty exists, but
only when that uncertainty is understood, bounded, visible, and
compatible with continued reliance.

Determination Uncertainty ≠ Determination Failure.

Uncertainty ≠ Error.
