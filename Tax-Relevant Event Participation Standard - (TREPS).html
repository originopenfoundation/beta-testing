Canonical Language: UCL — Universal Canonical Language Canonical Definition The Tax-Relevant Event Participation Standard (TREPS) governs how human, organizational, digital, machine, autonomous, infrastructural, and other materially relevant actors are represented as participants in a spatially and temporally governed Tax-Relevant Event. TREPS establishes the governance conditions under which an actor may legitimately be associated with the event, distinguished from other actors, represented according to its operational participation, and maintained as part of the governed event representation. TREPS governs participation in the event. It does not determine whether a participant is legally a taxpayer, taxable person, liable party, beneficial owner, permanent establishment, tax representative, withholding agent, employer, employee, supplier, customer, or any other jurisdiction-specific legal or tax status.
The foundational distinction is: Participant ≠ Taxpayer TREPS establishes the governed participation layer required before downstream tax systems determine what legal significance, if any, attaches to that participation. Purpose The purpose of TREPS is to ensure that materially relevant participation in a Tax-Relevant Event can be represented consistently across human, organizational, digital, autonomous, and machine-mediated economies.
Modern events may involve: individuals; companies; public organizations; platforms; intermediaries; service providers; AI agents; autonomous systems; robots; machines; software services; digital infrastructure; and combinations of these actors. The existence of an actor near, inside, connected to, recorded by, or technically involved with an event does not by itself establish governed participation.
TREPS therefore establishes the methodological layer between: Actor Association → Governed Event Participation → Jurisdiction-Specific Legal Interpretation Its purpose is to preserve operational participation before legal systems assign jurisdiction-specific meaning to it. Core Question Who or what materially participated in the governed event, and how can that participation be represented without converting operational participation into taxpayer status, legal identity, tax residence, liability, or another jurisdiction-specific legal conclusion? Governance Objective
TREPS shall ensure that Event Participation is: attributable to the governed event; operationally justified; distinguishable by participant; capable of representing different participation roles; compatible with human and non-human actors; capable of representing multiple participants; compatible with distributed and autonomous activity; traceable; continuity-aware; uncertainty-aware; jurisdiction-neutral; and suitable for subsequent Value Association governance. TREPS shall prevent participation data from being prematurely converted into legal tax conclusions. Governed Space

TREPS governs Tax-Relevant Event Participation as an
architecture-level space.

This includes governance of: participant presence within the
event; participant identification requirements; participation
differentiation; participation roles; actor-event association;
multiple-participant structures; human participation;
organizational participation; digital participation; machine
participation; autonomous participation; participation
continuity; participation change; participation uncertainty;
participation conflict; and participation traceability.

The detailed operational mechanisms for these areas belong to
TREPS Core Modules.

Participation Principle

An actor becomes a governed Event Participant only where its
relationship to the bounded event is sufficiently supported to
justify participation representation.

Mere association is insufficient.

Therefore:

Actor Association ≠ Event Participation by Default.

Examples of insufficient standalone indicators may include:
appearing in a database; receiving a notification; being
geographically present; owning infrastructure; owning a
participating machine; operating a platform; appearing on a
payment record; holding an account; being named in a contract; or
being technically connected to an event.

Such information may support participation governance.

It does not automatically establish participation.

Multi-Participant Events

A single Tax-Relevant Event may contain multiple participants.

TREPS shall support:

One Event

↓

Multiple Governed Participants

without assuming that every participant has the same operational
role, legal status, economic interest, responsibility, or
downstream tax significance.

Likewise:

Multiple Participants ≠ Multiple Events by Default.

Event multiplicity remains governed through Event Formation and
Event Boundary.

Participation Roles

Participants may perform materially different operational
functions within the same event.

TREPS shall require participation to be capable of role
differentiation where such differentiation is necessary to
represent the event faithfully.

However, operational participation roles shall remain separate
from jurisdiction-specific legal classifications.

Operational Participation Role ≠ Legal Tax Role.

A role governed by TREPS does not automatically establish that an
actor is legally: seller; buyer; taxpayer; employer; employee;
principal; agent under law; permanent establishment; taxable
person; beneficial owner; withholding agent; importer; exporter;
or another legally defined party.

Such legal classifications remain downstream.

Participation and Identity

Participation requires sufficient actor differentiation to
preserve the integrity of the event.

TREPS does not, however, own the complete governance of identity,
origin, ownership, authority, permission, or provenance.

Those governance spaces remain with OBIDENITY® where applicable.

The canonical distinction is:

Participation ≠ Identity Governance.

TREPS governs:

which actor participates in the event and in what operational
participation relationship.

OBIDENITY® governs:

the governed identity, origin, ownership, authority, permission,
and provenance structures associated with that actor.

TREPS may consume governed identity information without
redefining it.

Participation and Location

TRELS establishes where the event occurred.

TREPS shall not infer participation merely because an actor is
located at or near an Event Location.

Presence at Event Location ≠ Event Participation by Default.

Likewise, remote participation may be legitimate even where the
participant is physically located elsewhere.

Participant Location ≠ Event Location by Default.

Spatial proximity is evidence of possible association, not
automatic proof of participation.

Participation and Event Timing

Participation may begin, change, pause, resume, or end during the
governed event.

TREPS shall support participation structures that remain
compatible with Event Timing governed under TRETS.

However:

Participation Timing ≠ Event Timing Governance.

TRETS remains authoritative for Event Timing.

TREPS governs only the relationship between participation and
that governed temporal structure.

Participation and Event Boundary

Only participation materially associated with the bounded event
may become part of the TREPS representation.

TREPS shall not expand Event Boundary merely because additional
actors can be technically or commercially associated with the
event.

Actor Association ≠ Event Boundary Expansion.

TREBS remains authoritative for Event Boundary.

Machine and Autonomous Participation

TREPS shall support events in which machines or autonomous
systems materially participate without contemporaneous human
action.

Examples may include: autonomous purchasing; machine-to-machine
contracting workflows; automated resource acquisition; autonomous
logistics; robotic production; AI-mediated service execution;
automated financial operations; autonomous infrastructure; and
future machine economies.

TREPS shall preserve the operational reality of such
participation.

It shall not automatically substitute the machine owner,
developer, operator, manufacturer, supervising person, or
organization as the operational participant merely because a
human or organization exists somewhere in the control or
ownership structure.

Machine Participant ≠ Machine Owner.

Autonomous Participant ≠ Human Operator by Default.

Questions of authority, ownership, accountability,
responsibility, and legal attribution remain governed elsewhere.

Participation Continuity

Participation may evolve while the same governed event continues.

Participants may: enter; leave; change operational roles; become
inactive; be replaced; act sequentially; act simultaneously; or
participate through delegated or automated systems.

TREPS shall preserve materially relevant participation
continuity.

Participation Change ≠ New Event by Default.

Whether a new event exists remains governed through Event
Formation and Event Boundary.

Participation Uncertainty

TREPS shall permit participation to remain qualified where
available evidence does not support complete certainty.

Possible architecture-level participation conditions may include:
established; qualified; partial; disputed; conflicted;
unresolved; or unknown.

Participation Uncertainty ≠ Permission to Invent a Participant.

Where participation cannot legitimately be established,
uncertainty shall remain visible.

Participation Conflict

Different evidence sources may identify incompatible participants
or participation roles.

TREPS shall preserve material conflict rather than silently
selecting whichever representation best supports a downstream tax
outcome.

Participation Conflict ≠ Permission for Convenient Participant
Selection.

Conflict resolution mechanisms are operationalized through the
relevant TREPS modules and supporting evidence-governance
architectures.

Participation Traceability

Governed Event Participation shall remain reconstructable.

A downstream system should be able to determine: which
participants were represented; why their participation was
accepted; what operational role was governed; what evidence
supported the association; what uncertainty existed; what
material changes occurred; and what governance state applied.

TREPS establishes this requirement at Parent Standard level.

Detailed participant-record mechanics belong to the Core Modules.

Participation and Value

Participation alone does not determine which participant owns,
receives, creates, transfers, controls, consumes, or benefits
from value.

Those questions require separate governance.

The next Parent Standard, TRVAS, governs association of governed
value results with the event.

Therefore:

Event Participation ≠ Value Association.

Participant ≠ Value Owner by Default.

Participation Role ≠ Value Entitlement.

VFM® remains authoritative for the Value Lifecycle itself.

Participation and Accountability

Operational participation does not automatically establish
responsibility, accountability, fault, or liability.

Therefore:

Participant ≠ Responsible Party by Default.

Participation ≠ Accountability.

Participation ≠ Liability.

AGA™ governs accountability structures where applicable.

HALIA™ governs the lifecycle of harm and liability where
applicable.

TREPS preserves participation as an operational fact without
converting it into a conclusion about blame or legal
responsibility.

Participation and Taxpayer Status

TREPS shall preserve a strict separation between Event
Participation and jurisdiction-specific taxpayer determination.

A participant may later be evaluated by a jurisdiction as:
taxpayer; taxable person; supplier; customer; employer; employee;
withholding party; intermediary; marketplace operator; permanent
establishment; reporting party; or another legally defined actor.

TREPS does not make that determination.

Participation ≠ Permanent Establishment.

Participation ≠ Tax Nexus.

Participation ≠ Legal Liability.

Participation ≠ Filing Obligation.

Governance Requirements

TREPS implementations shall ensure that: participation belongs to
a governed event; participants are sufficiently distinguishable;
actor-event association is operationally justified; participant
roles can be differentiated where necessary; multiple
participants can be represented; human and non-human participants
can be represented; machine and autonomous participation can be
governed; participation can change without automatic Event
reformation; participation uncertainty can remain visible;
participation conflicts can remain visible; participation remains
traceable; participant location is not substituted for Event
Location; participation does not redefine Event Boundary;
identity governance remains distinct from participation
governance; participation does not automatically establish
accountability or liability; participation does not automatically
establish Value Association; participation does not determine
taxpayer status; jurisdiction-specific legal classifications
remain downstream; and the resulting participation structure is
suitable for subsequent TREGA™ governance.

Governance Logic

TREPS applies the following architecture-level logic:

IF a Governed Temporally and Spatially Situated Event exists,
THEN materially relevant actors may be evaluated for Event
Participation.

IF an actor's relationship to the event is sufficiently governed,
THEN the actor may be represented as a Governed Event
Participant.

IF multiple actors materially participate, THEN TREPS shall
preserve the multi-participant structure rather than force a
single-participant representation.

IF a machine, AI agent, or autonomous system materially
participates, THEN non-human participation may be represented
without substituting a human participant by default.

IF participation remains uncertain or conflicted, THEN that state
shall remain visible.

IF participation is sufficiently governed, THEN TREPS may produce
a Governed Participatory Event.

TREPS SHALL NOT convert Event Participation into taxpayer status
or another jurisdiction-specific legal conclusion.

Enterprise Application

TREPS provides a jurisdiction-neutral participation layer between
governed operational events and downstream tax systems.

A generalized enterprise flow is:

ERP / Commerce / Banking / Platform / AI Agent / Autonomous
System / Machine Network

→ TREGA™ Event Layer

→ Governed Temporally and Spatially Situated Event

→ TREPS

→ Governed Participatory Event

→ TRVAS — Value Association

→ Remaining TREGA™ Governance

→ Jurisdiction-Ready Tax-Relevant Event

→ Country A / Country B / Country C Tax Engines

This architecture allows a single governed representation of who
or what actually participated in an event to support multiple
legal tax interpretations without embedding one jurisdiction's
legal assumptions into the event itself.

Internal Module Architecture

TREPS is operationalized through five Core Modules: 1. EPIM —
Event Participant Identification Module

Governs identification and differentiation of actors that may
legitimately become Event Participants. 2. EPRM — Event
Participation Role Module

Governs the operational participation roles through which
identified participants act within the event. 3. EPAM — Event
Actor Association Module

Governs the association between participants, their governed
roles, and the bounded Tax-Relevant Event. 4. EPMAM — Event
Machine & Autonomous Actor Module

Governs participation structures involving machines, AI agents,
autonomous systems, robots, and other non-human operational
actors. 5. EPCM — Event Participation Continuity Module

Governs continuity, change, entry, exit, replacement, and
material evolution of participation during the event.

Canonical Boundary

TREPS owns the governance of participation within a Tax-Relevant
Event, including the architecture-level requirements for
participant identification, participation-role differentiation,
actor-event association, multi-participant representation, human
and non-human participation, machine and autonomous
participation, participation continuity, uncertainty, conflict,
and traceability.

TREPS does not own Event Context, Event Recognition, Event
Formation, Event Root Association, Event Boundary, Event Timing,
Event Location, Value Lifecycle governance, final Value
Association, Event Relationships, Event Determination, Event
Record Governance, or Event Lifecycle Governance.

TREPS does not determine complete identity, origin, ownership,
authority, permission, provenance, accountability,
responsibility, fault, liability, taxpayer status, taxable-person
status, tax residence, permanent establishment, tax nexus, place
of supply, source jurisdiction, legal situs, Tax Jurisdiction,
Legal Tax Point, Tax Period, taxability, Tax Liability, tax rate,
tax base, deduction, exemption, filing obligation, reporting
obligation, collection, payment, or enforcement.

Those governance and legal spaces remain outside TREPS.

Canonical Closing Statement

TREPS establishes who or what materially participated in the
governed event without allowing operational participation to
become legal identity, taxpayer status, responsibility,
liability, or tax jurisdiction by default.

Participation establishes involvement in the event. It does not
establish the legal tax consequence of that involvement.

Participant ≠ Taxpayer.

TREGA™ governs the event. The jurisdiction governs the tax.
