Governed Space: Tax-Relevant Value Association What Is TRVAS?

The Tax-Relevant Value Association Standard (TRVAS) is the TREGA™
Parent Standard that governs how value is connected to a governed
Tax-Relevant Event.

Its purpose is not to calculate tax.

Its purpose is to establish something that must exist before
reliable tax analysis can occur: Which governed value results
actually belong to this event?

A real-world or digital event may involve money, goods, services,
digital assets, rights, resources, energy, computational
capacity, economic benefits, losses, or multiple forms of value
simultaneously.

TRVAS provides a structured method for associating those value
results with the correct governed event.

It creates the value-association layer between operational
reality and later jurisdiction-specific taxation. Why Does TRVAS
Exist?

Modern economic systems generate enormous quantities of
value-related information.

Banks record payments.

ERP systems record invoices and accounting entries.

Marketplaces record prices, commissions, fees, refunds, and
settlements.

Blockchain networks record transfers of digital assets.

Industrial systems record resource consumption and production.

AI agents may initiate transactions or allocate resources.

Autonomous machines may consume energy, request services,
exchange information, or trigger economic activity.

But the existence of a number, payment, asset transfer, or
accounting entry does not automatically explain what value
belongs to the underlying event.

For example:

Payment ≠ Complete Event Value

Invoice Amount ≠ Taxable Value

Asset Transfer ≠ Ownership Determination

Recorded Amount ≠ Governed Value by
Default

Before a taxation system can legitimately interpret value, the
relationship between the value and the event must first be
understood.

TRVAS governs that relationship.

The Problem TRVAS Solves

Traditional systems often begin taxation analysis from
transaction records.

This works reasonably well when the economic structure is simple:

Seller

↓

Product

↓

Buyer

↓

Payment

Modern economic activity can be substantially more complex.

A single event may involve: several participants; multiple
payments; multiple currencies; digital assets; service
components; platform fees; automated settlement; rebates or
refunds; non-monetary value; machine-generated activity;
autonomous agents; distributed infrastructure; and several
jurisdictions.

In these environments, asking only:

“What amount was recorded?”

is no longer sufficient.

The more fundamental governance question is: “Which value results
materially belong to which event?”

TRVAS provides the methodology for answering that question before
jurisdiction-specific tax rules are applied.

What TRVAS Governs

TRVAS governs the relationship between a Tax-Relevant Event and
the value associated with that event.

It establishes a framework for determining: which governed value
information is relevant to the event; which value references
belong to the event; which governed Value States are associated
with the event; which governed Value Changes are associated with
the event; whether multiple values belong to the same event;
whether a value relationship is direct or indirect; whether the
association is sufficiently supported; whether uncertainty
exists; whether conflicting value associations exist; and whether
the relationship remains sufficiently traceable.

The result is a: Governed Value-Associated
Event

This becomes the input for subsequent TREGA™ governance.

What TRVAS Does Not Govern

TRVAS deliberately stops before several other governance and
legal questions.

It does not determine: who legally owns the value; where value
originated; the complete Value Lifecycle; who is entitled to the
value; whether the value is taxable; which jurisdiction may tax
it; the applicable Tax Base; taxable income; taxable gain;
customs value; transfer-pricing value; deductions; exemptions;
tax rates; or Tax Liability.

These questions belong to other governance architectures or
jurisdiction-specific taxation systems.

The central distinction is: Governed Event
Value ≠ Taxable Value

TRVAS prepares the event-value relationship.

The jurisdiction later determines its legal tax meaning.

Why Value Association Is Different from
Value Governance

TRVAS does not replace VFM® — Value Flow Mechanism.

VFM® governs what happens to value throughout its lifecycle.

Its governance may establish how value originates, emerges,
changes, develops, degrades, becomes nullified, is restored, or
ultimately becomes extinct.

TRVAS asks a different question:

Which of those governed value results belong to this particular
event?

The architectural separation is therefore: VFM® governs what
happened to value.TRVAS governs which governed value result
belongs to the event.

This prevents TREGA™ from becoming a second Value Lifecycle
architecture.

Why a Payment Is Not Enough

A payment is an operational fact.

It may be highly relevant.

But it does not necessarily represent the complete value
structure of the event.

A payment may include: several goods; several services; platform
charges; commissions; taxes; discounts; refunds; financing
components; deposits; bundled consideration; or amounts
associated with several events.

Likewise, an event may have value even when no conventional
payment occurs.

Therefore: Payment ≠ Complete Event Value

TRVAS allows systems to represent the actual event-value
structure rather than assuming that one payment field explains
the entire economic event.

Monetary and Non-Monetary Value

TRVAS is designed for economic environments where value cannot
always be reduced immediately to money.

Value associated with an event may include: currency; digital
assets; goods; services; rights; access; data-related value;
computational capacity; energy; productive resources; economic
benefits; or other governed forms of value.

This becomes increasingly important as digital and autonomous
economies develop.

TRVAS therefore does not begin with the assumption:

Value = Currency

Instead, it allows governed value information to exist first and
jurisdiction-specific valuation to occur later.

Why TRVAS Matters for Banks

Banks operate at one of the most important intersections between
events and value.

They observe: payments; settlements; transfers; account
movements; merchant transactions; financing; digital payments;
cross-border flows; and increasingly tokenized or programmable
financial assets.

But a bank transaction record does not necessarily contain the
complete economic event.

TRVAS provides a methodology for connecting financial value
information to a governed event structure.

Instead of seeing only:

Account A

↓

CHF 10,000

↓

Account B

a governed infrastructure can potentially understand:

Event

↓

Participants

↓

Value Association

↓

Related Events

↓

Tax-Relevant Event Record

This creates richer structured information for downstream
compliance, reporting, audit, risk, and tax systems without
requiring the bank itself to determine the tax consequence.

Why TRVAS Matters for Blockchain and
Digital Assets

Blockchain networks provide strong transaction records, but
transaction visibility alone does not explain economic meaning.

A token movement may represent: payment; collateral; transfer
between wallets controlled by the same actor; exchange; staking;
settlement; asset migration; protocol interaction; liquidity
movement; or another operational event.

Therefore: On-Chain Transfer ≠ Taxable
Event by Default

TRVAS can associate governed digital-asset value results with the
correct event while preserving the distinction between
operational reality and legal taxation.

This is especially important where one blockchain transaction
contains several economically relevant actions or where one
economic event spans several on-chain and off-chain operations.

Why TRVAS Matters for Businesses and ERP
Systems

Businesses already possess large amounts of value information.

It exists across: ERP; accounting; procurement; invoicing;
inventory; payments; logistics; CRM; marketplaces; and
operational systems.

The problem is often not absence of data.

The problem is that the data describes different fragments of the
same economic reality.

TRVAS provides a common governance layer for connecting governed
value information to the correct event.

For businesses, this can support: cleaner event records; stronger
auditability; better data interoperability; reduced ambiguity;
clearer cross-border analysis; more structured tax-engine inputs;
and better readiness for automated compliance.

The business value comes from improving the quality of the
information entering downstream decision systems.

Why TRVAS Matters for Digital Platforms

A platform may participate in millions of economically relevant
events involving customers, merchants, payment providers,
logistics providers, advertisers, service providers, and digital
infrastructure.

One platform event may contain:

Product Value + Service Value + Platform Fee + Payment Fee +
Discount + Refund + Digital Benefit

Treating this as one undifferentiated transaction amount can hide
the underlying event-value structure.

TRVAS provides a method for preserving materially distinct Value
Associations while keeping them connected to the same governed
event where appropriate.

This becomes particularly valuable for platforms operating across
multiple jurisdictions.

Why TRVAS Matters for AI Agents and
Autonomous Systems

Economic activity will increasingly be initiated and executed by
software.

An AI agent may select a supplier.

Another agent may negotiate a service.

A machine may automatically request maintenance.

An autonomous system may allocate computational resources.

Machines may exchange services or resources without
contemporaneous human action.

These activities may create or affect value even where there is
no traditional invoice at the moment the event occurs.

TRVAS allows those value relationships to be governed without
forcing them prematurely into traditional transaction
assumptions.

The principle is: No Human Transaction ≠
No Value Association

This makes TRVAS suitable not only for today's economy but also
for emerging machine and autonomous economic environments.

Multi-Jurisdiction Value Governance

One of the strongest reasons for separating Value Association
from tax determination is cross-border activity.

The same governed event and the same governed value structure may
be evaluated differently by different jurisdictions.

TRVAS supports:

One Governed Event

→ One Governed Value Association Structure

→ Multiple Jurisdictional Evaluations

Jurisdiction A may interpret the value one way.

Jurisdiction B may apply another legal classification.

Jurisdiction C may determine that only part of the associated
value is relevant.

TREGA™ does not need to rewrite operational reality for each
jurisdiction.

It provides a stable governed event layer that different
jurisdictional systems can consume.

↓

Value

TREPS establishes who or what materially participated.

TRERLS then establishes how that value-associated event relates
to other events.

How TRVAS Works at a High Level

TRVAS is operationalized through five Core Modules:

VAIM — Value Association Identification
Module

Identifies value information that may materially belong to the
event.

VARM — Value Reference Module

Establishes stable references between the event and governed
value results.

VACM — Value Change Association Module

Associates governed Value Changes with the event.

VASM — Value State Association Module

Associates relevant governed Value States with the event.

VATM — Value Association Traceability
Module

Preserves the reconstructability, qualification, uncertainty, and
traceability of the association structure.

Together they transform:

Governed Participatory Event + Governed
Value Results

into: Governed Value-Associated EventWhat Organizations Gain from
TRVAS

TRVAS provides a common methodological layer for organizations

The principal benefit is not another tax calculation.

TRVAS is therefore particularly relevant to:

banks, payment infrastructure, ERP providers, accounting
platforms, marketplaces, blockchain systems, digital-asset

AI-agent platforms, autonomous-system operators, auditors,
compliance infrastructures, and public tax administrations. Use
Case 1 — Global Digital Commerce Infrastructure

A customer purchases a bundled digital and physical service
through a global marketplace.

The event involves a merchant, marketplace, payment provider,
logistics provider, cloud infrastructure, and customer.

Operational systems contain: product value; digital-service
value; platform commission; payment-processing fee; delivery
charge; promotional discount; currency conversion; and later a
partial refund.

A conventional system may see several independent records or
reduce everything to a single invoice total.

TRVAS allows the governed event to preserve the materially
relevant value relationships without deciding which amount is
legally taxable.

The resulting Governed Value-Associated Event can subsequently be
evaluated by different jurisdictional tax systems according to
their own laws.

The same operational event therefore remains stable while its
legal tax interpretation may differ across jurisdictions. Use
Case 2 — Autonomous Digital-Asset and Machine Economy

An autonomous industrial system detects excess production
capacity.

An AI agent allocates that capacity to another organization
through a digital marketplace.

A second autonomous agent negotiates the resource allocation.

The service consumes energy and computational resources, produces
operational value, and settlement occurs through a digital asset.

Several governed value results may therefore exist: computational
capacity; energy consumption; operational service value;
digital-asset transfer; and associated value changes.

TRVAS can associate these governed value results with the event
without assuming that the blockchain transfer alone represents
the complete economic event.

It also does not determine whether the AI agent, machine owner,
operating company, platform, or another actor is legally taxable.

The resulting event-value structure provides downstream tax
systems with a governed representation of what occurred before
jurisdiction-specific law determines what is taxable and by whom.

Canonical Boundary

TRVAS governs the relationship between governed value results and
a Tax-Relevant Event.

It does not govern the complete Value Lifecycle, determine
ownership or entitlement, replace VFM®, determine Event
Participation, establish accountability or liability, or
calculate jurisdiction-specific taxable value, Tax Base, Tax
Liability, rates, exemptions, deductions, reporting obligations,
collection, payment, or enforcement.

Its purpose is to make the value dimension of an event
sufficiently structured, distinguishable, traceable, and
jurisdiction-neutral for subsequent governance and taxation
analysis.

Canonical Closing Statement

TRVAS creates the governed bridge between value reality and event
reality before tax law interprets either.

A payment is not necessarily the complete value of an event.

A blockchain transfer is not necessarily the economic event.

A participant is not necessarily the owner of the associated
value.

And a governed value associated with an event is not
automatically taxable value. VFM® governs what happened to value.
TREGA™ governs which governed value result belongs to the
event.Governed Event Value ≠ Taxable Value.

TREGA™ governs the event. The jurisdiction governs the tax.
