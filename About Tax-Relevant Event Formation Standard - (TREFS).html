About Tax-Relevant Event Formation Standard -
(TREFS)

Event Before Its Boundary Is Determined

A recognized occurrence does not automatically become a
sufficiently structured Tax-Relevant Event.

Recognition establishes that an occurrence legitimately warrants
further governance.

But modern economic activity may involve multiple operational
actions, system records, technical stages, machine operations,
distributed representations, dependencies, and autonomous
processes.

Those elements must still be structurally organized before TREGA™
can determine the final Event Boundary, Timing, Location,
Participation, Value Association, Event Relationships, or
downstream tax relevance.

Tax-Relevant Event Formation Standard (TREFS) establishes the
governed formation layer through which a sufficiently recognized
occurrence becomes a coherent Governed Formed Event.

Its purpose is to answer a fundamental structural question:

What governed event structure can legitimately be formed from
this recognized occurrence? What Is Tax-Relevant Event Formation
Standard?

Tax-Relevant Event Formation Standard (TREFS) is the third Parent
Standard of TREGA™ — Tax-Relevant Event Governance Architecture™.

It operates after TRERS — Tax-Relevant Event Recognition Standard
has established that an occurrence legitimately warrants further
event governance.

It operates before TREBS — Tax-Relevant Event Boundary Standard
determines the final extent of the formed event.

TREFS establishes the governance conditions required to identify
materially relevant formation elements, organize them into a
coherent event structure, establish material formation
dependencies, associate the structure with an operational
formation root, and determine whether the resulting formation
possesses sufficient integrity to proceed.

Its role is deliberately bounded:

TREFS forms the event structure. It does not determine the final
Event Boundary or whether tax is legally due.

Canonical Definition

Tax-Relevant Event Formation Standard (TREFS) defines the
governance conditions required to transform a sufficiently
recognized real-world, economic, operational, transactional,
digital, autonomous, machine-generated, or value-affecting
occurrence into a coherent Governed Formed Event through governed
treatment of formation components, structural assembly, material
dependencies, operational root association, formation
uncertainty, integrity, reconsideration, and traceability while
preserving the boundaries between Event Recognition, Event
Formation, Event Boundary, Event Determination, and
jurisdiction-specific taxation.

Without Event Formation governance, downstream systems may

membership; treat the earliest technical record as the event
root; use tax-law categories to reconstruct the operational event
backward.

TREFS exists to prevent those structural failures.

It creates a neutral formation layer between recognized
occurrence and final Event Boundary.

What TREFS Governs

TREFS governs Tax-Relevant Event Formation, including: formation
readiness; event-forming component identification; formation
component qualification; formation component differentiation;
structural assembly; formation coherence; formation dependencies;
operational formation root association; distributed event
formation; machine-generated event formation; autonomous event
formation; composite formation; formation ambiguity; formation
conflict; formation uncertainty; formation integrity; formation
insufficiency; formation reconsideration; formation traceability.

The purpose is not to collect every technical action associated
with the occurrence.

The purpose is to establish the minimum coherent governed
structure necessary for legitimate progression through the TREGA™
lifecycle.

What TREFS Does Not Determine

TREFS deliberately does not determine: whether an occurrence
should originally have been recognized; the final Event Boundary;
final inclusion or exclusion of every surrounding element; Event
Timing; the legal tax point; Event Location; Tax Jurisdiction;
Event Participation; taxpayer status; final Event-to-Value
Association; relationships among governed events; final Event
Determination; legal taxability; tax nexus; taxable value; tax
base; tax rate; exemption or deduction eligibility; tax
liability; reporting obligations; payment obligations;
enforcement.

A formed event is therefore not yet a legally taxable event.

Governed Formed Event ≠ Legally Taxable Event.

Why the Distinction Matters

The distinction between Event Formation and Event Boundary is
especially important.

Formation asks:

What coherent event structure can be constructed from the
recognized occurrence?

Boundary asks:

What ultimately belongs inside or outside that event?

The two questions are related but not identical.

A formation may depend on surrounding operational activity that
ultimately remains outside the final Event Boundary.

Likewise, several technical actions may need to be considered
during formation even though they do not become independent
events.

Separating these stages prevents early structural assumptions
from becoming permanent event boundaries.

Each stage answers a different governance question. Recognized
Occurrence ≠ Formed Event

A recognized occurrence has satisfied the conditions required to
continue through TREGA™.

But recognition does not establish the event's structural
composition.

For example, TRERS may determine that a digitally executed
service transaction warrants further governance.

That does not yet establish whether: authorization; activation;
consumption; delivery; settlement; invoicing

represent one event structure, several event candidates,
dependencies, or technical representations of the same underlying
activity.

TREFS governs that structural transition.

Formation Components Without Technical
Over-Fragmentation

Operational systems often generate many records around one
occurrence.

Not every record should become a formation component.

And not every formation component represents an independent
event.

For example:

an API call may be technically necessary but structurally
irrelevant;

a payment authorization may support formation without defining
the entire event;

a delivery action may be materially important while an internal
synchronization record is not.

TREFS therefore preserves:

Recorded Element ≠ Formation Component by Default.

and:

Formation Component ≠ Independent Event by Default.

This becomes increasingly important as machine-generated activity
scales.

Assembly Without Automatic Aggregation

Related elements must not be assembled merely because they share:
an identifier; participant; database; workflow; timestamp range;
account; platform; transaction reference.

These characteristics may provide useful structural signals.

They do not independently establish one coherent event.

TREFS requires governed assembly.

This prevents technical system design from silently becoming
event ontology.

Dependencies Without Automatic Event
Membership

A formed event may depend on activity that does not belong inside
the event itself.

For example:

a digital transaction may depend on prior account creation;

an autonomous purchase may depend on delegated authority;

a service may depend on previous infrastructure activation.

These dependencies may be necessary to understand how the event
formed.

But:

Formation Dependency ≠ Event Membership.

The final inclusion or exclusion decision belongs to TREBS.

Event Formation Root

TREFS also ensures that a formed event remains anchored to the
operational occurrence from which it emerged.

This is the purpose of the Event Formation Root.

The root answers:

What operational occurrence anchors this event formation?

It does not answer:

Where does the identity, ownership, or authority of the actor
originate?

Therefore:

Event Root ≠ Identity Origin.

And:

Root Association ≠ Provenance Governance.

This protects the boundary between TREGA™ and architectures such
as OBIDENITY®.

Formation Under Uncertainty

Not every recognized occurrence can immediately be formed with
complete structural confidence.

Records may conflict.

Component relationships may be unclear.

The operational root may remain uncertain.

Distributed systems may support several plausible formations.

TREFS must preserve this uncertainty rather than manufacturing a
structure merely because the implementing system requires one.

A case may remain:

Formation Unresolved

until a legitimate formation basis becomes available.

This preserves structural integrity.

Formation Integrity

Before the formed event enters Event Boundary governance, its
structural formation must be sufficiently coherent.

Formation Integrity asks whether the resulting event structure is
methodologically legitimate.

It does not independently validate every underlying claim.

It does not perform an audit.

Therefore:

Formation Integrity ≠ Validation.

and:

Formation Integrity ≠ Evidence Audit.

TREFS may consume governed outputs from VALIDOS™, ADIT®,
INTEGROS®, or other relevant architectures without absorbing
their governed spaces.

Formation Independence from Tax Law

A critical purpose of TREFS is to prevent tax rules from defining
the operational event backward.

The event should first be formed from governed operational
reality.

Jurisdiction-specific tax systems may later interpret that event
differently.

For example, the same formed event may later receive different
legal treatment under several jurisdictions.

The operational event does not need to be structurally rebuilt
each time the legal interpretation changes.

Therefore:

Tax Rule Change ≠ Event Reformation by Default.

This is fundamental to TREGA™ interoperability.

Formation Before Boundary

The immediate output of TREFS is a Governed Formed Event.

It is sufficiently coherent to enter TREBS — Tax-Relevant Event
Boundary Standard.

TREBS then determines: what belongs inside the event; what
remains outside it; how adjacent activity is separated; whether
the event boundary requires revision.

Therefore:

Formation Complete ≠ Boundary Determined.

And:

Boundary Determined ≠ Legally Taxable.

It is the structurally governed event representation required for
the next stage of TREGA™. Use Case 1 — Cross-Border Digital
Service Appears as Different Events in Every System

A global platform facilitates a digital service involving a
customer, platform operator, payment provider, service provider,
cloud infrastructure, and enterprise accounting system.

The marketplace records an order.

The payment provider records authorization and settlement.

The service platform records activation and consumption.

The provider records fulfilment.

The ERP creates an accounting transaction.

TRERS has already established that the underlying occurrence

Without TREFS, each system may define the event according to its
own technical representation.

One tax engine may use payment.

Another may use invoice creation.

Another may use service activation.

Another may receive several records and interpret them as several
events.

TREFS creates a governed formation layer across these
representations.

It identifies the materially relevant formation structure without
allowing any one source system to define the event automatically.

The result is a Governed Formed Event capable of entering Event
Boundary governance and subsequently supporting multiple
jurisdictional evaluations.

The structural benefit is fundamental:

the event is formed once from governed operational reality rather
than reconstructed separately by every downstream tax system. Use
Case 2 — Autonomous Machine Economy Generates Thousands of
Operations Around Several Economic Outcomes

An autonomous industrial network allows AI agents and machines to
negotiate and exchange energy, computing capacity, logistics
services, and digital resources.

A single autonomous workflow may produce thousands of: API calls;
reservations; machine allocations; state changes; service
invocations; transfers; settlement messages; automated releases.

TRERS has already removed technical noise and recognized the
occurrences that warrant further governance.

TREFS must now establish what governed event structures can
legitimately be formed from those recognized occurrences.

It may determine that several machine actions collectively
contribute to one coherent event formation while other actions
represent dependencies or separate formation candidates.

TREFS does not allow the number of technical machine operations
to determine the number of events.

Nor does it assume that one autonomous objective equals one
event.

This allows future tax infrastructure to process machine
economies without creating either millions of artificial events
or one artificially aggregated event around an entire autonomous
process.

Canonical Closing Statement

Tax-Relevant Event Formation Standard establishes the structural
formation layer of TREGA™ — Tax-Relevant Event Governance
Architecture™ by governing how sufficiently recognized human,
enterprise, digital, distributed, machine-generated, and
autonomous occurrences become coherent Governed Formed Events
before their final Event Boundary or jurisdiction-specific tax
consequences are determined. By separating technical records from
formation components, components from independent events,
dependencies from event membership, operational roots from
identity origin, distributed representations from automatic event
multiplicity, and formation integrity from legal taxability,
TREFS prevents operational systems, accounting structures,
machine execution granularity, shared identifiers, or tax-law
assumptions from defining the event prematurely. The Standard
creates the governed structural bridge between Event Recognition
and Event Boundary governance, enabling one operational reality
to acquire a reusable event representation capable of supporting
subsequent timing, location, participation, value, relationship,
determination, recording, lifecycle, and multi-jurisdictional
taxation analysis. TREFS therefore establishes not whether tax is
legally due and not yet where the final event ends, but what
coherent governed event structure has legitimately emerged from
the recognized occurrence and is now ready for its boundary to be
determined.
