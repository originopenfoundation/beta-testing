Simulation Output Limitation

Canonical Definition

Simulation Output Limitation Module (SOLM) defines the governance framework
through which material limitations attached to Simulation Outputs are
identified, classified, bounded, documented, propagated, and preserved so that
outputs cannot be interpreted, generalized, transferred, compared, or relied
upon beyond the conditions actually supported by the simulation that produced
them.

SOLM governs the boundary between:

what a Simulation Output shows

and:

what that output does not legitimately establish.

Its purpose is to prevent computational precision, high simulation volume,
visually convincing outputs, successful scenario execution, or apparently
stable results from expanding the legitimate meaning of a Simulation Output
beyond its governed simulation basis.

Operational Role

SOLM serves as the fourth internal governance space of the Simulation Output
Governance Standard (SOGS).

The preceding modules establish:

SOIM — What exactly did the simulation produce?

SOCM — What type of Simulation Output is it?

SIM — What inference, if any, may legitimately be drawn from that output?

SOLM asks:

Where does the legitimate meaning of that output stop, what conditions
constrain its interpretation, and which limitations must remain attached
whenever the output is communicated, compared, reused, or relied upon?

Module Function

SOLM exists to prevent: scenario-specific results being presented as universal
results, model-specific behavior being presented as real-world certainty,
nominal-condition outputs being generalized to edge or stress conditions,
outputs produced under one environment being applied to another without
qualification, outputs based on one population being generalized to another,
simulated time horizons being extended beyond what was modeled, stochastic
results being represented as deterministic truth, point estimates being
presented without material uncertainty, averages hiding adverse distributions,
incomplete Scenario Coverage being forgotten, unknown Scenario Space
disappearing from downstream interpretation, simulation capability limits
being mistaken for system limits, inferred results being presented as direct
observations, correlation being presented as causation, output precision being
confused with output certainty, and output reuse occurring after the governing
simulation conditions have materially changed.

Simulation Output Limitation

A Simulation Output Limitation is any material condition restricting what a
Simulation Output may legitimately: describe, imply, represent, compare,
generalize, predict, support, transfer, or be relied upon to establish.

A limitation may arise from any upstream SIMULOS® governance space.

A material output limitation should remain traceable to its origin where
possible.

Propagation

SIMULOS® establishes:

A material limitation does not disappear merely because the simulation
successfully produced an output.

If a limitation exists upstream and materially affects what the result can
mean, it should propagate into Output Governance.

Output Applicability Boundary

An Output Applicability Boundary defines where a Simulation Output may
legitimately apply.

Applicability may depend upon: Simulation Object version, system
configuration, population, environment, geography, time period, operating
state, scenario family, parameter range, input conditions, autonomy state, or
another relevant boundary.

Scope-Bound Output

A Simulation Output should remain bounded by the Simulation Purpose & Scope
under which it was produced.

For example:

simulation of braking behavior at 0–80 km/h

does not automatically support conclusions about:

120 km/h behavior.

No Scope Expansion Principle

SIMULOS® establishes:

A Simulation Output must not acquire a broader scope than the simulation
inquiry that produced it unless that broader applicability is independently
established.

Reality Representation Limitation

Outputs are constrained by what the simulation represented.

If Reality Representation excluded a material real-world feature, the
resulting Output cannot legitimately establish behavior involving that omitted
feature.

Therefore:

Unrepresented Reality → Unsupported Output
Claim Abstraction Limitation

A simulation may deliberately simplify reality.

An output derived from that abstraction should not be presented as if the
omitted detail had been simulated.

Fidelity Limitation

Simulation fidelity may be sufficient for one purpose but insufficient for
another.

For example:

a low-fidelity simulation may support early design exploration while remaining
unsuitable for detailed safety reliance.

SOLM preserves that boundary.

Assumption-Bound Limitation

Simulation Outputs may depend upon assumptions established under SAS.

Where an output materially depends upon an assumption, its interpretation
should remain conditional upon that assumption or upon the range within which
it remains applicable.

Conceptually:

Output Validity of Interpretation ≤ Applicability of Material Assumption No
Assumption Erasure Principle

A simulation assumption must not disappear from output interpretation when the
output materially depends upon that assumption.

Model-Bound Limitation

A Simulation Output reflects behavior of the governed simulation model.

It does not automatically establish equivalent behavior of the real system.

This distinction is fundamental:

Model Behavior ≠ Operational Reality by
Default Environment-Bound Limitation

The simulation environment may constrain the legitimate meaning of the output.

A result produced under:

Environment Version A

may not automatically transfer to:

Environment Version B

where material conditions differ.

Configuration Limitation

Outputs may apply only to a specific: hardware configuration, software
version, model version, sensor package, mission configuration, autonomy level,
or operational configuration.

Input-Bound Limitation

Simulation Outputs cannot legitimately exceed the representativeness, temporal
applicability, transformation basis, and uncertainty of the Simulation Inputs
on which they materially depend.

For example:

Input Population A

does not automatically support:

Output Claim for Population B.

Representativeness Limitation

Where SIDS identifies incomplete representativeness, that limitation should
remain attached to the output.

A simulation using input data representative only of:

urban daytime driving

cannot silently produce an output interpreted as representative of:

all driving environments.

Temporal Input Limitation

Historical or temporally restricted inputs may bound Output applicability.

For example:

an economic simulation using pre-regime-change data may not automatically
support post-regime conclusions.

Input Uncertainty Limitation

Material uncertainty in the Simulation Input basis should constrain downstream
interpretation.

A precise output derived from uncertain inputs may remain materially
uncertain.

No Precision Laundering Principle

SIMULOS® establishes:

A simulation may transform uncertain inputs into computationally precise
outputs, but it may not transform uncertainty into certainty merely through
computation.

Scenario-Bound Limitation

Outputs may apply only to the Scenario or Scenario Family under which they
were generated.

A nominal-scenario Output does not automatically establish behavior under:
edge cases, stress conditions, degraded states, failure conditions,
transitions, or recovery scenarios.

Scenario Coverage Limitation

A Simulation Output may be locally strong while the overall Scenario Set
remains incomplete.

SCCM may identify:

Scenario Coverage Gap

or:

Critical Scenario Coverage Gap.

SOLM ensures that such gaps constrain any broader Output claim.

No Coverage Inflation Principle

SIMULOS® establishes:

Strong results inside covered Scenario Space do not establish behavior inside
uncovered Scenario Space.

Unknown Scenario Space Limitation

Where unknown Scenario Space remains material, Output interpretation should
avoid implying exhaustive behavioral knowledge.

Edge-Case Limitation

Outputs produced under nominal or ordinary variation should not be interpreted
as demonstrating behavior at difficult edges unless those edges were
explicitly examined.

Stress-Condition Limitation

Successful performance under ordinary conditions does not establish robustness
under stress.

Likewise, one stress condition does not establish robustness across all stress
combinations.

Execution-Bound Limitation

A Simulation Output inherits relevant limitations from the execution that
produced it.

Examples include: execution deviation, interrupted run, incomplete run,
unexpected intervention, configuration mismatch, resource limitation,
execution drift, or incomplete observation.

Execution Completeness Limitation

An output from a partially completed execution should not be represented as if
it came from a complete execution where completion materially affects
interpretation.

Observation Limitation

SES may identify Observation Blind Spots.

Where material internal events or states were not observable, Output
interpretation should not imply knowledge about those hidden regions.

Randomness Limitation

Where simulation behavior is stochastic, one run may not characterize the
distribution of possible outcomes.

SOLM may therefore preserve a limitation such as:

Single stochastic realization — not sufficient to characterize outcome
distribution.

Repetition Limitation

Repeated runs may remain insufficient where: repetitions are too few,
configuration drift exists, seed coverage is narrow, relevant variation is
missing, or runs are not comparable.

Statistical Limitation

Simulation Outputs may have statistical limitations involving: sample size,
variance, confidence, distribution tails, sampling strategy, rare-event
representation, convergence, or estimator uncertainty.

Mean Limitation

Average behavior may conceal: extremes, variance, multimodality, adverse
tails, or subgroup differences.

SIMULOS® establishes:

An average Simulation Output does not represent every simulated condition or
every possible outcome.

Distribution Limitation

A distribution estimated from simulation is bounded by: scenario generation,
sampling method, assumptions, model behavior, input distributions, and number
of executions.

It is not automatically equivalent to the real-world distribution.

Rare-Event Limitation

Low observed frequency in simulation may result from: genuine rarity,
inadequate sampling, scenario-selection bias, insufficient run count, or
missing Scenario Space.

A rare simulated event rate should therefore not automatically be interpreted
as a reliable real-world probability.

Resolution Limitation

Output resolution may constrain interpretation.

Examples include: temporal resolution, spatial resolution, state resolution,
population granularity, behavioral granularity, or aggregated outputs.

No Resolution Inflation Principle

A Simulation Output cannot legitimately reveal distinctions that the
simulation did not represent or observe at sufficient resolution.

Temporal Output Limitation

A Simulation Output may apply only within a defined simulated time horizon.

For example:

0–24 hours

does not automatically support:

30-day behavior.

Long-Horizon Limitation

Uncertainty, model drift, state accumulation, or changing environments may
increase with simulated horizon.

Outputs should remain bounded accordingly.

Spatial Limitation

A simulation covering one: location, terrain, orbital region, climate,
infrastructure topology, or geographic region

does not automatically support spatial generalization elsewhere.

Population Limitation

Outputs may be bounded to the population represented by: data, actor models,
simulation scenarios, or relevant configuration.

Version Limitation

Simulation Outputs are attached to the versions that produced them.

Conceptually:

Output O1 → Model v3.1 + Environment v5.0
+ Input Set 22 + Scenario Set 7

A later model or environment version should not automatically inherit the
Output.

No Version Inheritance Principle

SIMULOS® establishes:

Simulation Outputs do not automatically remain applicable after a material
change in the Simulation Object, Model, Environment, Inputs, Parameters, or
Scenario Basis.

Inference Limitation

SIM — Simulation Inference Module establishes what may legitimately be
inferred from a Simulation Output.

SOLM establishes the boundary beyond which that inference must not expand.

Direct Output vs Inferred Meaning

For example:

Direct Output: simulated component temperature reached 112°C.

Possible inference:

Under the simulated conditions, the component model crossed the defined
thermal threshold.

Unsupported inference without further basis:

The real component will fail at exactly 112°C in all operational environments.

SOLM governs that distinction.

No Observation-to-Causation Principle

A simulated association does not automatically establish causal truth.

If:

Condition A

and:

Output B

occur together in simulation, causality requires a sufficient simulation and
inference basis.

No Simulation-to-Reality Equivalence
Principle

SIMULOS® establishes:

What occurred in simulation is evidence of simulated behavior under governed
simulated conditions; it is not automatically proof of identical behavior in
Operational Reality.

ORA™ and VALIDOS® may participate in downstream correlation and validation.

Generalization Limitation

A Generalization Limitation restricts extension of an output beyond the
represented conditions.

Generalization may concern: population, environment, geography, time,
configuration, system type, behavior, or operating condition.

Transfer Limitation

A Simulation Output generated for one Simulation Object should not
automatically be transferred to another object merely because the objects are
similar.

Cross-System Transfer

For example:

Robot Model A

may share architecture with:

Robot Model B

but differences in: mass, software, sensors, actuators, control logic, or
environment

may materially limit transfer.

Comparison Limitation

Two Simulation Outputs should not be compared as equivalent where material
differences exist in: Simulation Purpose, Scenario, Model, Environment,
Inputs, Parameters, execution conditions, output type, or inference basis.

No Apples-to-Oranges Principle

SIMULOS® establishes:

Numerically similar Simulation Outputs are not necessarily methodologically
comparable.

Reuse Limitation

A Simulation Output may be reused in: another analysis, another scenario,
another model, another validation process, another decision, or another
simulation.

Reuse should remain bounded by the Output's original applicability and
limitations.

No Context-Free Reuse Principle

A Simulation Output must not be detached from the conditions necessary to
understand what it legitimately means.

Extrapolation Limitation

Outputs should not be extrapolated outside: modeled ranges, simulated
horizons, Scenario Space, input distributions, or represented boundaries

without explicit governance.

Aggregation Limitation

Aggregated Simulation Outputs may hide important: scenario differences,
subgroup differences, tail behavior, failures, or contradictory outcomes.

Output Selection Limitation

If only selected outputs or successful runs are presented, interpretation may
become biased.

Material negative, anomalous, or contradictory outputs should remain visible
where they affect the legitimate conclusion.

No Cherry-Picked Output Principle

SIMULOS® establishes:

A Simulation Output claim must not be strengthened by selectively excluding
materially relevant outputs that contradict or weaken that claim.

Output Contradiction

Simulation Outputs may conflict across: scenarios, repetitions, versions,
parameter configurations, or model states.

A contradiction may itself create an Output Limitation.

Unresolved Contradiction Limitation

Where conflicting outputs cannot yet be reconciled, the legitimate inference
may need to remain:

Conditional

Partial

Conflicted

or:

Undetermined.

SOLM preserves the limitation.

Decision-Use Limitation

A Simulation Output may support:

exploration

without supporting:

deployment authorization.

It may support:

design comparison

without supporting:

safety certification.

It may support:

hypothesis generation

without supporting:

operational reliance.

The intended decision context matters.

Exploratory Output

An exploratory Simulation Output may legitimately support: investigation,
hypothesis generation, design direction, anomaly discovery, or scenario
refinement.

It should not automatically be represented as validated evidence for
high-consequence reliance.

Operational Reliance Limitation

SOGS does not authorize operational reliance.

VALIDOS®, ORA™, ASGA™, GOA™, or other relevant architectures may determine
what downstream reliance is legitimate.

SOLM preserves limitations that those downstream processes need to know.

Communication Limitation

Limitations should remain visible when Simulation Outputs are: reported,
visualized, summarized, presented to management, published, transferred
between teams, used by AI systems, or integrated into decision-support tools.

No Limitation Loss Through Summarization
Principle

SIMULOS® establishes:

A material Output Limitation must not disappear merely because the underlying
simulation record is summarized into a dashboard, chart, score, executive
summary, or AI-generated explanation.

Machine-Readable Limitation

Where Simulation Outputs are consumed automatically, limitations may require
machine-readable representation.

Examples may include: applicability tags, scenario constraints, object-version
references, uncertainty flags, scope boundaries, comparison restrictions, or
reliance warnings.

Not every known limitation requires equal prominence.

A limitation is material where ignoring it could reasonably change:
interpretation, inference, comparison, decision, validation, operational
reliance, or assessment of the Simulation Object.

Critical Output Limitation

produce materially misleading or high-consequence interpretation.

Resolved Limitation

No Silent Resolution Principle

SIMULOS® establishes:

A Simulation Output Limitation must not be treated as resolved merely because

Limitation Propagation Failure

Limitation-to-Output Binding

Every material limitation should be bound to the relevant: individual Output,
Output Set, Scenario Output, Campaign Output, derived result, or inference.

Limitation-to-Inference Binding

Where a specific limitation constrains a particular inference:

Output → Inference → Limitation

should remain traceable.

Limitation-to-Decision Binding

Where an Output is used for decision support, material limitations should
remain available to that decision process.

Limitation Reassessment Trigger

Reassessment may be required when: additional scenarios are executed, coverage
expands, new data becomes available, model fidelity improves, environment
changes, assumptions change, input uncertainty decreases, new validation
evidence appears, the Output is reused for another purpose, or a new reliance
context is proposed.

Output Limitation Change

A limitation may: narrow, expand, become more severe, become less severe,
resolve, or become newly discovered

as the simulation lifecycle evolves.

Historical Limitation Preservation

Later resolution of a limitation must not rewrite the limitation state of
earlier Output use.

Point-in-time reconstruction should remain possible.

No Retrospective Strengthening Principle

SIMULOS® establishes:

A later improvement in simulation capability must not retroactively strengthen
what an earlier Simulation Output legitimately established at the time it was
produced.

Simulation Output Boundary Statement

A governed Output may carry a concise boundary statement such as:

This Simulation Output applies only to the identified Simulation Object
version, Simulation Scope, Scenario Set, Model & Environment configuration,
Input & Data Basis, execution conditions, and Output limitations recorded for
the producing simulation activity.

Space and Mission Systems

Space and mission simulation creates particularly important Output Limitation
questions because operational reality may be difficult or impossible to
reproduce before deployment.

A mission simulation may produce:

95.8% successful autonomous recovery

across a large Monte Carlo campaign.

That number alone does not establish:

95.8% real-mission recovery probability.

SOLM asks:

Which mission conditions were represented?

Which communication states were absent?

Which hardware failures were modeled?

Which dependency combinations remained uncovered?

Which environmental uncertainties were simplified?

Was the recovery model validated against comparable operational behavior?

The Output may remain extremely useful.

But its legitimate meaning must remain bounded by the simulated conditions.

Autonomous Systems

An autonomous system may achieve:

99.9% simulated task success.

SOLM prevents that number from silently becoming:

99.9% operational safety

or:

99.9% real-world reliability

unless the required governance basis independently supports those claims.

The Output might legitimately establish:

99.9% success within the defined governed Scenario Set and simulation
conditions.

That is a stronger methodological statement because its boundary is explicit.

AI Agent Systems

An AI agent may complete:

98% of simulated workflows

within a test environment.

SOLM may reveal that: tool permissions were fixed, external systems were
stable, memory was always available, human instructions were unambiguous, and
authority transitions were not simulated.

The legitimate Output claim therefore remains bounded to that condition space.

Industrial Simulation

A manufacturer may simulate equipment under:

normal + elevated load

and observe no failures.

SOLM prevents that result from being generalized automatically to: extreme
temperature, degraded maintenance, sensor failure, long-duration operation, or
combined load and environmental stress

if those conditions were not represented.

Existing Simulation Program Diagnostic

SOLM can be applied to existing simulation programs by asking:

What claims are being made from our Simulation Outputs?

Which output limitations are explicit?

Which limitations exist upstream but disappear from reporting?

Do dashboards show precision without uncertainty?

Are nominal outputs being generalized to uncovered Scenario Space?

Are outputs from old Model Versions still being reused?

Are Monte Carlo frequencies being presented as real-world probabilities
without qualification?

Are outputs being used for decisions beyond the purpose for which the
simulation was designed?

Can each output claim be traced to its limiting conditions?

This can reveal that the key weakness is not the quality of the simulation
itself but the way its outputs are interpreted and communicated.

Module Boundary

SOLM begins when a Simulation Output has been sufficiently: identified under
SOIM, classified under SOCM, and associated with a governed inference basis
under SIM.

SOLM ends when the materially relevant limitations affecting: interpretation,
inference, applicability, generalization, comparison, transfer, reuse, and
downstream reliance

have been sufficiently identified, classified, bounded, documented, and
attached to the affected Simulation Output.

SOLM does not govern: general integrity, data integrity, output tamper
resistance, validation determination, operational deployment authorization,
general risk management, or final reliance authorization.

Integrity remains under INTEGROS®.

Architectural Boundary

SOLM specifically avoids the concept:

Output Integrity

because that governance space belongs to INTEGROS®.

The architectural distinction is:

SOLM → What limitations constrain the meaning and use of this Simulation
Output?

INTEGROS® → Has the relevant Output or record preserved the integrity
properties required of it?

This separation prevents SIMULOS® from duplicating integrity governance.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Identify Material Output Limitations

Determine which upstream or output-specific conditions materially constrain
interpretation. 2. Classify Limitation Origin

Identify whether the limitation arises from: scope, representation,
assumption, model, environment, inputs, scenarios, execution, observation,
statistics, uncertainty, or inference.

3. Assess Limitation Materiality

Determine whether ignoring the limitation could materially alter
interpretation, comparison, validation, or reliance. 4. Establish Output
Applicability Boundary

Define where the Output may and may not legitimately apply. 5. Establish
Inference Boundary

Determine what the Output may support and which broader conclusions remain
unsupported. 6. Establish Generalization and Transfer Restrictions

Identify restrictions upon use across: populations, environments, versions,
systems, scenarios, time periods, and configurations.

7. Bind Limitations to Outputs

Ensure each material limitation remains attached to the affected Output or
Output Set. 8. Establish Propagation Requirements

Determine where limitations must remain visible in downstream: documentation,
reporting, validation, analysis, decision support, and reuse.

9. Define Reassessment Triggers

Identify which new simulations, evidence, model changes, Scenario expansions,
or reuse contexts require limitation reassessment. 10. Preserve Historical
Limitation State

Maintain the limitation record applicable at the time the Output was produced
and used.

Use Case 1 — Spacecraft Autonomous Landing
Simulation

Scenario

A spacecraft landing simulation produces a result showing:

99.4% successful autonomous landing

across a large governed Simulation Campaign.

The number appears highly convincing.
Application

SOLM identifies material limitations: only the modeled terrain classes were
represented, communication-loss conditions were limited, certain
sensor-failure combinations remained outside Scenario Coverage, atmospheric
uncertainty was bounded to a defined range, the Output applies to one vehicle
configuration, and the result is a simulated campaign frequency, not a direct
empirical mission-success probability.

Result

The legitimate Output statement becomes:

99.4% simulated landing success across the governed Scenario Set, Model &
Environment configuration, Input basis, and uncertainty conditions represented
by the identified Simulation Campaign.

The limitation does not weaken the simulation.

It prevents a strong simulation result from being converted into a stronger
real-world claim than the simulation can legitimately support. Use Case 2 —
Autonomous Vehicle Safety Simulation

Scenario

An autonomous-driving platform reports:

99.99% collision-free simulation performance. Application

SOLM identifies that most Simulation Executions occur within: mapped urban
environments, defined weather ranges, supported sensor configurations, known
road-user classes, and a bounded Scenario Set.

Some compound edge conditions remain
uncovered. Result

The organization cannot legitimately communicate the Output as:

99.99% real-world safety.

The governed Output remains:

99.99% collision-free performance within the identified simulated conditions
and Scenario Coverage basis.

The remaining limitations become explicit inputs to further Scenario
expansion, validation, and operational reliance assessment.

Foundational Principle

A Simulation Output is only as broadly meaningful as the governed conditions
under which it was produced, and no output may legitimately escape the
assumptions, representation, data, scenarios, execution conditions,
uncertainty, and coverage boundaries that materially constrain what it can
demonstrate.
