Canonical Definition

The Tax-Relevant Event Location Standard (TRELS) governs how a
temporally situated real-world, economic, operational,
transactional, digital, autonomous, machine-generated,
distributed, or value-affecting event is spatially situated
through the identification, differentiation, association,
correlation, qualification, and governance of the physical,
digital, distributed, and operational locations relevant to that
event.

TRELS establishes a governed location representation for the
Tax-Relevant Event before jurisdiction-specific taxation rules
are applied.

The Standard determines how location information becomes
legitimately associated with the governed event, how multiple or
conflicting location signals are differentiated, how physical and
digital locations coexist, how distributed events are spatially
represented, and how location uncertainty, ambiguity, change, and
correlation are governed.

TRELS does not determine which country, state, municipality, tax
authority, or other legal jurisdiction has the legal right to tax
the event.

Event Location ≠ Tax Jurisdiction.

A governed operational location may subsequently become an input
into one or more jurisdiction-specific tax determinations, but
TRELS itself does not make that legal determination.
------------------------------ 1. Purpose

The purpose of TRELS is to establish a consistent, traceable, and
jurisdiction-neutral governance structure for determining where a
Tax-Relevant Event occurred operationally.

Modern events frequently cannot be represented by a single
physical location.

An event may involve: a buyer in one country, a seller in
another, a digital platform operated elsewhere, a payment
processor in another jurisdiction, cloud infrastructure
distributed across multiple regions, an autonomous agent
operating without a meaningful human operating location, physical
goods moving between locations, digital services consumed
remotely, machines communicating across industrial networks, or
distributed infrastructure executing different components of the
same event.

Without governed Event Location, downstream taxation systems may
consume inconsistent, incomplete, technically incidental, or
contextually misleading location signals.

TRELS therefore establishes the governed spatial representation
of the event before tax law determines whether any particular
location creates jurisdictional significance.
------------------------------ 2. Core Question

Where did the governed event occur operationally, and how can its
physical, digital, distributed, and correlated locations be
represented without converting Event Location into Tax
Jurisdiction? ------------------------------ 3. Governance
Objective

TRELS shall ensure that Event Location is: operationally
grounded, associated with the governed event, distinguishable by
location type, sufficiently bounded, traceable to its relevant
source, capable of representing multiple locations, capable of
representing distributed events, appropriately qualified where
uncertain, preserved when conflicting location signals exist,
compatible with machine and autonomous activity, and suitable for
downstream jurisdiction-specific evaluation.

The Standard shall prevent location information from being
prematurely transformed into a legal conclusion regarding
taxation authority. ------------------------------ 4. TREGA™

------------------------------ 6. Event Location Governance

Event Location represents the operational spatial relationship
between the governed event and the locations materially relevant
to its occurrence.

Location governance may include: physical location, digital
location, infrastructure location, execution location, service
location, asset location, transaction location, device location,
machine location, network-associated location, distributed
location, origin or destination location where operationally
relevant, and correlated location structures.

A location becomes part of the governed event only where its
association with that event can be operationally justified.

Location Availability ≠ Event-Location Relevance.

The mere presence of a location signal does not make that
location part of the governed Event Location.
------------------------------ 7. Physical Event Location

TRELS governs physical location where the event or a materially
relevant component of the event occurs in physical space.

Physical location may relate to: people, goods, machinery,
facilities, infrastructure, assets, operational activities,
delivery, production, storage, movement, or physical execution.

Physical Event Location shall be distinguished from addresses or
geographic information that are merely administratively
associated with an actor.

Registered Address ≠ Physical Event Location by Default.

Participant Residence ≠ Event Location by Default.

Corporate Headquarters ≠ Event Location by Default.

A location must be connected to the operational reality of the
governed event. ------------------------------ 8. Digital Event
Location

Digital events may occur without a single meaningful physical
event location.

TRELS therefore governs digital location as a distinct spatial
representation.

Digital location may include materially relevant: platform
environments, service environments, application environments,
logical infrastructure regions, cloud regions, digital execution
environments, network environments, system domains, virtual
operational spaces, or other digitally bounded locations.

Digital location shall not automatically inherit the physical
location of the infrastructure on which the digital operation is
technically executed.

Server Location ≠ Digital Event Location by Default.

IP Address ≠ Event Location by Default.

Cloud Region ≠ Tax Jurisdiction.

Technical infrastructure may provide location evidence, but its
relevance must be governed in relation to the event.
------------------------------ 9. Distributed Event Location

A single governed event may legitimately involve multiple
operational locations.

TRELS shall therefore support distributed spatial representation.

A distributed event may involve:

Location A + Location B + Location C

↓

One Governed Distributed
Event Location Structure

The Standard shall not force distributed events into an
artificial single-location representation where operational
reality does not support one.

Distributed Event Location may include: simultaneous locations,
sequential locations, complementary locations, origin and
destination locations, execution and consumption locations,
machine-to-machine locations, physical-digital combinations, and
infrastructure-spanning locations.

Multiple Event Locations ≠ Multiple Events by Default.

Whether multiple operational activities constitute one event or
several events remains governed by TREGA™ Event Formation and
Event Boundary rules. ------------------------------ 10. Location
Signal Governance

TRELS may receive location signals from multiple sources.

Examples include: GPS data, device location, IP-derived location,
network information, platform records, logistics records,
facility records, machine telemetry, cloud infrastructure
metadata, payment records, shipping information, delivery
information, user-declared location, contractual information,
asset records, and operational system data.

Each location signal shall be evaluated according to its
relationship to the governed event.

Location Signal ≠ Governed Event Location.

A technically precise signal may still be operationally
irrelevant.

A less technically precise signal may be operationally material.

TRELS therefore governs relevance before location is incorporated
into the governed event representation.
------------------------------ 11. Location Source
Differentiation

Location sources shall remain distinguishable where their
meaning, origin, precision, or operational function differs.

The governed representation should preserve, where materially
relevant: source type, source origin, observed location, declared
location, inferred location, derived location, correlated
location, precision, confidence, temporal applicability, and
relevant qualification.

Location normalization shall not destroy materially relevant
source distinctions.

Normalized Location ≠ Original Location Evidence.
------------------------------ 12. Event Location Correlation

Some Event Locations cannot be established through one direct
location signal.

TRELS therefore governs location correlation.

Location correlation may combine multiple operational signals to
establish a coherent spatial representation.

For example:

Device Location + Operational Record + Asset Position + Network
Signal + Delivery Record

↓

Correlated Event Location

Correlation shall not create unsupported spatial certainty.

Where correlation remains probabilistic or incomplete, the
governed Event Location shall preserve that qualification.

Location Correlation ≠ Permission to Manufacture Precision.
------------------------------ 13. Location Precision

Event Location shall be represented at the level of spatial
precision justified by the available operational information.

Possible levels may include: exact coordinate, facility, site,
building, city, region, country, digital environment,
infrastructure region, distributed spatial structure, or another
operationally appropriate representation.

TRELS shall not require artificial precision.

Technical Location Precision ≠ Operational Location Precision.

If the event can legitimately be located only to a region, the
governed record shall not manufacture a specific address or
coordinate. ------------------------------ 14. Location Ambiguity

Location ambiguity exists where multiple spatial interpretations
remain operationally plausible.

TRELS shall preserve ambiguity where it cannot legitimately be
resolved.

A governed Event Location may therefore be: established,
qualified, distributed, conflicting, uncertain, or unresolved.

Location Ambiguity ≠ Permission for Arbitrary Location Selection.

Downstream systems shall be able to distinguish established
spatial facts from qualified or unresolved spatial
interpretations. ------------------------------ 15. Conflicting
Location Signals

Different systems may produce conflicting location information.

Examples include: device location conflicting with declared
location, shipping origin conflicting with transaction records,
platform location conflicting with infrastructure metadata,
machine telemetry conflicting with facility records, or digital
service records indicating multiple execution regions.

TRELS shall require conflicts to be identified rather than
silently collapsed.

Conflict resolution shall be based on governed operational
relevance, not convenience for downstream taxation.

Location Conflict ≠ Permission to Select the Most
Tax-Advantageous Location. ------------------------------ 16.
Location Change

Events may move through space during their lifecycle.

Examples include: transported goods, mobile machinery, autonomous
vehicles, moving devices, distributed computing workloads,
migrating cloud processes, or services executed across changing
infrastructure environments.

TRELS shall preserve materially relevant location change where
required to represent the event accurately.

A changing location does not automatically create a new event.

Location Change ≠ New Event by Default.

Event identity and boundary remain governed by the relevant
upstream TREGA™ standards. ------------------------------ 17.
Machine and Autonomous Event Location

Machine-generated and autonomous events may produce complex
spatial structures.

A machine or autonomous agent may: initiate activity in one
environment, execute through another, interact with physical
infrastructure elsewhere, consume digital services across
distributed infrastructure, and complete an operational event
without a directly involved human actor.

TRELS shall distinguish between: machine physical location,
machine operational location, autonomous execution environment,
infrastructure location, network location, digital service
location, and other materially relevant spatial signals.

Machine Location ≠ Event Location by Default.

Autonomous Execution Location ≠ Tax Jurisdiction.

The governed Event Location shall represent operational reality
rather than assuming that the physical position of a server,
robot, device, or autonomous agent independently determines the
event's legal jurisdiction. ------------------------------ 18.
Physical-Digital Hybrid Events

Many modern events cross physical and digital spaces.

Examples include: e-commerce, digitally controlled manufacturing,
autonomous logistics, smart infrastructure, remote professional
services, IoT transactions, digital asset operations linked to
physical assets, and AI-mediated commercial activity.

TRELS shall allow both physical and digital location dimensions
to coexist within the same governed Event Location.

Physical Location ≠ Digital Location.

Neither dimension shall automatically override the other.

Their operational relevance depends on the structure of the
governed event. ------------------------------ 19. Event Location
States

TRELS may assign governance states
including: Location Ready

Sufficient location information exists to begin location
governance.

Location Under Governance

Location signals are being identified, differentiated,
associated, or correlated.

Location Qualified

A location representation exists but requires explicit
qualification.

Location Distributed

The event legitimately occupies multiple relevant locations.

Location Conflicted

Material location signals remain inconsistent.

Location Unresolved

Available information is insufficient to establish a legitimate
spatial representation.

Location Established

The event has a sufficiently governed operational location
representation.

Governed Temporally and Spatially Situated
Event

The event has completed TRELS sufficiently to proceed to Event
Participation governance. ------------------------------ 20.
Event Location and Tax Jurisdiction

TRELS establishes operational spatial facts.

Tax jurisdiction is a legal determination performed outside
TRELS.

A jurisdiction may later consider: event location, participant
location, establishment, residence, destination, origin, place of
supply, asset location, consumption location, permanent
establishment, economic nexus, statutory sourcing rules, treaty
rules, or other jurisdiction-specific legal criteria.

TRELS does not decide how those rules apply.

The same governed Event Location may legitimately be evaluated
differently by multiple jurisdictions.

One Governed Event Location

↓

Multiple Jurisdictional Evaluations

This is a core design principle of TREGA™.
------------------------------ 21. Jurisdiction Readiness

TRELS contributes spatial information required for eventual
jurisdiction-specific evaluation.

However:

Jurisdiction-Ready ≠ Jurisdiction-Determined.

TRELS may establish that an event has: a physical location,
multiple physical locations, a digital location, distributed
locations, origin and destination locations, correlated
locations, or unresolved location components.

It shall not conclude that any location creates legal taxing
authority. ------------------------------ 22. Cross-Architecture

TRELS owns the operational spatial representation of the
Tax-Relevant Event. ------------------------------ 23. Governance
Requirements

A TRELS-conformant implementation shall:

1. identify location information potentially relevant to the
governed event; 2. distinguish physical, digital, distributed,
and other operational location types; 3. associate location
signals with the governed event; 4. preserve materially relevant
location-source distinctions; 5. assess operational relevance
before accepting location signals; 6. support multiple legitimate
Event Locations; 7. support distributed Event Location; 8.
preserve location precision honestly; 9. identify uncertainty and
ambiguity; 10. identify materially conflicting location signals;
11. support location correlation where direct location evidence
is insufficient; 12. preserve materially relevant location
change; 13. support machine-generated and autonomous Event
Locations; 14. distinguish technical infrastructure location from
operational Event Location; 15. preserve traceability of governed
location representations; 16. prevent Event Location from being
represented as Tax Jurisdiction; 17. prevent location governance
from determining legal taxability; 18. produce a sufficiently
governed spatial representation for downstream TREGA™ governance.

------------------------------ 24. Governance Logic

TRELS applies the following canonical logic:

IF a Governed Temporally Situated Event exists AND location
signals relevant to that event can be identified THEN those
signals shall be differentiated and associated according to their
operational role.

IF multiple locations are materially relevant THEN the event
shall support a distributed or multi-location representation
rather than forcing artificial singularity.

IF location signals conflict THEN the conflict shall be governed,
qualified, or preserved rather than silently resolved.

IF location precision exceeds what operational evidence supports
THEN the representation shall be reduced to the justified level
of precision.

IF an operational Event Location is sufficiently governed THEN
TRELS may produce a Governed Temporally and Spatially Situated
Event.

TRELS SHALL NOT infer Tax Jurisdiction solely from Event
Location. ------------------------------ 25. Minimum
Implementation Framework

A minimum TRELS implementation should
provide: 1. Location Signal Register

Identifies candidate spatial information.
2. Location Type Classification

Distinguishes physical, digital, distributed, technical,
inferred, and other location roles. 3. Event-Location Association

Connects legitimate location information to the governed event.
4. Location Source Record

Preserves materially relevant source information. 5. Location
Precision Record

Defines justified spatial precision. 6. Location Conflict and
Ambiguity Record

Preserves unresolved or conflicting spatial information. 7.
Distributed Location Structure

Represents events spanning multiple locations. 8. Location
Correlation Structure

Documents legitimate correlation between spatial signals. 9.
Location Governance State

Records the current governance status. 10.
Event Location Output

Produces the governed spatial representation passed downstream.

28. Enterprise and Infrastructure Application

TRELS may operate as a jurisdiction-neutral Event Location layer
between operational systems and taxation infrastructure.

Example architecture:

ERP / Commerce / Banking / Digital Platform / AI Agent / Machine
System ↓ TREGA™ Event Layer ↓ TRELS — Governed Event Location ↓
Governed Temporally and Spatially Situated Event ↓ Country A Tax
Engine / Country B Tax Engine / Country C Tax Engine ↓
Jurisdiction-Specific Tax Determination

This allows multiple tax systems to consume the same governed
operational event without requiring each system to reconstruct
spatial reality independently. ------------------------------ 29.
Parent Standard Internal Module Flow

TRELS is operationalized through five Core Modules: 1. ELIM —
Event Location Identification Module

Identifies location information potentially relevant to the
governed event. 2. ELPM — Event Physical Location Module

Governs materially relevant physical Event Locations. 3. ELDM —
Event Digital Location Module

Governs materially relevant digital Event Locations. 4. ELDSM —
Event Distributed Location Module

Governs events whose legitimate spatial representation spans
multiple locations. 5. ELCM — Event Location Correlation Module

Correlates relevant location information into a coherent governed
spatial representation.

Internal Module Progression

ELIM — What location information legitimately belongs to the
event? ↓ ELPM — Where did the event occur in physical operational
space? ↓ ELDM — Where did the event occur in digital operational
space? ↓ ELDSM — How is the event represented when it spans
multiple legitimate locations? ↓ ELCM — How are relevant location
dimensions correlated into a coherent governed Event Location? ↓
Governed Temporally and Spatially Situated Event

The modules shall preserve the distinction between operational
spatial governance and jurisdiction-specific legal
interpretation. ------------------------------ 30. Relationship
to the Next Parent Standard

TRELS passes its governed output to:

TREPS — Tax-Relevant Event Participation
Standard

The transition is:

Governed Temporally Situated Event ↓ TRELS — Event Location
Governance ↓ Governed Temporally and Spatially Situated Event ↓
TREPS — Event Participation Governance

TRELS answers:

Where did the event occur operationally?

TREPS subsequently answers:

Who or what participated in the event, and in what operational
role?

TREPS does not convert Event Location into participant identity,
and TRELS does not infer Event Participation merely from spatial
presence. ------------------------------ 31. Canonical Boundary

TRELS owns the governance of the operational spatial
representation of a Tax-Relevant Event, including: Event Location
identification, physical Event Location, digital Event Location,
distributed Event Location, location-source differentiation,
location association, location correlation, spatial precision,
location ambiguity, location conflict, materially relevant
location change, machine and autonomous Event Location, and
readiness for Event Participation governance.

TRELS does not own: Event Context, Event Recognition, Event
Formation, Event Root Association, Event Boundary, Event Timing,
Event Participation, participant identity, taxpayer
determination, value lifecycle governance, final Value
Association, Event Relationships, final Event Determination, tax
jurisdiction, tax nexus, place-of-supply determination,
permanent-establishment determination, legal tax point, tax
period, tax base, tax rate, exemptions, deductions, legal
taxability, tax liability, filing obligations, reporting
obligations, collection, payment, or enforcement.

Those responsibilities remain with their respective TREGA™
standards, other OOF® architectures, or jurisdiction-specific
legal and taxation systems. ------------------------------

Canonical Closing Statement

TRELS governs where the event occurred as governed operational
reality — not which jurisdiction has the legal authority to tax
it.

Event Location ≠ Tax Jurisdiction.

TREGA™ governs the event. The jurisdiction governs the tax.
