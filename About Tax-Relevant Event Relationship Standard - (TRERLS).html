Tax-Relevant Event Relationship Standard?

The Tax-Relevant Event Relationship Standard (TRERLS) is the
TREGA™ Parent Standard governing how already-governed
Tax-Relevant Events relate to one another.

Modern economic activity rarely consists of one isolated action.

A single commercial, financial, digital, industrial, or
autonomous activity may generate an order, authorization,
payment, settlement, delivery, resource allocation, asset
movement, refund, reversal, machine execution, smart-contract
interaction, or numerous other operational occurrences.

The central methodological problem is that these occurrences do
not automatically correspond one-to-one with Tax-Relevant Events.

One economic activity may contain several governed events.

At the same time:

Multiple technical operations may belong to one governed event.

TRERLS provides the relationship layer needed to represent this
complexity without prematurely collapsing several events into one
or artificially fragmenting one event into many.

Its foundational principle is:

Event Relationship ≠ Event Identity.

Why TRERLS Exists

Digital economies produce enormous numbers of operational
records.

An ordinary online purchase may generate records across: commerce
infrastructure; ERP systems; payment processors; banks; logistics
providers; marketplaces; accounting systems; and tax systems.

Blockchain and autonomous environments can produce even more
fragmented operational structures through smart-contract calls,
ledger transactions, agent instructions, machine actions,
resource allocations, and automated settlements.

The existence of multiple records does not tell us how many
economically meaningful governed events actually occurred.

More Records ≠ More Events.

The opposite problem is equally important.

Several operational activities may belong to one commercial
arrangement while still constituting materially distinct governed
events.

Therefore:

One Contract ≠ One Event by default.

One Invoice ≠ One Event by default.

One Payment ≠ One Event by default.

Without an explicit Event Relationship governance layer, systems
risk confusing technical architecture, business process
structure, economic activity, and tax interpretation.

TRERLS separates these questions.

The Problem TRERLS Solves

Consider a global marketplace transaction.

A customer places an order.

A payment is authorized.

A merchant accepts the order.

Goods are shipped.

A platform fee is generated.

Payment is settled.

A partial refund occurs later.

The systems involved may contain dozens of records.

There is no universal rule saying that these records represent:
one event; seven events; dozens of events; or some other event
structure.

That structure must be governed.

TRERLS provides the methodology for representing relationships
among the events after the relevant events have already been
formed, bounded, temporally and spatially situated, associated
with participants, and connected with governed value.

It allows the resulting structure to remain faithful to
operational reality before jurisdiction-specific tax law is
applied.

What TRERLS Governs

TRERLS governs relationships among already-governed Tax-Relevant
Events.

These relationships may include: dependency; direction; sequence;
parallel relationships; conditional relationships; parent–child
structures; component relationships; grouping; Event Chains;
Event Networks; convergence; divergence; reversal; refund
relationships; replacement; supersession; continuation; merge;
split; uncertainty; conflict; and relationship reconsideration.

The purpose is not to force every event structure into one
universal pattern.

The purpose is to make the relationship structure explicit and
governable.

Independent Events Can Still Be Related

Two events do not need to become one event simply because they
are strongly related.

A purchase and later refund may remain separate events.

A payment may depend on an earlier commercial event.

A settlement may combine value originating from several earlier
events.

A delivery may follow a purchase without becoming identical to
that purchase.

TRERLS allows those relationships to remain explicit.

Related Events ≠ Same Event by default.

Likewise:

Event Dependency ≠ Event Merger.

This distinction is essential for reconstructing complex economic
activity accurately.

One Economic Activity Does Not
Automatically Mean One Event

Business language frequently describes an entire process as one
“transaction.”

Governance may reveal a more complex structure.

A commercial relationship may contain several independently
formed events connected through dependency, sequence, value,
participants, or common context.

TRERLS therefore preserves the principle:

One Economic Activity ≠ One Event by default.

This prevents operational convenience from becoming an Event
Identity rule.

Multiple Technical Operations Do Not
Automatically Mean Multiple Events

The opposite error is increasingly important in digital systems.

A single governed event may produce: several API calls; database
updates; payment messages; ledger entries; smart-contract
executions; blockchain transactions; machine instructions; or
autonomous-agent actions.

Technical systems naturally fragment execution into operations.

Governance must not automatically reproduce that fragmentation as
Event Reality.

Therefore:

Technical Operation ≠ Tax-Relevant Event by default.

and:

Multiple Technical Operations ≠ Multiple Economically Independent
Events by default.

Grouping Does Not Destroy Event Identity

Several related events may legitimately belong to a common Event
Group.

For example, events may belong to the same commercial activity,
settlement structure, contractual arrangement, supply chain, or
autonomous workflow.

Grouping makes the relationship visible.

It does not convert the group into one event.

Event Group ≠ Single Event.

Individual Event Identity remains preserved unless upstream Event
Formation and Boundary governance support another conclusion.

Why Merge and Split Matter

Sometimes an event structure initially appears different from
what later evidence supports.

Two events initially represented separately may later prove to be
one event.

One event may later prove to contain several materially
independent events.

TREGA™ therefore requires governed ways to represent merge and
split relationships.

However, these mechanisms cannot become tools for producing a
preferred downstream tax result.

Tax Advantage ≠ Event Merge Criterion.

Tax Exposure ≠ Event Split Criterion.

Operational reality must remain primary.

TRERLS preserves the relationship history while respecting the
authority of Event Formation and Event Boundary governance.
Refunds, Reversals, and Replacement Events

Later activity frequently modifies the economic consequences of
earlier activity.

A payment may be reversed.

Goods may be returned.

A service may be cancelled.

A refund may occur.

A failed execution may be replaced.

TRERLS allows these later events to remain structurally connected
to the earlier event without erasing history.

Reversal Event ≠ Original Event Erasure.

Replacement Event ≠ Original Event Nonexistence.

This makes the complete event structure reconstructable rather
than representing only its latest state.

Why TRERLS Matters for Banks and Payment
Infrastructure

Payment infrastructure frequently operates downstream from the
commercial activity producing the payment.

One payment may settle one event.

One payment may settle several events.

Several payments may settle one event.

A settlement may aggregate large numbers of earlier transactions.

Therefore:

One Payment ≠ One Event by default.

TRERLS provides the relationship layer needed to connect payment
events with other governed events without assuming that payment
architecture defines Event Reality.

Why TRERLS Matters for Blockchain

Blockchain infrastructure makes technical execution highly
visible.

That visibility is valuable, but it can also create a
methodological trap.

One economic event may involve several blockchain transactions.

One blockchain transaction may contain several operational
actions.

Some economically relevant activity may also occur off-chain.

Therefore:

One Blockchain Transaction ≠ One Tax-Relevant Event by default.

and:

Multiple Blockchain Transactions ≠ Multiple Tax-Relevant Events
by default.

TRERLS enables blockchain activity to participate in a broader
governed Event Relationship structure rather than allowing ledger
structure alone to define the economic event.

Why TRERLS Matters for AI Agents and
Autonomous Systems

Autonomous economies make Event Relationship governance even more
important.

An AI agent may initiate an activity.

Another agent may negotiate or execute part of it.

A machine may provide the physical service.

A resource system may allocate computational or energy capacity.

A smart contract may settle value.

Another autonomous process may later reverse or modify part of
the activity.

Dozens or thousands of technical actions may occur without direct
human execution.

TRERLS provides the methodology for determining how the resulting
governed events relate.

One Autonomous Workflow ≠ One Tax-Relevant Event by default.

Likewise:

Multiple Agent Actions ≠ Multiple Tax-Relevant Events by default.

This makes TRERLS applicable not only to current commerce but
also to emerging machine economies.

Why TRERLS Matters for Multi-Jurisdiction
Tax Infrastructure

Different jurisdictions may legally interpret the same sequence
of related events differently.

One jurisdiction may aggregate several related events for a
particular tax purpose.

Another may treat them independently.

A third may apply specific rules to a sequence, composite
arrangement, or related-party structure.

TREGA™ should not resolve those legal questions before the
jurisdiction applies its law.

Instead, TRERLS provides:

One Governed Event Relationship Structure

↓

Multiple Jurisdictional Evaluations

The governed operational structure remains stable.

The legal tax interpretation may differ.

This is central to TREGA™:

TREGA™ governs the event. The jurisdiction governs the tax.

What TRERLS Does Not Govern

TRERLS does not determine whether an occurrence constitutes a new
event.

That belongs primarily to Event Formation governance.

It does not independently decide where one event ends and another
begins.

That belongs to Event Boundary governance.

It does not determine Event Timing, Event Location, Event
Participation, or Value Association.

It also does not determine: taxable value; Tax Base; taxpayer
status; Tax Liability; tax jurisdiction; place of supply;
permanent establishment; taxable period; deduction; exemption;
tax rate; reporting obligation; or other jurisdiction-specific
tax consequences.

Most importantly:

Governed Event Relationship ≠ Tax Aggregation Determination.

A tax system may later legally aggregate events without changing
the underlying TREGA™ event structure.

How TRERLS Works at a High Level

TRERLS is operationalized through five Core Modules:

ERLIM — Event Relationship Identification Module Identifies
materially relevant relationships among governed events.

ERLDM — Event Relationship Dependency Module Governs dependency,
direction, conditionality, and structural dependence.

ERLSM — Event Relationship Sequence Module Governs temporal and
operational sequence without converting sequence into Event
Identity or causation.

ERLGM — Event Relationship Grouping Module Governs Event Groups,
chains, networks, parent–child structures, and other multi-event
structures while preserving individual Event Identity.

ERLMM — Event Relationship Merge & Split Module Governs justified
merge and split relationships while preserving historical
traceability and upstream Event Formation and Boundary authority.

Organizations Gain

TRERLS gives organizations a methodology for representing complex
event structures before tax law is applied.

This can provide: clearer separation between technical records
and economic events; stronger cross-system event reconstruction;
better representation of multi-step commercial activity; more
reliable relationships between payments and underlying events;
governed treatment of refunds and reversals; clearer blockchain
Event Relationships; support for AI-agent and machine-generated
economies; improved interoperability between operational systems
and tax engines; reusable event structures across jurisdictions;
and stronger historical reconstructability.

Most importantly, it prevents downstream systems from being
forced to decide what an event is merely because no governed
relationship layer exists. Use Case 1 — Global Marketplace with
Payment, Delivery, Refund, and Settlement

A global marketplace processes a customer purchase.

The activity produces a purchase event, payment event, merchant
settlement event, delivery event, platform-fee event, and later a
partial refund event.

Several events share participants and Value References.

The merchant settlement covers multiple purchases.

The refund relates only to part of the original activity.

Different systems represent the activity differently.

Without Event Relationship governance, one implementation may
collapse everything into one transaction while another may treat
every database or payment operation as an independent event.

TRERLS establishes the governed relationships among the events,
including dependency, sequence, shared value, grouping,
settlement convergence, and the relationship between the refund
and earlier events.

Individual Event Identities remain preserved where required while
the complete economic activity can still be represented as a
coherent Event Network.

A jurisdiction can subsequently decide whether its tax law
aggregates, separates, or otherwise interprets those events.

TRERLS preserves the governed reality on which that decision can
be based. Use Case 2 — Autonomous Machine and Blockchain Event
Network

An industrial AI agent identifies a requirement for additional
computational capacity and initiates a resource request.

Another autonomous system accepts the request.

Machine capacity is allocated.

Energy is consumed.

A smart contract initiates digital-asset settlement.

Several blockchain operations execute that settlement.

A later autonomous process adjusts the consumed resources and
triggers a partial reversal.

The infrastructure generates numerous records across AI agents,
machines, blockchain infrastructure, resource systems, and
settlement mechanisms.

TRERLS allows the governed events to be represented as an Event
Network.

It identifies which events are dependent, which occur in
sequence, which belong to a common group, and how the later
reversal relates to earlier activity.

It does not assume that every smart-contract call, blockchain
transaction, or machine action constitutes a separate
Tax-Relevant Event.

It also does not assume that the entire autonomous workflow
constitutes one event merely because one AI system coordinated
it.

The resulting Governed Event Relationship Structure can proceed
to formal Event Determination and subsequently be evaluated under
the tax rules of one or more jurisdictions.

Canonical Boundary

TRERLS governs how already-governed Tax-Relevant Events relate to
one another.

It does not independently create those events, redefine their
boundaries, determine their participants or value, or determine
their legal tax treatment.

Its governance ends when the relevant dependency, sequence,
grouping, network, merge, split, reversal, replacement,
continuation, and other material Event Relationships are
sufficiently represented for downstream Tax-Relevant Event
Determination.

The canonical boundary is:

Event Relationship ≠ Event Identity.

and:

Governed Event Relationship ≠ Tax Aggregation Determination.

Canonical Closing Statement

TRERLS establishes the relationship structure required to
represent complex economic activity without confusing technical
operations, business processes, and legally taxable events.

One economic activity may contain several governed events.
Several technical operations may belong to one governed event.
The purpose of Event Relationship governance is to preserve that
difference before law interprets it.

Event Relationship ≠ Event Identity.

Events by default.
