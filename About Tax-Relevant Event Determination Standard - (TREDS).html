Governed Space: Tax-Relevant Event Determination What Is the
Tax-Relevant Event Determination Standard?

The Tax-Relevant Event Determination Standard (TREDS) is the
Parent Standard within TREGA™ that governs the formal
determination of whether an event has been sufficiently governed
to become a Tax-Relevant Event.

Before reaching TREDS, an event may already have a governed
Context, Formation, Boundary, Timing, Location, Participation
structure, Value Association, and Event Relationship structure.

TREDS asks the next critical question:

Is the resulting event sufficiently complete, coherent, stable,
and reconstructable to be formally admitted as a Tax-Relevant
Event for downstream taxation analysis?

TREDS therefore establishes the determination threshold between
Event governance and the subsequent creation of a governed Event
Record.

It does not determine the tax.

Why TREDS Exists

Modern taxation infrastructure often receives information from
systems designed for completely different purposes: ERP
platforms; payment processors; marketplaces; accounting systems;
banks; blockchain networks; digital platforms; AI agents;
autonomous systems; industrial infrastructure; machine-to-machine
environments.

These systems may generate transactions, logs, records,
instructions, settlements, payments, ledger entries, API calls,
or machine operations.

But the existence of a technical record does not establish that a
sufficiently governed Tax-Relevant Event exists.

Without a formal determination layer, downstream tax systems may
be forced to decide simultaneously:

What happened?

How many events occurred?

Who participated?

Where did the event occur?

What value belongs to it?

How is it related to other events?

And what does tax law do with it?

TREDS separates those questions.

TREGA™ first governs the Event Reality.

Only afterward does jurisdiction-specific taxation infrastructure
interpret that governed reality under law.

The Problem TREDS Solves

The critical problem is the absence of a formal threshold
between:

an operational or digital occurrence

and:

an event sufficiently governed for taxation analysis.

A payment record may not be an independent Tax-Relevant Event.

An invoice may represent several events.

Several invoices may relate to one event.

A blockchain transaction may represent one event, part of an
event, several relationships, or merely a technical operation.

Hundreds of AI-agent actions may belong to one governed Event
structure.

A settlement may depend on hundreds of independently governed
events.

TREDS prevents these technical representations from entering
downstream taxation systems as unquestioned Event Reality.

Technical Record ≠ Determined Tax-Relevant Event.

What TREDS Governs

TREDS governs the formal Event Determination layer.

At a high level, this includes: Determination Readiness;
Determination Conditions; material sufficiency; governance
coherence; Event stability; Determination Decision; Determination
qualification; Determination uncertainty; Determination conflict;
Determination reasoning; Determination reconsideration;
Determination correction; Determination supersession; and formal
establishment of a Determined Tax-Relevant Event.

TREDS does not repeat the governance performed by earlier Parent
Standards.

Instead, it evaluates whether their combined outputs are
sufficiently governed to support formal Event Determination. What
Does “Tax-Relevant” Mean?

Within TREGA™, Tax-Relevant has a precise methodological meaning.

It means that an event has been governed sufficiently to become
eligible for downstream taxation evaluation.

It does not mean that tax is legally due.

Therefore:

Tax-Relevant Event ≠ Legally Taxable Event.

A Determined Tax-Relevant Event may later be evaluated by a
jurisdiction and found to be: taxable; non-taxable; exempt;
outside jurisdiction; outside scope; subject to special
treatment; reportable but not taxable; or legally irrelevant
under a particular tax regime.

Those are downstream legal conclusions.

TREDS does not make them.

Event Determination Is Not Tax
Determination

This distinction defines the boundary of the Standard.

TREDS determines:

“Is there a sufficiently governed Tax-Relevant Event?”

A jurisdiction-specific tax system determines:

“What does the applicable tax law do with this event?”

The two decisions must remain separate.

Event Determination ≠ Taxability Determination.

This separation allows the same governed Event Reality to support
multiple legitimate legal interpretations.

Determination Readiness

Not every event reaching TREDS is automatically ready for
determination.

TREDS evaluates whether materially necessary upstream governance
has been completed sufficiently.

Depending on the event, this may require governed information
concerning: Context; Recognition; Formation; Boundary; Timing;
Location; Participation; Value Association; Event Relationships.

The requirement is not maximum information.

The requirement is materially sufficient governance.

Determination Completeness ≠ Total Data Capture.

A simple event may require relatively little governance
information.

A distributed cross-border autonomous transaction may require
substantially more.

Material Sufficiency

TREDS is based on material sufficiency rather than mechanical
data completeness.

An event should contain enough governed information that
downstream taxation infrastructure can evaluate it without
reconstructing basic Event Reality from raw operational data.

The question is therefore not:

“Do we have every possible field?”

It is:

“Do we have enough governed Event Reality to support a defensible
downstream evaluation?”

This makes TREDS applicable across both simple and highly complex
Event structures.

Governance Coherence

Completeness alone is insufficient.

The upstream Event structure must also be coherent.

For example: Formation should be compatible with Boundary; Timing
should correspond to the governed Event; Participation should
relate to the correct Event; Value Association should attach to
the correct Event; Event Relationships should preserve Event
Identity.

Material contradictions may prevent a positive determination.

Data Availability ≠ Governance Coherence.

TREDS therefore acts as a synthesis point across the preceding
Event governance layers.

Determination Outcomes

TREDS does not force every event into a binary yes/no result.

Depending on governance conditions, an event may become:

Tax-Relevant Event Determined

Determination Conditional

Determination Deferred

Determination Rejected

Determination Uncertain

Determination Conflicted

Determination Under Reconsideration

This preserves operational reality rather than hiding uncertainty
merely to produce a downstream answer.

No Decision ≠ Positive Determination.

Conditional and Deferred Determination

A Conditional Determination may be appropriate where an event is
sufficiently governed for controlled downstream use but a bounded
condition remains.

A Deferred Determination is appropriate where materially
necessary governance is still missing.

These states are materially different.

Conditional Determination ≠ Unqualified Determination.

Deferred Determination ≠ Rejected Event.

An event may become determinable once the missing governance is
completed.

Rejected Determination

TREDS may reject determination where the apparent event fails the
required Event governance threshold.

For example, an apparent “event” may ultimately prove to be: a
duplicate technical record; an internal system operation; an
incorrectly recognized occurrence; an invalid Event Formation; or
an Event representation whose Boundary cannot be legitimately
established.

Rejection means the structure does not currently qualify as a
governed Tax-Relevant Event.

It does not mean that tax law has concluded that the activity is
non-taxable.

Determination Rejection ≠ Legal Non-Taxability.

Uncertainty and Conflict

Real operational systems are imperfect.

Data may be incomplete.

Systems may disagree.

Participant information may conflict.

Event Boundaries may be reconsidered.

Relationships may be ambiguous.

TREDS does not hide these conditions.

It governs their effect on Event Determination.

TREDS does not seize ownership of that problem.

Instead, it can refer the matter back to the appropriate
governance layer.

Why Determination Is Different from
Recognition

TREGA™ deliberately separates Event Recognition from Event
Determination.

TRERS operates near the beginning of the lifecycle.

It asks whether an occurrence should enter Event governance.

TREDS operates after the event has passed through the major
governance layers.

It asks whether the resulting governed structure is sufficient to
become a formal Tax-Relevant Event.

Therefore:

Event Recognition ≠ Event Determination.

Recognition opens the governance process.

Determination establishes whether that process has produced a
sufficiently governed Event.

Why Determination Is Different from
Formation

TREFS establishes what constitutes the Event.

TREDS does not reform the Event.

It evaluates whether the formed, bounded, situated, attributed,
value-associated, and relationship-governed Event is ready for
formal determination.

Event Formation ≠ Event Determination.

A defective Formation cannot be repaired merely by issuing a
positive Determination.

Technical Systems Do Not Define the Event

TREDS remains independent of the technical system producing the
data.

Therefore:

Invoice ≠ Tax-Relevant Event by default.

Payment ≠ Tax-Relevant Event by default.

Blockchain Transaction ≠ Tax-Relevant Event by default.

Smart-Contract Execution ≠ Tax-Relevant Event by default.

Agent Action ≠ Tax-Relevant Event by default.

Machine Operation ≠ Tax-Relevant Event by default.

The Event must first pass through TREGA™ governance.

This prevents technical architecture from silently becoming
tax-event methodology.

AI and Autonomous Systems

TREDS is designed to remain applicable as economic activity
becomes increasingly autonomous.

An AI agent may negotiate a service.

Another agent may allocate resources.

A machine may execute the service.

Sensors may measure consumption.

A settlement system may transfer value.

A smart contract may execute part of the settlement.

These technical actions do not automatically establish how many
Tax-Relevant Events exist.

TREGA™ governs that structure first.

TREDS then determines whether the resulting events are
sufficiently governed for downstream taxation analysis.

Agent Action Count ≠ Tax-Relevant Event Count.

Machine Operation Count ≠ Tax-Relevant Event Count.

Multi-Jurisdiction Governance

TREDS is jurisdiction-neutral.

Its purpose is to create a stable governed Event Reality that can
support different downstream legal evaluations.

The model is:

One Determined Tax-Relevant Event

↓

Multiple Jurisdictional Evaluations

Country A may interpret the event one way.

Country B may interpret it differently.

Country C may determine that the event produces no tax
consequence.

The underlying governed Event does not need to be reconstructed
separately for each jurisdiction.

This is one of the core infrastructure advantages of TREGA™.

Jurisdiction-Ready Does Not Mean
Jurisdiction-Determined

TREDS may establish that an event is sufficiently governed for
jurisdiction-specific analysis.

It does not determine which jurisdiction has taxing rights.

Therefore:

Jurisdiction-Ready ≠ Jurisdiction-Determined.

Event Location provides governed operational location
information.

Tax jurisdiction remains a legal determination downstream.

What TREDS Does Not Govern

TREDS does not determine: legal taxability; Tax Jurisdiction; tax
nexus; taxpayer status; legal tax point; tax period; taxable
value; Tax Base; Tax Rate; exemptions; deductions; withholding;
filing obligations; collection; payment; enforcement; or Tax
Liability.

It also does not replace upstream TREGA™ governance.

It does not own Event Formation, Event Boundary, Event Timing,
Event Location, Event Participation, Value Association, or Event
Relationship construction.

Its role is determination of the governed whole.

Cross-Architecture Boundary

TREDS operates within a wider OOF® governance environment.

permission, and provenance.

VFM® — Value Flow Mechanism governs what happened to value
throughout its lifecycle.

TREGA™ governs the Tax-Relevant Event lifecycle.

TREDS governs whether the resulting Event is sufficiently
governed to become a formal Tax-Relevant Event.

The jurisdiction-specific taxation system then determines legal
tax consequences.

What Organizations Gain

TREDS provides a formal Event-admission threshold before taxation
infrastructure.

This enables organizations to: stop raw technical records from
being mistaken for tax events; separate Event Reality from legal
tax interpretation; expose unresolved Event governance before it
reaches tax engines; preserve uncertainty instead of hiding it;
reuse the same governed Event across jurisdictions; improve
interoperability between operational and taxation systems;
support AI and autonomous economic activity; reduce repeated
reconstruction of the same Event across tax systems; and maintain
traceable reasoning for why an Event was admitted into downstream
taxation analysis.

Instead of every downstream tax engine independently asking “What
actually happened?”, TREGA™ provides a governed Event layer that
can be consumed consistently. Use Case 1 — Global Digital
Marketplace Across Multiple Tax Jurisdictions

A global marketplace processes a customer purchase involving a
merchant, payment provider, fulfillment provider, marketplace
commission, cross-border delivery, settlement, and later partial
refund.

Operational systems produce numerous records.

TREGA™ governs the relevant Events through Context, Recognition,
Formation, Boundary, Timing, Location, Participation, Value
Association, and Event Relationships.

TREDS then evaluates whether each resulting Event is sufficiently
governed for downstream taxation analysis.

A purchase Event may receive positive Determination.

A settlement Event may receive positive Determination.

A refund Event may remain conditional because one material Value
Association is unresolved.

The marketplace does not need to declare at TREDS level whether
VAT, sales tax, withholding, or another tax is legally due.

Instead, the resulting governed Events can be supplied to
multiple jurisdiction-specific tax systems.

One Governed Event Reality

↓

Multiple Jurisdictional Evaluations.
Use Case 2 — Autonomous Machine Economy with AI-Agent Settlement

An AI agent negotiates computational capacity with an autonomous
infrastructure provider.

Another system allocates resources.

Machines execute the workload.

Sensors measure consumption.

A pricing mechanism associates value with the service.

A digital settlement system transfers value automatically.

The infrastructure produces thousands of technical operations.

TREGA™ determines which of those operations form independently
governed Events and how they relate.

TREDS then evaluates whether the resulting service,
resource-consumption, and settlement Events have sufficient
Context, Formation, Boundary, Timing, Location, Participation,
Value Association, and Relationship governance to become formal
Tax-Relevant Events.

The determination does not decide which country may tax the
activity, whether the AI agent can legally be a taxpayer, what
Tax Base applies, or whether any tax is due.

It provides the governed Event Reality upon which those
downstream questions can legitimately be asked.

Canonical Boundary

TREDS governs whether a fully structured TREGA™ Event is
sufficiently complete, coherent, stable, materially supported,
and reconstructable to become a formal Tax-Relevant Event.

It owns Determination Readiness, Determination Conditions,
Determination Decision, Determination qualification,
Determination uncertainty, Determination conflict, Determination
reasoning, Determination reconsideration, correction, and
supersession.

It does not recreate upstream Event governance and does not
determine jurisdiction-specific tax consequences.

TREDS begins when materially necessary upstream Event governance
and the Governed Event Relationship Structure are available.

TREDS ends when a sufficiently governed Determination state has
been established for downstream Event Record Governance under
TREGS.

TREDS establishes the methodological threshold between an event
that has been governed and an event that taxation infrastructure
can legitimately begin to evaluate.

It does not decide the tax. It ensures that tax systems receive a
governed representation of what happened before they decide what
that event means under law.

Tax-Relevant Event ≠ Legally Taxable Event.

Event Determination ≠ Taxability Determination.

Jurisdiction-Ready ≠ Jurisdiction-Determined.

TREGA™ governs the event. The jurisdiction governs the tax.
