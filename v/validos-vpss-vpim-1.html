Validation Purpose Identification

Canonical Definition

Validation Purpose Identification Module (VPIM) defines the
governance framework for identifying, articulating, distinguishing,
documenting, and maintaining the specific purpose for which
validation of an established Validation Object is required.

It establishes the conditions necessary to ensure that every
governed validation begins with an explicit validation purpose
capable of directing subsequent scope, depth, applicability,
criteria, methodology, evidence, execution, determination, state,
and reliance governance.

Operational Role

VPIM governs the purpose-identification layer of Validation Purpose
& Scope governance.

Its role is to transform a general request, requirement, concern,
decision need, operational need, regulatory obligation, or other
validation trigger into an explicit and governable
Validation Purpose.

VPIM ensures that validation is not initiated merely because
validation is possible.

It must be possible to determine why validation is necessary and
what governance need it is intended to address.

Module Operational Space

VPIM governs:

  • validation-purpose identification,
  • validation-purpose articulation,
  • validation-purpose differentiation,
  • validation objectives,
  • intended validation use,
  • validation triggers,
  • validation-purpose justification,
  • purpose ambiguity,
  • competing validation purposes,
  • purpose changes,
  • purpose traceability,
  • and validation-purpose continuity.


Module Function

VPIM applies whenever an established Validation Object is proposed
or required for validation.

Its function is to prevent:

  • validation without a defined purpose,
  • vague validation objectives,
  • incompatible purposes being treated as one validation requirement,
  • technical activity being mistaken for validation purpose,
  • validation designed for one purpose being represented as supporting another,
  • undocumented purpose changes,
  • and validation conclusions being detached from the governance need that originally justified validation.


Validation Purpose Identification

A Validation Purpose should establish the governance reason for
which validation is being performed.

A purpose may arise from:

  • operational deployment,
  • reliance requirements,
  • decision support,
  • regulatory obligations,
  • compliance requirements,
  • assurance requirements,
  • risk conditions,
  • system change,
  • model change,
  • object change,
  • incident response,
  • procurement or adoption decisions,
  • certification or qualification requirements,
  • governance review,
  • or another legitimate validation need.


The existence of a trigger does not itself define the
Validation Purpose.

VPIM requires the underlying governance objective to be
made explicit.

Purpose and Objective Distinction

VPIM distinguishes between the broader Validation Purpose and the
specific Validation Objective.

The Validation Purpose explains:

Why is validation required?

The Validation Objective explains:

What must this validation establish in order to satisfy
that purpose?

For example:

Purpose: determine whether an AI system can support a defined
operational deployment.

Objective: establish whether the system satisfies the validation
conditions required for that deployment environment.

This distinction creates a clearer basis for subsequent validation
scope and criteria.

Purpose Specificity

A Validation Purpose must be sufficiently specific to guide
subsequent governance.

Statements such as:

  • "validate the system," "check the model," "confirm quality," or "make sure it works"


may describe an intention to perform validation but do not
necessarily establish a sufficiently governed Validation Purpose.

VPIM requires sufficient specificity to determine what governance
need the validation is expected to satisfy.

Multiple Validation Purposes

A single Validation Object may have multiple legitimate
Validation Purposes.

For example, the same AI system may require validation for:

  • internal deployment,
  • regulatory compliance,
  • safety-critical use,
  • customer reliance,
  • model modification,
  • or transfer into a new operational environment.


VPIM requires materially different purposes to
remain distinguishable.

Where multiple purposes can legitimately share a validation process,
their relationship should be documented.

Where they require materially different scope, depth, criteria,
methodology, or evidence, they should not be collapsed into a single
ambiguous purpose.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Identify the Validation Trigger

Determine what event, requirement, decision, change, obligation, or
governance need initiated validation.

2. Establish the Validation Purpose

Define why validation is required and which governance need it is
intended to address.

3. Establish the Validation Objective

Determine what validation must establish for the identified purpose
to be meaningfully satisfied.

4. Assess Purpose Clarity

Determine whether the purpose is sufficiently explicit to
guide subsequent:

  • scope,
  • depth,
  • applicability,
  • criteria,
  • methodology,
  • evidence requirements,
  • and validation determination.


5. Preserve Purpose Traceability

Maintain:

  • validation trigger,
  • purpose statement,
  • validation objective,
  • intended use,
  • purpose justification,
  • relevant authority or requirement references,
  • purpose changes,
  • timestamps,
  • and sufficient traceability throughout the validation lifecycle.


Purpose Change Governance

The Validation Purpose may change after validation has begun.

A purpose change may be material where it alters:

  • the intended use of validation,
  • the decision validation is expected to support,
  • the required scope,
  • the required depth,
  • validation criteria,
  • methodology,
  • evidence requirements,
  • applicability,
  • or eventual reliance.


Material purpose change must therefore be recognized and governed
rather than silently absorbed into the existing validation process.

Where necessary, subsequent validation activities must be reassessed
against the modified purpose.

Purpose–Object Alignment

The identified Validation Purpose must be applicable to the
established Validation Object.

A valid governance purpose cannot compensate for an incorrectly
identified object.

Similarly, a precisely established object does not justify a
validation process whose purpose remains unclear.

VPIM therefore maintains explicit alignment between:

Validation Object → Validation Purpose → Validation Objective

before detailed validation scope is established.

Use Case 1 — AI Deployment Validation

Scenario

An organization intends to deploy an AI model within a
customer-facing decision-support environment and requests that the
model be "validated."

Application

VPIM identifies the deployment decision as the validation trigger
and establishes the purpose as determining whether the model can
support the defined operational use under governed conditions.

The corresponding objective establishes what the validation must
demonstrate to support that deployment purpose.

Result

Subsequent validation governance is designed around a specific
operational purpose rather than an undefined request to "validate
the AI."

Use Case 2 — Regulatory Validation Requirement

Scenario

A regulated organization receives a requirement to demonstrate that
a critical analytical process has undergone validation.

Application

VPIM distinguishes the regulatory trigger from the Validation
Purpose and identifies the governance objective that the validation
must satisfy.

Result

The validation process can subsequently establish a scope, depth,
criteria, and evidence structure directly connected to the actual
regulatory validation need rather than merely documenting that
validation activity occurred.

Canonical Closing Statement

Validation Purpose Identification Module establishes the canonical
governance framework for determining why validation is required
before its scope, depth, criteria, methodology, evidence, or
execution are defined. By governing validation triggers, purpose
articulation, objectives, intended use, purpose differentiation,
change, and traceability, VPIM ensures that every governed
validation begins with an explicit reason and remains connected to
the governance need it was created to satisfy.
