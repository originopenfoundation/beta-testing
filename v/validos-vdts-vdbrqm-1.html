Validation Determination Basis & Result Qualification

Canonical Definition

Validation Determination Basis & Result Qualification Module
(VDBRQM) defines the governance framework for assembling, verifying,
bounding, qualifying, and preserving the complete governed
information basis from which Validation Determination may
legitimately proceed and for determining which Validation Execution
Results are methodologically eligible, conditionally eligible,
restricted, superseded, or ineligible to contribute to
that determination.

It establishes the transition from a completed Validation Execution
Record into an identifiable and governed Validation
Determination Basis.

Operational Role

VDBRQM serves as the first internal governance space of the
Validation Determination Standard.

The preceding Validation Execution Standard establishes:

  • what was actually executed,
  • what outputs were generated,
  • whether execution was complete,
  • whether execution was conformant,
  • which deviations occurred,
  • which limitations remain,
  • and whether execution is Determination Ready.


VDBRQM asks: Which parts of that governed execution record may
legitimately participate in Validation Determination, under what
conditions, and within which determination boundaries?

The governed progression is:

Validation Execution Record → Determination Readiness Verification →
Determination Basis Assembly → Result Identification → Result
Qualification → Result Restriction → Determination Basis Freeze →
Qualified Validation Determination Basis

VDBRQM does not determine whether a Validation Criterion
is satisfied.

It establishes the legitimate information basis from which criterion
determination may begin.

  • Validation Determination Basis,

  • Determination Readiness verification,

  • Validation Depth continuity,

  • execution-result identification,

  • determination-basis freeze,
  • and determination-basis traceability.


Module Function

VDBRQM applies after Validation Execution has reached a
Determination Ready or Conditionally Ready condition.

Its function is to prevent:

  • every generated output automatically entering determination,
  • invalid execution results being mixed with legitimate results,
  • superseded results being counted twice,
  • duplicate measurements distorting interpretation,
  • results being detached from their object version,
  • results being interpreted outside their executed scope,
  • conditionally usable results losing their conditions,
  • evidence limitations disappearing,
  • source restrictions disappearing,
  • execution deviations being forgotten,
  • incomparable results being aggregated,
  • partial execution being represented as a complete determination basis,
  • and later criterion determinations relying upon information whose methodological eligibility was never established.


Validation Determination Basis

The Validation Determination Basis is the governed collection of
information from which formal Validation Determination is permitted
to proceed.

It may include:

  • Validation Object reference,
  • object version or state,
  • Validation Purpose,
  • actual Validation Scope,
  • achieved Validation Depth,
  • applicable Validation Criteria,
  • applicable thresholds,
  • acceptance conditions,
  • rejection conditions,
  • approved Validation Method,
  • Evidence Fitness information,
  • Source Reliability information,
  • Validation Execution Record,
  • Execution Completeness status,
  • Execution Conformity status,
  • qualified execution results,
  • residual deviations,
  • unresolved exceptions,
  • execution limitations,
  • evidence limitations,
  • source limitations,
  • uncertainty,
  • and applicable determination restrictions.


Determination Basis Is Not the Entire Execution Record

The complete Validation Execution Record may contain information
that should not participate equally in determination.

For example, it may contain:

  • failed test attempts,
  • invalid measurements,
  • superseded outputs,
  • diagnostic data,
  • repeated tests,
  • outputs generated during control loss,
  • results produced against the wrong configuration,
  • exploratory observations,
  • or records retained only for traceability.


VDBRQM therefore distinguishes:

Validation Execution Record

from: Validation Determination Basis

The first records what occurred.

The second establishes what may legitimately support the conclusion.

Determination Readiness Verification

Before assembling the Determination Basis, VDBRQM verifies the
Determination Readiness status produced by VEXS.

Possible incoming conditions include:

Determination Ready

Determination Conditionally Ready

Determination Readiness Undetermined

Determination Not Ready

or:

Determination Blocked

VDBRQM must preserve this status rather than bypass it.

Determination Ready

Where execution is Determination Ready, the execution record may
proceed into result qualification subject to normal
determination governance.

Determination Conditionally Ready

Where execution is Conditionally Ready, VDBRQM must propagate the
applicable conditions into the Determination Basis.

For example:

  • reduced scope,
  • restricted criterion set,
  • unresolved minor limitation,
  • conditionally usable execution portion,
  • or another explicit boundary.


Determination Not Ready

Where execution is Not Ready, VDBRQM should not construct a
substantive determination basis as though execution were complete.

The process may require:

  • additional execution,
  • corrective execution,
  • missing records,
  • deviation resolution,
  • or another remediation.


Determination Blocked

Where VEXS establishes a Determination Block, VDBRQM preserves
that block.

VALIDOS® establishes:

A determination layer cannot repair an execution layer that lacks
legitimate determination basis.

Validation Object Continuity

The Determination Basis must remain bound to the exact Validation
Object established and executed against.

VDBRQM therefore verifies continuity across:

Validation Object → Execution Object → Determination Object

A material mismatch may block or restrict determination.

Object Version Continuity

Where version matters, the determination basis must identify
the exact:

  • version,
  • configuration,
  • release,
  • state,
  • temporal instance,
  • dataset edition,
  • model checkpoint,
  • or other relevant object representation.


Results from materially different versions should not be
silently merged.

Determination Purpose Continuity

The purpose of determination must remain within the governed
Validation Purpose.

A result generated for one validation purpose should not
automatically be repurposed to support another materially
different determination.

Therefore:

Result Relevance to Purpose Must Be Established

Determination Scope Continuity

VDBRQM establishes the maximum scope within which results may
contribute to determination.

The actual determination scope cannot legitimately exceed the
execution scope supporting it.

Where execution was reduced, the Determination Basis must inherit
that reduction.

Validation Depth Continuity

The achieved Validation Depth is carried into the
Determination Basis.

A result generated under limited validation depth cannot
automatically support a high-assurance determination.

VDBRQM therefore preserves: Achieved Validation Depth → Permissible
Determination Strength

Criteria Set Continuity

The applicable Validation Criteria should remain identifiable before
result qualification begins.

VDBRQM preserves:

  • criterion identity,
  • criterion version,
  • criterion hierarchy,
  • mandatory status,
  • critical status,
  • thresholds,
  • acceptance conditions,
  • rejection conditions,
  • and applicable criterion dependencies.


Validation Execution Result

A Validation Execution Result is an output generated through a
validation activity that may potentially contribute to
Validation Determination.

Results may include:

  • measurements,
  • observations,
  • classifications,
  • comparisons,
  • statistical outputs,
  • simulation outputs,
  • test outcomes,
  • detected behaviors,
  • expert assessments,
  • AI-generated analytical outputs,
  • system responses,
  • or other execution-derived information.


Generation does not establish eligibility.

Result Identity

A material Validation Execution Result should be
sufficiently identifiable.

A Result Record may contain:

  • Result Identifier,
  • Execution Instance,
  • Validation Object,
  • object state,
  • method step,
  • applicable criterion,
  • generating activity,
  • evidence used,
  • source used,
  • time,
  • environment,
  • configuration,
  • control state,
  • and applicable deviation relationship.


Result Qualification

Result Qualification determines whether an execution result
possesses sufficient methodological standing to contribute to
Validation Determination.

Qualification asks:

  • Was the result generated by legitimate execution?
  • Is it connected to the correct Validation Object?
  • Was the correct method used?
  • Is it relevant to the applicable criterion?
  • Were required controls active?
  • Are evidence conditions satisfied?
  • Are source conditions satisfied?
  • Is the result within scope?
  • Is the result sufficiently traceable?
  • Is it superseded?
  • Is it duplicated?
  • Is it affected by unresolved deviation?
  • Can it be meaningfully interpreted?


Result Qualification Status

VDBRQM may classify results as:

Determination Eligible

The result may legitimately contribute to Validation Determination
within its governed scope.

Conditionally Determination Eligible

The result may contribute only under explicit conditions,
limitations, restrictions, corroboration requirements, or
interpretive boundaries.

Determination Restricted

The result is legitimate only for a narrower purpose, criterion,
scope, population, environment, or analytical role.

Determination Ineligible

The result should not contribute substantively to the
Validation Determination.

Eligibility Undetermined

Available information is insufficient to establish the result's
methodological eligibility.

Eligibility Is Not Favorability

Result Qualification must remain independent of whether the result
supports the desired outcome.

A favorable result should not receive easier qualification.

An unfavorable result should not receive stricter qualification.

VALIDOS® establishes:

Result Eligibility Must Be Determined by Methodological Fitness, Not
Outcome Preference

Conditional Result Eligibility

A result may be conditionally eligible where:

  • corroboration is required,
  • a source has limited reliability,
  • execution was conditionally conformant,
  • environmental conditions restrict interpretation,
  • a measurement has known uncertainty,
  • scope is limited,
  • or another governed condition applies.


The condition must remain attached to the result
throughout determination.

Result Restriction

A valid result may have a narrower legitimate role than
originally intended.

For example, a result may be usable:

  • descriptively but not conclusively,
  • for one criterion but not another,
  • for one population only,
  • for one environment,
  • as supporting evidence only,
  • or within a defined time period.


VDBRQM makes such restrictions explicit.

Result Exclusion

A result may require exclusion where it is:

  • generated by invalid execution,
  • connected to the wrong object,
  • methodologically corrupted,
  • produced during critical control loss,
  • insufficiently traceable,
  • outside scope,
  • based upon unusable evidence,
  • dependent upon a disqualified source,
  • or otherwise incapable of legitimately supporting determination.


Exclusion must remain recorded.

Excluded Results Must Not Disappear

An excluded result should not simply be deleted from
validation history.

VALIDOS® preserves:

Result Generated → Qualification Assessment → Result Excluded
→ Reason

This prevents selective removal of inconvenient findings.

Result Supersession

A result may be replaced by a later legitimate result.

Examples include:

  • corrected measurement,
  • repeated test,
  • corrected calculation,
  • re-executed activity,
  • or newer governed output.


VDBRQM may classify the earlier result as:

Superseded

rather than deleting it.

Superseded Does Not Mean Erased

Historical traceability should preserve:

Original Result → Reason for Re-Execution or Correction → New Result
→ Supersession Relationship

Duplicate Result Control

Validation processes may unintentionally count the same underlying
observation multiple times.

For example:

  • one event appears in multiple logs,
  • one dataset is represented by several derived metrics,
  • the same source is repeated through multiple channels,
  • or one measurement is copied into several reports.


VDBRQM prevents duplicated information from artificially increasing
determination strength.

Result Independence

Where multiple results are treated as independent support, their
actual independence should be considered.

Ten outputs derived from the same underlying source are not
necessarily equivalent to ten independent observations.

This is particularly important for corroboration and confidence.

Result Comparability

Results may be methodologically valid individually but not
directly comparable.

Differences may include:

  • method,
  • units,
  • population,
  • environment,
  • configuration,
  • object state,
  • time,
  • sampling,
  • or measurement basis.


VDBRQM determines whether comparison or aggregation is legitimate.

Comparable Results

Results may be considered sufficiently comparable where material
differences do not undermine the intended analytical relationship.

Conditionally Comparable Results

Results may be compared only after:

  • normalization,
  • scope restriction,
  • adjustment,
  • explicit caveat,
  • or another governed treatment.


Non-Comparable Results

Results that cannot legitimately support direct comparison should
remain separate.

VALIDOS® establishes:

Available Together ≠ Methodologically Comparable

Result Provenance

Every material result should remain traceable to its origin within
validation execution.

Result provenance may include:

Execution Instance → Activity → Method Step → Evidence → Source →
Object State → Result

This allows later determination to understand how the result came
into existence.

Evidence Condition Propagation

Evidence conditions established earlier in VALIDOS® must remain
connected to results derived from that evidence.

If evidence was:

Conditionally Fit

then a dependent result may inherit relevant limitations.

VDBRQM prevents evidence restrictions from disappearing during
determination preparation.

Source Condition Propagation

Source Reliability conditions also propagate where
materially relevant.

For example:

If a source required corroboration, a result depending upon that
source may remain conditionally eligible until corroboration
is established.

Execution Limitation Propagation

Execution limitations identified by VEXS must remain visible in the
Determination Basis.

These may include:

  • reduced scope,
  • incomplete activity,
  • partial conformity,
  • environmental limitation,
  • configuration limitation,
  • traceability limitation,
  • or another execution condition.


Residual Deviation Propagation

A deviation may have been closed operationally while leaving
residual methodological effects.

VDBRQM attaches those residual effects to affected results.

This prevents deviation closure from being mistaken for restoration
of unrestricted result eligibility.

Uncertainty Propagation

Known uncertainty associated with:

  • measurement,
  • method,
  • evidence,
  • source,
  • execution,
  • sampling,
  • or object behavior


should remain connected to affected results.

Uncertainty should not first appear only at the final
determination stage.

Result Conflict Awareness

VDBRQM may identify preliminary conflicts between qualified results.

At this stage, it does not resolve the conflict.

It records that:

Result A and Result B may support materially
different interpretations.

Formal conflict governance occurs later within VDTS.

Result Completeness

Result Completeness asks whether the execution record contains the
expected material results necessary for criterion determination.

A result set may be:

Complete Conditionally Complete

Partially Complete

Incomplete

or:

Completeness Undetermined

This is distinct from Execution Completeness.

Execution may have occurred while one expected result was not
successfully generated.

Missing Result

A Missing Result exists where an expected material output
is unavailable.

A missing result may arise from:

  • execution failure,
  • recording failure,
  • corrupted output,
  • unavailable measurement,
  • system failure,
  • or another cause.


VALIDOS® establishes:

Missing Result ≠ Negative Result

Determination Basis Completeness

The complete Determination Basis should contain sufficient
legitimate information to support criterion-level determination.

Possible conditions include:

Basis Complete

Basis Conditionally Complete

Basis Partially Complete

Basis Incomplete

or:

Basis Completeness Undetermined

Determination Basis Consistency

A complete Determination Basis may still contain
incompatible information.

VDBRQM therefore assesses consistency across:

  • object identity,
  • versions,
  • scope,
  • criteria,
  • methods,
  • results,
  • evidence,
  • sources,
  • execution conditions,
  • and limitations.


Determination Basis Conflict

A Determination Basis Conflict exists where two or more components
of the basis impose materially incompatible interpretations or
conditions before criterion determination begins.

Examples include:

  • two valid results generated against materially different object states,
  • incompatible method outputs,
  • contradictory evidence conditions,
  • incompatible source restrictions,
  • or conflicting execution scopes.


Such conflicts must remain explicit.

Basis Freeze

Before criterion-level determination begins, the material
Determination Basis should be version-fixed.

A Determination Basis Freeze establishes which:

  • results,
  • criteria,
  • evidence,
  • sources,
  • execution records,
  • limitations,
  • deviations,
  • and uncertainty conditions


were available to the determination process.

Why Determination Basis Freeze Matters

Without a Basis Freeze, information may be added, removed, or
replaced while determination is being performed without preserving
which information actually supported the conclusion.

VALIDOS® therefore establishes:

A Validation Determination must be traceable to the information
basis from which it was actually derived.

Determination Basis Versioning

Material changes should create distinguishable basis versions.

For example:

Determination Basis 1.0

Determination Basis 1.1

Determination Basis 2.0

The implementation may choose its own versioning mechanism.

The governance requirement is preservation of material change.

Basis Change

After freeze, material changes may include:

  • newly discovered result,
  • excluded result,
  • corrected result,
  • evidence reassessment,
  • source reassessment,
  • execution reassessment,
  • newly discovered deviation,
  • uncertainty revision,
  • or scope correction.


Such changes may require determination restart or reassessment.

Qualification Reassessment

A result's qualification may change when new information emerges.

Possible transitions include:

Eligible → Conditionally Eligible

Eligible → Ineligible

Conditionally Eligible → Eligible

Undetermined → Eligible

or another governed transition.

Earlier qualification should remain historically visible.

Determination Basis Integrity Awareness

VDBRQM depends upon the integrity of the records it consumes.

It does not replace INTEGROS® or specialized
evidence-integrity governance.

Where record integrity is materially uncertain, that condition
becomes part of the Determination Basis.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Verify Determination Readiness

Confirm whether execution is permitted to enter determination.

2. Establish Validation Object Continuity

Bind determination to the correct Validation Object, version,
configuration, and state.

3. Establish Determination Boundaries

Preserve:

  • Validation Purpose,
  • actual Validation Scope,
  • achieved Validation Depth,
  • and applicable Criteria Set.


4. Assemble Execution Results

Identify all material outputs potentially relevant to determination.

5. Qualify Results

Classify each material result according to its
methodological eligibility.

6. Propagate Conditions

Attach relevant:

  • Evidence Fitness conditions,
  • Source Reliability conditions,
  • execution limitations,
  • residual deviations,
  • and uncertainty.


7. Control Duplicates, Supersession, and Comparability

Prevent artificial inflation or invalid aggregation of results.

8. Assess Result-Set Completeness

Determine whether required material results are available.

9. Assess Determination Basis Completeness & Consistency

Identify missing information, contradictions, or Basis Conflicts.

10. Freeze and Version the Determination Basis

Preserve the exact governed information basis entering
criterion determination.

11. Preserve Lifecycle Traceability

Maintain:

  • Determination Basis Identifier,
  • Validation Object reference,
  • object version,
  • Purpose,
  • Scope,
  • Depth,
  • Criteria Set,
  • Execution Instance,
  • results,
  • Result Qualification Status,
  • restrictions,
  • exclusions,
  • supersession relationships,
  • comparability status,
  • Evidence Fitness conditions,
  • Source Reliability conditions,
  • execution limitations,
  • deviations,
  • uncertainty,
  • completeness,
  • conflicts,
  • Basis version,
  • Basis Freeze,
  • changes,
  • reassessments,
  • and sufficient lifecycle continuity.

Use Case 1 — AI Model Validation

Scenario

An AI model produces 1,000 validation outputs.

Thirty were generated during a period when a required execution
control was unavailable, and fifty were later repeated under
restored conditions.

Application

VDBRQM does not treat all 1,050 recorded outputs as equivalent
determination evidence.

It identifies:

  • affected original outputs,
  • corrected repetitions,
  • supersession relationships,
  • control-loss conditions,
  • duplicate relationships,
  • and result eligibility.


Result

Only the methodologically qualified result set enters
criterion determination.

The larger raw output count cannot artificially increase
validation confidence.

Use Case 2 — Industrial Validation

Scenario

Two measurement systems produce results for the same
Validation Criterion.

Both systems are individually valid, but they use different
measurement scales and environmental conditions.

Application

VDBRQM assesses result comparability before the outputs
are aggregated.

Result

The results may be classified as:

Conditionally Comparable

and require normalization or separate interpretation rather than
being directly averaged.

Use Case 3 — Autonomous Validation System

Scenario

An autonomous validation agent generates thousands of test outputs,
including failed attempts, retries, diagnostic outputs, and final
successful executions.

Application

VDBRQM distinguishes:

  • diagnostic outputs,
  • failed attempts,
  • superseded results,
  • final eligible results,
  • duplicate information,
  • and results affected by execution deviations.


Result

Automated scale does not become methodological weight by itself.

The Validation Determination Basis contains only results
legitimately capable of supporting determination.

Architectural Position

Validation Determination Basis & Result Qualification is the first
internal governance space of the Validation Determination
Standard (VDTS).

The transition is:

Validation Execution → Validation Determination Basis

VDBRQM establishes:

Which governed validation results may legitimately
enter determination?

The next VDTS governance stage can then ask:

What does each qualified result set demonstrate relative to each
applicable Validation Criterion?

The VDTS internal progression therefore begins:

Determination Basis & Result Qualification → Criterion Determination
→ Conflict & Uncertainty Governance → Cross-Criterion Integration →
Overall Validation Determination

Foundational Principle

A validation conclusion can be no more legitimate than the results
permitted to participate in its determination.

Canonical Closing Statement

Validation Determination Basis & Result Qualification Module
establishes the governed entry layer of Validation Determination
within VALIDOS® by transforming the complete Validation Execution
Record into an identifiable, bounded, qualified, versioned, and
traceable Validation Determination Basis. Through readiness
verification, object and scope continuity, result qualification,
eligibility, restriction, exclusion, supersession, duplicate
control, comparability, provenance, condition propagation,
uncertainty preservation, completeness assessment, consistency
assessment, conflict identification, basis freezing, and
reassessment, VDBRQM ensures that criterion-level and overall
Validation Determinations can be derived only from results
possessing legitimate methodological standing rather than from the
undifferentiated outputs of validation execution.
