Overall Validation Determination

Canonical Definition

Overall Validation Determination Module (OVDM) defines the
governance framework for transforming the complete governed
Validation Determination Basis, criterion-level determinations,
conflict and uncertainty conditions, cross-criterion integration
structure, scope boundaries, Validation Depth, methodological
limitations, and determination constraints into a formal, bounded,
traceable, and methodologically defensible Overall
Validation Determination.

It establishes the final conclusion stage of the Validation
Determination Standard and determines what the complete governed
validation event legitimately demonstrates about the
Validation Object.

Operational Role

OVDM serves as the fifth and final internal governance space of the
Validation Determination Standard.

The preceding modules establish:

VDBRQM — Which results may legitimately participate
in determination?

VCDM — What does the qualified result set demonstrate about each
Validation Criterion?

VDCUGM — Which conflicts, uncertainties, and Determination
Constraints remain?

VCCIM — How should criterion outcomes be integrated according to
their governance significance and relationships?

OVDM asks:

What Overall Validation Determination does the complete governed
validation record legitimately support?

The governed progression is:

Qualified Determination Basis → Criterion Determinations → Conflict
& Uncertainty Conditions → Cross-Criterion Integration Structure →
Overall Determination Assessment → Determination Classification →
Formal Validation Determination Record

OVDM closes Validation Determination.

It does not assign the persistent Validation State of the
Validation Object.

Module Function

OVDM applies after the cross-criterion determination structure has
been sufficiently established.

Its function is to prevent:

  • criterion outcomes remaining unintegrated while a global validation claim is issued,
  • positive determination being inferred from majority satisfaction alone,
  • critical failures being diluted,
  • Not Determinable criteria being treated as neutral where they are materially significant,
  • unresolved conflict being erased at conclusion,
  • uncertainty disappearing,
  • conditional findings becoming unconditional,
  • partial validation becoming universal validation,
  • executed scope being expanded during reporting,
  • Validation Depth being overstated,
  • determination authority remaining unclear,
  • and a phrase such as “validated” being issued without a reconstructable methodological meaning.


Overall Validation Determination

An Overall Validation Determination is the formal conclusion
supported by the complete governed validation event.

It describes what the validation record legitimately demonstrates
about the Validation Object within:

  • the validated object version,
  • Validation Purpose,
  • actual Validation Scope,
  • achieved Validation Depth,
  • applicable Validation Criteria,
  • approved methodological structure,
  • evidence conditions,
  • source conditions,
  • execution conditions,
  • conflict state,
  • uncertainty state,
  • and applicable limitations.


It is a bounded methodological conclusion.

Determination Is an Event-Level Conclusion

OVDM governs the conclusion produced by a particular governed
validation event.

This is important because the same Validation Object may
later undergo:

  • modification,
  • new validation,
  • revalidation,
  • expanded validation,
  • reduced-scope validation,
  • or validation under different conditions.


The Overall Validation Determination therefore remains linked to the
validation event that produced it.

Determination Classification

OVDM may establish the following canonical Overall Validation
Determination classes:

Positive Validation Determination

The complete governed validation record sufficiently demonstrates
satisfaction of the criteria required for a positive conclusion
within the defined scope, depth, and conditions.

Conditional Validation Determination

The validation record supports a positive conclusion only under
explicit conditions, restrictions, limitations, assumptions, reduced
scope, or other governed boundaries.

Negative Validation Determination

The validation record sufficiently demonstrates non-satisfaction of
one or more criteria whose governance significance prevents a
positive conclusion for the defined determination scope.

Indeterminate Validation Determination

The governed validation record does not provide sufficient
legitimate basis to establish either a positive or negative
overall conclusion.

Partial Validation Determination

Distinct parts of the Validation Object, Validation Scope, criterion
structure, population, environment, or operational domain
legitimately support different determination outcomes.

These classes must remain distinct.

Positive Validation Determination

A Positive Validation Determination requires more than many
favorable results.

OVDM asks whether the complete integration structure permits a
positive outcome.

This may require:

  • all mandatory criteria satisfied,
  • no disqualifying critical failures,
  • required prerequisites satisfied,
  • no exclusionary conditions triggered,
  • required Determination Constraints resolved,
  • uncertainty remaining within acceptable bounds,
  • and sufficient scope and depth for the intended positive conclusion.


Positive Does Not Mean Universal

VALIDOS® establishes:

Positive Validation Determination ≠ Universal Validity

A positive determination may apply only to:

  • a specific version,
  • specific population,
  • specific environment,
  • specific configuration,
  • specific operational state,
  • specific jurisdiction,
  • specific time,
  • or another bounded condition.


The determination must preserve those boundaries.

Conditional Validation Determination

A Conditional Validation Determination is not a weaker wording for a
Positive Determination.

It is a distinct governed conclusion.

The object is positively determined only if the relevant conditions
remain satisfied.

Conditions may include:

  • continued control,
  • specific operating environment,
  • restricted scope,
  • specific configuration,
  • limited population,
  • monitoring requirement,
  • corroboration requirement,
  • specific evidence condition,
  • limited duration,
  • or another methodological restriction.


Conditionality Must Be Operationally Meaningful

A condition should be sufficiently precise to allow later governance
to determine whether it remains satisfied.

Statements such as:

“Valid if used appropriately”

are too vague unless “appropriately” is governed elsewhere.

Conditions should be operationally referenceable where possible.

Negative Validation Determination

A Negative Validation Determination exists where the governed record
sufficiently demonstrates that the requirements for a positive
determination are not met because of actual non-satisfaction of one
or more materially significant criteria.

Negative determination must remain grounded in positive evidence
of non-satisfaction.

It should not be produced merely because:

  • validation was incomplete,
  • evidence was missing,
  • uncertainty was high,
  • or execution failed.


Those conditions may produce indeterminacy instead.

Indeterminate Validation Determination

An Indeterminate Validation Determination is appropriate where the
governed validation process cannot legitimately establish either
positive or negative determination.

Possible causes include:

  • mandatory criterion Not Determinable,
  • unresolved critical conflict,
  • determination-blocking uncertainty,
  • insufficient determinant results,
  • incompatible scopes,
  • unresolved cross-criterion integration conflict,
  • or another methodological limitation.


VALIDOS® establishes:

Indeterminate is an outcome, not an omission.

Partial Validation Determination

Partial Determination preserves structural truth where one global
outcome would be misleading.

For example:

  • Subsystem A: Positive
  • Subsystem B: Conditional
  • Subsystem C: Negative
  • Subsystem D: Indeterminate


or:

  • Adult Population: Positive
  • Pediatric Population: Indeterminate


or:

  • Normal Operation: Positive
  • Degraded Operation: Negative


OVDM may represent these outcomes within one structured
determination rather than forcing one universal label.

Hierarchical Validation Determination

For complex Validation Objects, OVDM may produce a hierarchical
conclusion structure.

For example:

Criterion Determinations

↓

Capability Determinations

↓

Subsystem Determinations

↓

Domain Determinations

↓

Overall Validation Determination

Each higher-level conclusion should remain traceable to the
lower-level determinations supporting it.

Overall Determination Scope

OVDM establishes the exact scope to which the Overall Validation
Determination applies.

The determination scope must not exceed:

  • legitimately executed scope,
  • supported criterion scope,
  • applicable evidence scope,
  • reliable source scope,
  • applicable object version,
  • and conditions surviving cross-criterion integration.


Scope Intersection

Where required criteria support different scopes, the permissible
overall positive scope may be constrained to the common
supported region.

Conceptually:

Positive Determination Scope ≤ Common Supported Scope of
Required Criteria

Where no coherent common scope exists, Partial Determination may
be required.

Scope Exclusion

Specific areas may need explicit exclusion.

For example:

Positive Validation Determination excluding high-altitude operation.

Positive Validation Determination excluding pediatric use.

Conditional Validation Determination excluding autonomous operation
without human supervision.

Exclusions should remain part of the determination record.

Determination Depth

The determination should preserve the Validation Depth
actually achieved.

Possible architecture implementations may distinguish:

  • preliminary validation,
  • standard validation,
  • enhanced validation,
  • high-assurance validation,
  • or other governed depth levels.


The determination must not imply a deeper assurance level than the
validation performed.

Determination Strength Is Not Determination Type

A Positive Validation Determination may be supported at different
strengths or depths.

Therefore:

Positive at Preliminary Depth

is not methodologically equivalent to:

Positive at High-Assurance Depth

OVDM preserves this distinction.

Determination Conditions

OVDM collects and consolidates material conditions propagated from:

  • criterion determinations,
  • Evidence Fitness,
  • Source Reliability,
  • Execution Conformity,
  • conflict governance,
  • uncertainty governance,
  • cross-criterion integration,
  • and scope constraints.


Conditions should not disappear merely because an overall conclusion
has been reached.

Determination Restrictions

Restrictions may specify what the determination must not be used
to imply.

Examples include:

  • not applicable outside tested environment,
  • not valid for another system version,
  • not sufficient for regulatory approval,
  • not sufficient for autonomous deployment,
  • not valid after specified modification,
  • or not transferable to another population.


Restrictions help prevent conclusion overreach.

Determination Limitations

Limitations describe known weaknesses or boundaries of the
validation conclusion.

These may include:

  • limited long-term evidence,
  • limited rare-event coverage,
  • incomplete external validation,
  • constrained population representation,
  • limited operational duration,
  • unresolved low-frequency uncertainty,
  • or other known limitations.


A positive determination may coexist with limitations.

Determination Uncertainty

OVDM preserves residual uncertainty remaining after VDCUGM
and VCCIM.

The final determination should therefore be uncertainty-aware.

Where appropriate, uncertainty may be represented as:

  • acceptable,
  • conditionally acceptable,
  • material but bounded,
  • determination-limiting,
  • or determination-blocking.


Determination Conflict Effects

A conflict may remain unresolved without completely
preventing determination.

For example:

The conflict may be localized to one subsystem outside the positive
determination scope.

OVDM therefore considers the actual effect of remaining conflict.

Determination Constraint Compliance

Before issuing the Overall Validation Determination, OVDM verifies
whether applicable Determination Constraints produced earlier have
been respected.

Examples include:

  • required reduced scope,
  • prohibition on unconditional positive determination,
  • additional validation requirement,
  • no aggregation across defined criteria,
  • or mandatory Partial Determination.


Overall Determination Logic

OVDM should preserve the logic connecting:

Criterion Outcomes → Integration Rules → Constraints →
Overall Determination

This logic must remain reconstructable.

A global conclusion should not appear as an unexplained final label.

Determination Rationale

A Validation Determination should include or reference sufficient
rationale to explain:

  • why the conclusion was selected,
  • which criteria controlled it,
  • which conflicts affected it,
  • which uncertainty remained,
  • which conditions apply,
  • and why alternative determination classes were not selected where material.


Determination Authority

Where a formal determination requires identifiable authority, OVDM
may govern:

  • determination issuer,
  • approval authority,
  • delegated authority,
  • decision system,
  • required independence,
  • required competence,
  • authority scope,
  • and authorization conditions.


OVDM does not create legal authority externally.

It governs the authority used within the methodology.

Single Determination Authority

One authorized evaluator or system may issue the
final determination.

Where this occurs, the determination must still remain traceable to
the governed validation record rather than personal preference.

Multi-Party Determination

Some validation environments may require multiple parties.

Possible structures include:

  • validator + reviewer,
  • technical evaluator + governance authority,
  • automated determination + human approval,
  • independent assessor + regulator,
  • or other governed arrangements.


Determination Disagreement

Where authorized determination actors disagree, OVDM does not
automatically use majority vote unless the governance structure
explicitly defines that mechanism.

Disagreement may require:

  • escalation,
  • additional review,
  • preserved dissent,
  • or Indeterminate Determination.


Human Overall Determination

Human determination may be appropriate where integration involves
substantial context, uncertainty, or judgment.

The governing requirement is that the human conclusion remains
constrained by:

  • criteria,
  • results,
  • integration rules,
  • conflict conditions,
  • uncertainty,
  • scope,
  • and methodological record.


Automated Overall Determination

Where determination logic is sufficiently explicit, OVDM may
be automated.

Automated rules may implement:

  • mandatory-criteria logic,
  • critical-condition logic,
  • hierarchical integration,
  • exclusion rules,
  • threshold structures,
  • conditionality,
  • and Determination Constraints.


The rule set should remain versioned and auditable.

AI-Assisted Overall Determination

AI may assist with:

  • synthesis,
  • rationale preparation,
  • traceability reconstruction,
  • condition extraction,
  • inconsistency detection,
  • scope checking,
  • or explanation generation.


However:

AI Assistance ≠ Authority to Invent Determination Logic

The final determination must remain grounded in governed rules
and records.

Determination Effective Point

The Overall Validation Determination should have an identifiable
effective point.

This may include:

  • issuance date,
  • approval date,
  • validation completion date,
  • or another governed reference.


This is important because the next Parent Standard must determine
the Validation State of the object over time.

Determination Is Not Necessarily Permanent

A determination reflects a governed conclusion at a specific point
based upon a specific Validation Object and validation basis.

It may later change because of:

  • new evidence,
  • source reassessment,
  • execution correction,
  • object modification,
  • method error,
  • discovered conflict,
  • or revalidation.

Determination Statement

A formal determination statement should be capable of communicating
more than:

“Validated.”

A governed statement should conceptually communicate:

What object

what version

for what purpose

within what scope

at what depth

against what criteria

with what outcome

subject to what conditions

with what limitations

at what effective point

This creates validation meaning.

Determination Transparency

A qualified reviewer should be able to understand the methodological
basis of the Overall Validation Determination.

Transparency may include:

  • criteria outcome map,
  • integration logic,
  • conditions,
  • limitations,
  • conflict status,
  • uncertainty status,
  • and determination rationale.


Transparency does not require publication of confidential technical
information beyond applicable governance obligations.

Determination Versioning

Materially revised determinations should remain distinguishable.

Possible representations may include:

Determination 1.0

Determination 1.1

Determination 2.0 The exact implementation can vary.

Historical determination states must remain available.

Determination Correction

A correction applies where the original determination contained an
error but the underlying validation event can still support a
corrected determination.

The record should preserve:

Original Determination → Error → Correction →
Corrected Determination

Determination Reassessment

Reassessment may occur when new information materially affects the
original conclusion.

Possible triggers include:

  • corrected result,
  • newly identified execution deviation,
  • evidence reassessment,
  • Source Reliability reassessment,
  • conflict resolution,
  • new uncertainty,
  • or methodological correction.


Determination Change

Possible determination transitions may include:

Positive → Conditional

Positive → Negative

Positive → Indeterminate

Conditional → Positive

Conditional → Negative

Negative → Positive

Indeterminate → Positive

or other governed transitions.

Change does not erase historical conclusions.

Determination Suspension

A determination may be suspended where serious new information
creates material doubt before reassessment is complete.

Suspension means the determination should not continue to be
represented as an unrestricted current conclusion.

Determination Withdrawal

A determination may be withdrawn where its methodological foundation
is materially destroyed.

Possible causes include:

  • wrong Validation Object,
  • invalid critical execution,
  • compromised decisive evidence,
  • fundamental source unreliability,
  • incorrect criterion architecture,
  • or determination fraud.


Determination Supersession

A later governed validation event may produce a new determination
superseding an earlier one.

The relationship should be explicit:

Determination A → Superseded by Determination B

Supersession does not delete Determination A.

Determination Continuity

OVDM preserves determination history across:

  • object versions,
  • revalidation,
  • corrections,
  • updates,
  • scope changes,
  • and lifecycle transitions.


This enables the next VALIDOS® Parent Standard to reason about the
current Validation State rather than relying on one
isolated conclusion.

Determination vs Validation State

This boundary is fundamental.

OVDM answers:

What did this validation event demonstrate?

The next Parent Standard must answer:

Given the relevant determinations and current lifecycle conditions,
what Validation State does the object now hold?

Therefore:

Overall Validation Determination ≠ Validation State

Determination vs Reliance

OVDM also does not authorize reliance.

A Positive Validation Determination may exist while:

  • deployment is prohibited,
  • usage is restricted,
  • regulatory approval is absent,
  • revalidation is due,
  • or the determination is unsuitable for a specific relying party.


Therefore:

Positive Validation Determination ≠ Reliance Authorization

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Verify Cross-Criterion Integration

Confirm that the criterion integration structure is sufficiently
complete and consistent.

2. Identify Controlling Criterion Conditions

Identify:

  • mandatory criteria,
  • critical criteria,
  • exclusion conditions,
  • unresolved Not Determinable criteria,
  • and other controlling factors.


3. Verify Determination Constraints

Ensure all material conflict, uncertainty, and integration
constraints have been respected.

4. Establish Permissible Determination Scope

Define the maximum scope legitimately supported by the
validation record.

5. Establish Determination Depth

Preserve the Validation Depth actually achieved.

6. Consolidate Conditions, Restrictions, and Limitations

Carry forward all material boundaries from the complete VALIDOS®
validation record.

7. Establish Overall Validation Determination Classify the
result as:

  • Positive Validation Determination,
  • Conditional Validation Determination,
  • Negative Validation Determination,
  • Indeterminate Validation Determination,
  • or Partial Validation Determination.


8. Establish Determination Rationale & Authority

Preserve why the conclusion was reached and by whom or what it was
formally issued where applicable.

9. Establish Effective Point

Record when the determination becomes methodologically effective.

10. Create Overall Validation Determination Record

Preserve the complete conclusion basis.

11. Preserve Change & Continuity Governance

Maintain:

  • corrections,
  • reassessments,
  • suspensions,
  • withdrawals,
  • supersession,
  • versions,
  • and historical continuity.

Use Case 1 — Autonomous AI System

Scenario

An autonomous AI system satisfies all mandatory
performance criteria.

One critical safety criterion is Conditionally Satisfied only where
human override remains continuously available.

Application

VCCIM propagates the safety condition into the integrated
criterion structure.

OVDM cannot issue an unrestricted Positive Validation Determination.

Result

The system receives:

Conditional Validation Determination

limited to operation under continuous human override availability.

The condition becomes part of the formal determination rather than
an informal footnote.

Use Case 2 — Healthcare AI

Scenario

A diagnostic AI system is positively determined across the adult
validation population.

Pediatric validation remains Not Determinable because the evidence
basis is insufficient.

Application

OVDM limits the determination scope to the validated
adult population.

Result

The system may receive:

Positive Validation Determination — Adult Population

together with an explicit exclusion of pediatric use from the
positive determination scope.

It is not represented as universally validated.

Use Case 3 — Industrial Autonomous System

Scenario

An industrial system satisfies all normal-operation criteria but
fails one critical recovery criterion during degraded operation.

Application

The cross-criterion integration structure identifies the recovery
criterion as non-compensatory and critical.

Result

OVDM may establish:

Negative Validation Determination for the intended unrestricted
operational scope

while preserving any separately valid positive determination for a
narrower operational mode if that scope can be
methodologically isolated.

Architectural Position

Overall Validation Determination is the fifth and final internal
governance space of the Validation Determination Standard (VDTS).

The complete VDTS progression is:

Validation Determination Basis & Result Qualification → Validation
Criterion Determination → Validation Determination Conflict &
Uncertainty Governance → Validation Cross-Criterion Integration →
Overall Validation Determination

Together, these five governed spaces transform validation execution
results into a formal and bounded validation conclusion.

The progression is:

Execution Outputs

↓

Qualified Determination Basis

↓

Criterion Determinations

↓

Governed Conflict & Uncertainty

↓

Cross-Criterion Integration

↓

Overall Validation Determination

The next VALIDOS® governance question becomes
fundamentally different:

What formal Validation State should the Validation Object now hold
as a result of the applicable determination and current
lifecycle conditions?

Foundational Principle

A Validation Determination is legitimate only when its scope,
strength, conditions, and conclusion do not exceed what the complete
governed validation record is capable of demonstrating.

Canonical Closing Statement

Overall Validation Determination Module completes the formal
conclusion lifecycle of the Validation Determination Standard within
VALIDOS® by transforming qualified results, criterion-level
determinations, conflicts, uncertainty, integration logic, scope,
Validation Depth, conditions, restrictions, limitations, and
Determination Constraints into a formal Overall Validation
Determination. OVDM ensures that validation conclusions remain
explicit, bounded, traceable, versioned, revisable, and
methodologically defensible while preserving the critical
architectural separation between what a validation event
demonstrates, what Validation State an object subsequently holds,
and whether any person, system, or organization may ultimately rely
upon that state.
