About Validation Object Standard

Establishing What Is Being Validated Before Validation Begins

Validation cannot begin with a method.

It cannot begin with evidence.

It cannot begin with a conclusion.

It must begin with the object.

Validation Object Standard (VOBS) is the foundational Parent
Standard of VALIDOS® — Validation Governance Architecture governing
how a subject of validation becomes a formally established,
identifiable, bounded, versioned, relationally situated, and
validation-ready object.

VOBS addresses one foundational question:

What exactly is being validated?

Why Validation Object Standard Exists

Validation conclusions are meaningful only when they can be
connected to the exact object that was examined.

An AI model may change.

A dataset may be updated.

A system may operate under a different configuration.

A claim may be modified.

A process may depend on external components.

A decision may relate to a specific operational state.

Without a governed Validation Object, a statement that something has
been "validated" may therefore be incomplete or misleading.

VOBS establishes the methodological foundation required to determine
precisely what a validation result applies to.

The Object Before the Validation

Before validation scope can be established, the object must
be known.

Before validation criteria can be defined, the object must
be bounded.

Before a validation method can be selected, the relevant version or
state must be identifiable.

Before evidence can be evaluated, it must be possible to determine
which object that evidence supports.

Before a validation determination can be issued, the exact object to
which the determination applies must remain traceable.

VOBS establishes this foundation.

The governing sequence begins:

Candidate Subject → Object Identification → Object Boundary → Object
Version → Object Relationships → Object Fitness → Validation
Readiness → Governed Validation Object

Only then should the object proceed into the wider VALIDOS®
validation governance lifecycle.

What Is a Validation Object?

A Validation Object is the formally established subject to which
validation governance applies.

It may be:

  • a claim,
  • information,
  • an AI-generated output,
  • a decision,
  • a prediction,
  • a simulation,
  • a dataset,
  • an algorithm,
  • a model,
  • an AI system,
  • an autonomous system,
  • a process,
  • an operational state,
  • a methodology,
  • a policy,
  • a document,
  • a control,
  • a digital asset,
  • a digital identity,
  • a physical object,
  • an infrastructure component,
  • or another subject requiring formal validation.


VOBS does not require all Validation Objects to be validated in the
same way.

It requires every Validation Object to be sufficiently established
before its validity is assessed.

Identity, Boundary, Version, Relationship, and Fitness

VOBS approaches the Validation Object through five fundamental
governance dimensions.

Identification establishes which object is being validated.

Boundary establishes what belongs to the object and what remains
outside it.

Version establishes which state, configuration, release, or temporal
representation is subject to validation.

Relationship establishes the dependencies and connections capable of
materially affecting the object.

Fitness determines whether the object is sufficiently defined and
examinable to proceed into governed validation.

Together, these dimensions transform an undefined candidate subject
into a validation-ready governed object.

Validation Readiness Is Not Validity

One of the most important distinctions established by VOBS is the
separation between validation readiness and validity.

An object that cannot yet be reliably identified, bounded,
versioned, or examined should not automatically be classified
as invalid.

Instead, it may be:

Validation Not Ready

This distinction prevents methodological uncertainty about the
object from being incorrectly transformed into a conclusion about
the object's validity.

VOBS governs readiness.

Subsequent VALIDOS® standards govern the process through which
validity is ultimately determined.

Validation Must Follow the Object

A validation result applies to the object that was
actually validated.

If that object materially changes, the original validation cannot
automatically be assumed to remain applicable.

Changes to:

  • identity,
  • boundaries,
  • composition,
  • configuration,
  • version,
  • dependencies,
  • operational state,
  • or material relationships


may affect the applicability of an existing
validation determination.

VOBS therefore creates the persistent object reference needed to
connect validation results to the precise object examined and to
identify when change may require revalidation.

Governance Rather Than Technical Testing

VOBS does not prescribe laboratory procedures, software testing
methods, model benchmarks, statistical tests, verification
technologies, or implementation-specific validation techniques.

Those mechanisms may be used within a validation process.

VOBS governs something more foundational:

the conditions under which the subject of those activities can be
recognized as a properly established Validation Object.

This distinction allows VOBS to remain technology-neutral and
applicable across different domains, systems, methodologies, and
future technologies.

Position Within VALIDOS®

Validation Object Standard is the first Parent Standard of VALIDOS®
— Validation Governance Architecture.

It establishes the foundation for the complete validation
governance lifecycle:

Validation Object → Validation Purpose & Scope → Validation Criteria
→ Validation Method → Validation Evidence Fitness → Validation
Source Reliability → Validation Execution → Validation Determination
→ Validation State → Validation Reliance & Revalidation

VOBS answers the first question.

What exactly are we validating?

The next VALIDOS® governance spaces determine why it is being
validated, against which criteria, by which methodology, using which
evidence and sources, how validation is executed, what determination
follows, which state should be assigned, and whether reliance
remains justified.

Relationship to Other OOF® Architectures

VOBS operates alongside complementary OOF® governance architectures.

OBIDENITY® may establish identity, origin, ownership, provenance,
authority, and continuity.

INTEGROS® may establish relevant integrity conditions and
integrity states.

ORA™ may establish operational reality and
operational-state information.

GOA™ may establish governance authority, scope, boundaries,
and delegation.

AGA™ may establish accountability and responsibility relationships.

RIS™ may provide relevant risk information.

VOBS does not reproduce these governance responsibilities.

It uses relevant governed information where necessary to establish
the precise object upon which validation governance will operate.

Why It Matters

As AI systems, autonomous systems, digital assets, models, datasets,
simulations, and operational environments become increasingly
dynamic, the question "What was actually validated?" becomes
increasingly important.

A validation associated with the wrong version may be misleading.

A validation applied beyond its original boundary may create
false confidence.

A validation detached from material dependencies may overlook
important conditions.

A validation applied to a materially changed object may no longer
be reliable.

VOBS provides the governance foundation needed to prevent
these failures.

Foundational Principle

A validation cannot be more precise than the object to which
it applies.

Canonical Closing Statement

Validation Object Standard establishes the foundational
object-governance layer of VALIDOS® — Validation Governance
Architecture. By ensuring that every subject of validation is
identifiable, bounded, versioned, relationally situated, and
validation-ready before formal validation proceeds, VOBS creates the
stable methodological reference required to connect validation
determinations to the exact objects they govern throughout the
complete validation lifecycle.
