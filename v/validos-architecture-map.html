VALIDOS® serves as the official OOF® validation governance reference
map used to identify, define, structure, navigate, validate, govern,
interpret, monitor, and revalidate governable validation spaces
across AI systems, autonomous systems, digital assets,
organizations, critical infrastructure, scientific and technical
environments, decision systems, operational processes, and future
validation ecosystems.

The purpose of VALIDOS® is not to determine in advance whether an
object, claim, system, process, model, dataset, prediction,
simulation, product, or decision is valid.

The purpose of VALIDOS® is to identify, define, separate, and map
the governable spaces through which Validation Objects are
identified, scoped, evaluated, methodologically examined,
evidentially supported, operationally validated, formally
determined, assigned Validation States, relied upon, monitored, and
revalidated throughout their complete lifecycle.

Core Architectural Principle

Every validation governance space answers one primary
governance question.

No validation governance space duplicates the purpose of another
validation governance space.

Every validation governance space has a defined beginning, a defined
operational purpose, and a defined governance boundary.

Every validation governance space begins where the preceding
validation requirement has been sufficiently established.

No downstream validation conclusion may legitimately exceed the
validation basis established by the upstream governance spaces upon
which it depends.

Together, these validation governance spaces form the official
validation governance reference map of VALIDOS®.

Official Validation Governance Flow

Validation Object → Validation Purpose & Scope → Validation Criteria
→ Validation Method → Validation Evidence Fitness → Validation

1. Validation Object Standard (VOS)

Governed Space: Validation Object

Core Question

What exactly is being validated, how is the Validation Object
bounded and identified, and how can its identity remain stable
throughout the validation lifecycle?

Canonical Definition

Validation Object Standard (VOS) defines the governance conditions
under which an object, claim, model, system, dataset, process,
product, method, prediction, simulation, output, decision, or other
validation target is formally identified, bounded, versioned,
distinguished, and maintained as a stable governed
Validation Object.

Governance Boundary

Begins when something is proposed for validation.

Ends when the Validation Object has been sufficiently identified,
bounded, distinguished from materially different objects, and
documented to support governed Validation Purpose & Scope.

2. Validation Purpose & Scope Standard (VPSS)

Governed Space: Validation Purpose & Scope

Core Question Why is validation being performed, what must it cover,
what remains outside its boundaries, and at what Validation Depth
must the inquiry operate?

Canonical Definition

Validation Purpose & Scope Standard (VPSS) defines the governance
conditions under which Validation Purpose, Validation Scope,
exclusions, applicability boundaries, consequence context, intended
reliance, and Validation Depth are explicitly established before
validation proceeds.

Governance Boundary

Begins when a sufficiently defined Validation Object requires a
governed validation purpose and boundary.

Ends when the purpose, scope, exclusions, applicability, intended
reliance, consequence context, and required Validation Depth have
been sufficiently established to support Validation Criteria.

3. Validation Criteria Standard (VCS)

Governed Space: Validation Criteria

Core Question

Against which explicit, applicable, sufficient, and governed
criteria must the Validation Object be evaluated?

Canonical Definition

Validation Criteria Standard (VCS) defines the governance conditions
under which validation requirements, thresholds, properties,
questions, acceptance conditions, evaluation rules, and criterion
dependencies are established, justified, bounded, and governed for a
defined Validation Object, Purpose, Scope, and Depth.

Governance Boundary

Begins when the Validation Object, Purpose, Scope, and Validation
Depth are sufficiently established.

Ends when the applicable Validation Criteria have been defined,
structured, justified, documented, and made available for governed
Validation Method selection.

4. Validation Method Standard (VMS)

Governed Space: Validation Method

Core Question

Which validation method is appropriate for the Validation Object,
purpose, scope, criteria, consequence, and required
Validation Depth?

Canonical Definition

Validation Method Standard (VMS) defines the governance conditions
under which validation methods are selected, designed, justified,
bounded, combined, adapted, controlled, and documented so that the
chosen method is capable of evaluating the applicable Validation
Criteria within the intended Validation Scope and Depth.

Governance Boundary

Begins when governed Validation Criteria require a method capable of
evaluating them.

Ends when an applicable, justified, sufficiently controlled, and
documented Validation Method is available for evidence qualification
and operational execution.

5. Validation Evidence Fitness Standard (VEFS)

Governed Space: Validation Evidence Fitness

Core Question

Is the available evidence sufficiently relevant, complete, current,
representative, usable, and fit to support the intended
validation determination?

Canonical Definition

Validation Evidence Fitness Standard (VEFS) defines the governance
conditions under which validation evidence is assessed for
relevance, sufficiency, quality, completeness, representativeness,
currency, usability, contradiction, limitation, and fitness for the
intended Validation Purpose, Scope, Criteria, Method, and Depth.

Governance Boundary

Begins when a governed Validation Method requires evidence capable
of supporting validation.

Ends when evidence has been sufficiently qualified, classified,
bounded, and documented to support Source Reliability assessment and
governed Validation Execution.

6. Validation Source Reliability Standard (VSRS)

Governed Space: Validation Source Reliability

Core Question

Are the sources producing or supporting validation evidence
sufficiently identifiable, capable, reliable, independent where
necessary, and traceable for the intended validation purpose?

Canonical Definition

Validation Source Reliability Standard (VSRS) defines the governance
conditions under which sources contributing evidence, observations,
measurements, records, expertise, or validation inputs are
identified, assessed, qualified, bounded, monitored, and documented
according to capability, reliability, independence, provenance,
relevance, and traceability.

Governance Boundary

Begins when qualified validation evidence depends upon
identifiable sources.

Ends when material Validation Sources have been sufficiently
assessed and their reliability status, limitations, dependencies,
and traceability are established for governed Validation Execution.

7. Validation Execution Standard (VES)

Governed Space: Validation Execution

Core Question

How must an approved validation method be operationally executed,
controlled, observed, documented, and audited so that the resulting
validation record remains trustworthy?

Canonical Definition Validation Execution Standard (VES) defines the
governance conditions under which approved validation methods are
instantiated, authorized, controlled, observed, executed, monitored,
deviation-managed, completed, documented, and made sufficiently
traceable for formal Validation Determination.

Governance Boundary

Begins when the Validation Object, Criteria, Method, Evidence, and
Source basis are sufficiently established for operational
validation activity.

Ends when the required validation activities have been sufficiently
executed, deviations governed, observations preserved, and the
validation record prepared for formal determination.

8. Validation Determination Standard (VDS)

Governed Space: Validation Determination

Core Question

What conclusion does the complete governed validation record
legitimately support, and how must uncertainty, conflict, negative
findings, conditions, and partial outcomes remain reflected in
that determination?

Canonical Definition

Validation Determination Standard (VDS) defines the governance
conditions under which the complete validation record is synthesized
into a formal Validation Determination that remains bounded by the
actual criteria, evidence, source reliability, method, execution,
uncertainty, conflict, limitations, negative findings, and
applicable scope.

Governance Boundary

Begins when sufficiently complete and governed Validation Execution
results are available for conclusion.

Ends when a formal Validation Determination has been established,
justified, bounded, documented, and made available for Validation
State Governance.

9. Validation State Governance Standard (VSGS)

Governed Space: Validation State Governance

Core Question

What Validation State does the Validation Object legitimately hold
now, where and under which conditions does that state apply, and how
must the state change when validation-relevant reality changes?

Canonical Definition

Validation State Governance Standard (VSGS) defines the governance
conditions under which Validation Determinations are translated into
current Validation States, bounded by applicability, conditions,
restrictions, time, scope, object version, material change, state
transitions, revalidation triggers, and complete
lifecycle continuity.

Governance Boundary

Begins when a formal Validation Determination exists and must be
translated into an operationally governable state.

Ends when the current Validation State, its applicability,
conditions, restrictions, lifecycle status, transition logic,
revalidation triggers, and historical continuity are sufficiently
established for reliance governance.

10. Validation Reliance & Revalidation Standard (VRRS)

Governed Space: Validation Reliance & Revalidation

Core Question

Who or what may legitimately rely upon the current Validation State,
for which purpose and scope, under which conditions and level of
consequence, for how long, and when must that reliance be
reconsidered through revalidation?

Canonical Definition

Validation Reliance & Revalidation Standard (VRRS) defines the
governance conditions under which a current Validation State may
support operational reliance by a defined actor, system,
organization, process, or decision context, and establishes how
reliance eligibility, authorization, conditions, restrictions,
monitoring, dependency, change propagation, Validation Delta,
validation reuse, revalidation scope, revalidation execution,
renewal, suspension, withdrawal, and lifecycle continuity
are governed.

Governance Boundary

Begins when a current Validation State exists and a governed actor
intends to rely upon it, or when a validation-relevant lifecycle
event requires revalidation.

Continues while reliance remains active, conditional, restricted,
monitored, reassessed, renewed, suspended, withdrawn, or returned
through the appropriate VALIDOS® layers for revalidation.

Recursive Validation Lifecycle

VALIDOS® begins as a sequential validation architecture but becomes
recursive once operational reliance begins.

The extended lifecycle is:

Validation Object → Validation Purpose & Scope → Validation Criteria
→ Validation Method → Validation Evidence Fitness → Validation
Source Reliability → Validation Execution → Validation Determination
→ Validation State → Validation Reliance → Monitoring → Material
Change → Revalidation Trigger → Validation Delta → Lifecycle
Re-Entry → Revalidation Execution → New Validation Determination →
New Validation State → Renewed / Restricted / Suspended /
Withdrawn Reliance

A Validation Object may re-enter VALIDOS® repeatedly throughout its
operational lifetime.

Architectural Position

VALIDOS® governs the complete validation governance lifecycle.

The architecture progresses through:

Validation Object → Validation Purpose & Scope → Validation Criteria
→ Validation Method → Validation Evidence Fitness → Validation
Source Reliability → Validation Execution → Validation Determination
→ Validation State → Validation Reliance & Revalidation

Together, these validation governance spaces govern how Validation
Objects are identified, bounded, evaluated, methodologically
examined, evidentially supported, source-qualified, operationally
validated, formally determined, assigned current Validation States,
relied upon, monitored, and returned into revalidation whenever the
reality supporting validation materially changes.

VALIDOS® therefore governs not merely whether validation occurred,
but whether the resulting validation claim remains structurally
defensible across its complete lifecycle.

What VALIDOS® Defines

VALIDOS® defines:

  • where validation governance begins,
  • how Validation Objects are formally identified and bounded,
  • how Validation Purpose and Scope are established,
  • how Validation Depth is determined,
  • which criteria govern validation,
  • how Validation Methods are selected and controlled,
  • how evidence fitness is assessed,
  • how Validation Source Reliability is governed,
  • how validation is operationally executed,
  • how deviations and incomplete execution are preserved,
  • how Validation Determinations are produced,
  • how negative, conditional, partial, conflicted, and indeterminate outcomes remain visible,
  • how Validation States are assigned and governed,
  • how state applicability and material change are monitored,
  • how state transitions and revalidation triggers operate,
  • how validation may legitimately support reliance,
  • how reliance conditions, restrictions, authorization, dependencies, and monitoring are governed,
  • how Validation Delta is established,
  • how previous validation may be reused,
  • how revalidation scope and depth are determined,
  • how VALIDOS® is re-entered after material change,
  • how new determinations and states are established,
  • and how reliance is renewed, restricted, suspended, withdrawn, or terminated.

VALIDOS® Four-Layer Validation Protocol™

VALIDOS® may be operationalized through the VALIDOS® — Four-Layer
Validation Protocol™ (4LVP™).

The protocol provides a compact operational pathway through the
broader architecture:

Layer 1 — Object & Scope Validation

↓

Gate 1 — Validation Basis Defined

↓ Layer 2 — Evidence & Source Validation

↓

Gate 2 — Validation Basis Supported

↓

Layer 3 — Execution & Determination Validation

↓

Gate 3 — Validation Conclusion Established

↓

Layer 4 — State & Reliance Validation

↓

Gate 4 — Validation Claim Bounded

↓

Protocol Validation Outcome

The four layers do not replace the 10 Parent Standards.

They compress the architecture into an operational
validation protocol.

The protocol's central anti-bypass rule is:

No validation claim may exceed the weakest unresolved validation
layer on which that claim materially depends.

Individual layers may be used independently.

However:

Layers may operate independently. Full Protocol Conformity may not.

Canonical Principle

VALIDOS® does not determine in advance what is valid. VALIDOS®
governs the structural conditions under which a validation claim may
be established, bounded by evidence and method, translated into a
current Validation State, relied upon within defined limits, and
returned to validation whenever the reality supporting that claim
materially changes.

Canonical Closing Statement

VALIDOS® serves as the official validation governance reference map
for AI systems, autonomous systems, digital assets, organizations,
scientific and technical environments, decision systems, operational
processes, critical infrastructure, and future validation
ecosystems. By defining the primary governable spaces of validation,
VALIDOS® provides the architecture used to identify Validation
Objects, establish validation purpose and scope, define criteria,
select methods, qualify evidence and sources, govern execution,
produce determinations, assign Validation States, control reliance,
detect material change, and perform revalidation throughout the
complete operational lifecycle.

Cross-Architecture Interfaces

→ GOA™ — Governance Architecture
Direction: Bidirectional governance interface
Governed boundary: GOA governs governance architecture and constitutional conditions; VALIDOS governs the lifecycle through which Validation Objects acquire, maintain, change, or lose governed validation status.

→ ORA™ — Operational Reality Architecture
Direction: Operational Reality to validation governance, with governed feedback
Governed boundary: ORA governs what is occurring in Operational Reality; VALIDOS governs whether a defined Validation Object can support a validation determination under governed conditions.

→ OBIDENITY® — Origin-Bound Identity Governance Architecture
Direction: Identity and provenance into validation governance
Governed boundary: OBIDENITY governs origin, identity, ownership, authority, permission, provenance, transfer, and continuity; VALIDOS governs their validation-specific use.

→ INTEGROS® — Integrity Governance Architecture
Direction: Integrity conditions into validation determination and reliance
Governed boundary: INTEGROS governs integrity across objects, information, states, processes, and records; VALIDOS governs whether the complete validation basis supports determination, state, and bounded reliance.

→ ADIT® — Continuous Audit & Evidence Governance Architecture
Direction: Evidence governance into the validation lifecycle
Governed boundary: ADIT governs evidential observation, preservation, verification, traceability, and auditability; VALIDOS governs evidence fitness and use within validation.

→ AGA™ — Accountability Governance Architecture
Direction: Accountability governance around validation activity
Governed boundary: AGA governs authority, responsibility, accountability, attribution, escalation, and decision relationships; VALIDOS governs validation methodology, execution, determination, state, reliance, and revalidation.
