------------------------------ Understanding the Tax-Relevant
Event Boundary Standard

Real-world and digital activity rarely arrives as perfectly
separated events.

A commercial transaction may contain multiple operational steps.
A digital service may involve several systems, settlements,
infrastructure operations, and machine-generated actions. An
autonomous process may execute hundreds or thousands of technical
operations while contributing to only one or several meaningful
governed events.

Before such activity can progress through tax-relevant event
governance, the system must establish where one governed event
ends, what belongs within it, what remains outside it, and where
separation from surrounding activity is required.

The Tax-Relevant Event Boundary Standard (TREBS) establishes the
governance layer for making that distinction.

TREBS does not determine whether the event is taxable.

It determines the legitimate structural extent of the governed
event that may later be evaluated by taxation systems.
------------------------------ What Is the Tax-Relevant Event
Boundary Standard?

TREBS is the fourth Parent Standard within TREGA™.

It receives a sufficiently formed event from the Tax-Relevant
Event Formation Standard (TREFS) and governs the establishment of
its structural boundary.

Its purpose is to prevent operational systems, transaction
identifiers, database structures, platform conventions, machine
processes, or technical workflows from automatically defining
what constitutes the governed extent of an event.

TREBS therefore addresses a fundamental question:

What actually belongs to this governed event, and what does not?

This distinction becomes increasingly important when economic
activity spans multiple organizations, platforms, systems,
jurisdictions, digital environments, autonomous agents, or
machine operations. ------------------------------

Canonical Definition

The Tax-Relevant Event Boundary Standard governs how a
sufficiently formed real-world, economic, operational,
transactional, digital, autonomous, machine-generated, or
value-affecting event is bounded as a distinct governed event by
establishing its legitimate structural extent, including what
belongs within the event, what remains outside it, and where
separation from adjacent or overlapping activity is required.

TREBS preserves the distinction between Event Formation, Event
Boundary, Event Timing, Event Location, Event Participation,
Event Relationships, Event Determination, and
jurisdiction-specific taxation.

Event Formation ≠ Event Boundary.

A structurally formed event does not automatically possess a
legitimate final boundary. ------------------------------ Why
TREBS Exists

Operational reality does not naturally divide itself according to
database rows, invoice numbers, transaction IDs, API calls,
machine instructions, accounting records, or tax categories.

The same underlying activity may appear differently across
different systems.

A marketplace may organize activity around an order.

A payment provider may organize it around settlements.

A logistics system may organize it around shipments.

An ERP system may organize it around invoices.

An autonomous system may organize it around objectives, tasks,
executions, or machine actions.

None of those representations should automatically determine the
governed Event Boundary.

Without explicit Event Boundary governance, the same operational
reality can be artificially aggregated into one oversized event
or fragmented into numerous artificial events.

TREBS provides the methodology for preventing both conditions.

TREBS therefore occupies the governance layer immediately after
an event has been structurally formed and before its governed
temporal position is established. ------------------------------
What TREBS Governs

TREBS governs the structural extent of a formed event.

This includes governance of: Event Boundary identification Event
Boundary scope inclusion within the governed event exclusion from
the governed event separation from adjacent activity separation
of potentially independent event structures overlapping boundary
conditions boundary ambiguity boundary revision prevention of
artificial event aggregation prevention of artificial event
fragmentation traceability of boundary decisions

The result is a Governed Bounded Event capable of progressing
into Event Timing governance. ------------------------------ What
TREBS Does Not Determine

TREBS does not determine: whether the event is legally taxable
which jurisdiction may tax the event when a legal tax point
occurs the applicable tax period who is legally the taxpayer the
applicable tax base tax rate exemptions or deductions reporting
obligations payment obligations tax liability enforcement
consequences

It also does not replace the responsibilities of later TREGA™
Parent Standards.

TREBS establishes the event boundary.

It does not determine the legal tax consequence of that boundary.
------------------------------ Why the Distinction Matters

A boundary is a representation of the governed event.

It is not a tax conclusion.

For example, an activity may span Switzerland, Germany, and
France and still require one coherent governed event
representation.

Conversely, a single commercial contract or platform order may
contain several operationally distinct events that should not
automatically be collapsed into one governed event.

TREBS must therefore establish the event structure independently
of the tax interpretation subsequently applied.

This supports the broader TREGA™ principle:

One Event

↓

One Governed Event Layer

↓

Multiple Jurisdictional Evaluations

The governed event should remain stable even when different
jurisdictions interpret its tax consequences differently.
------------------------------ Event Inclusion and Exclusion

A boundary becomes meaningful only when the system can
distinguish between what belongs to the event and what does not.

TREBS therefore requires a governed basis for inclusion and
exclusion.

An operational action should not become part of an event merely
because it: occurred in the same system shares a transaction
identifier belongs to the same customer account occurred within
the same workflow was executed by the same machine belongs to the
same commercial contract occurred within the same accounting
period

Each relationship must be evaluated according to the governed
structure of the event.

Technical Association ≠ Event Membership.
------------------------------ Event Separation

TREBS also governs when activity must remain structurally
separated.

Two occurrences may be closely connected while still representing
distinct governed events.

Separation may become necessary where independent operational
structures, materially different purposes, distinct formation
structures, or other governed characteristics demonstrate that
treating the activity as one event would distort operational
reality.

TREBS therefore protects against artificial aggregation.

At the same time, it protects against the opposite problem:
artificial fragmentation.

Multiple technical operations do not automatically represent
multiple governed events. ------------------------------ Boundary
Revision

Event boundaries may require reconsideration when materially
relevant information becomes available.

TREBS allows a governed boundary to be revised while preserving
the historical traceability of the previous boundary state.

A revision must not silently rewrite the historical event
representation.

Boundary Revision ≠ Historical Erasure.

Where new information demonstrates that the original boundary was
incomplete, excessive, or structurally incorrect, the boundary
may be reconsidered through governed revision.

Whether the change instead requires formation of a new event
remains subject to the wider TREGA™ lifecycle.
------------------------------ Machine and Autonomous Activity

TREBS is actor-neutral.

Its governance applies whether the underlying activity was
produced by: a human an organization a software platform an API
an AI agent an autonomous system a robot a machine network a
distributed digital system

Machine activity creates a particularly important boundary
problem because one economically meaningful activity may generate
thousands or millions of technical operations.

Those operations must not automatically become thousands or
millions of governed tax-relevant events.

Likewise, a single autonomous objective must not automatically
collapse all resulting activity into one event.

TREBS provides the boundary governance necessary between these
extremes. ------------------------------

------------------------------ Use Case 1 — Global Digital
Marketplace Activity

A customer places one order through a global marketplace.

The order produces: three shipments two payment settlements one
partial refund multiple platform fees logistics operations
payment-provider records merchant records marketplace records
automated accounting entries

Different systems represent the activity differently.

Using the order ID as the Event Boundary could incorrectly
aggregate activity that should remain structurally
distinguishable.

Using every settlement, shipment, or system record as a separate
event could create the opposite problem and fragment one coherent
activity into artificial events.

TREBS establishes the governed boundary independently of those
technical representations.

The resulting event structure can then progress into timing,
location, participation, value association, relationship, and
determination governance.

The system identifier does not decide the Event Boundary.
------------------------------ Use Case 2 — Autonomous Industrial
Production Network

An autonomous manufacturing environment receives a production
objective.

AI-controlled systems coordinate material movement, robotic
welding, quality checks, machine scheduling, energy allocation,
inventory movements, logistics preparation, and automated
settlement.

Thousands of machine actions may occur.

Treating every machine action as an independent tax-relevant
event would create extreme artificial fragmentation.

Treating the entire autonomous production objective as one event
could hide materially distinct operational events.

TREBS establishes governed boundaries between the relevant event
structures without determining their tax consequences.

Those bounded events can subsequently be evaluated by different
jurisdictions or tax systems without requiring the underlying
operational reality to be reconstructed.

Machine topology does not define the governed Event Boundary.
------------------------------

Canonical Closing Statement

The Tax-Relevant Event Boundary Standard establishes the governed
structural perimeter of a formed event before timing, location,
participation, value association, relationships, determination,
and jurisdiction-specific taxation are applied.

It prevents databases, transaction identifiers, commercial
labels, workflows, platform structures, autonomous objectives,
and machine operations from becoming accidental substitutes for
governed Event Boundaries.

TREBS governs where the event begins and ends as a governed
structure — not what tax law ultimately decides that event means.

TREGA™ governs the event. The jurisdiction governs the tax.
