Methodology Scope

Defining the Boundaries of a Methodology

Every methodology operates within clearly defined boundaries.
Without a well-defined scope, it becomes impossible to determine
where a methodology applies, where its applicability ends, or
which class of problems it is intended to govern.

Methodology Scope establishes the operational boundaries that
define the applicability, limitations, and intended use of a
methodology. It provides the context within which methodological
principles, processes, and outcomes remain valid,
consistent, and meaningful.

A methodology should clearly define the problems it addresses, the
environments in which it may be applied, the assumptions upon
which it relies, and the limitations that preserve its structural
integrity. Well-defined boundaries reduce ambiguity, prevent
inappropriate application, and improve comparability across
different methodologies and operational contexts.

As methodologies evolve, their scope may expand, specialize, or
become more refined. Such evolution should always preserve clarity
regarding the methodology's original purpose while ensuring that
new applications remain consistent with its governing principles.

A clearly defined scope enables multiple methodologies to coexist,
complement one another, and remain distinguishable without
unnecessary overlap, supporting greater interoperability across
disciplines and organizations.

Canonical Closing Statement

A methodology without clearly defined boundaries cannot determine
where its authority begins or where its applicability ends. Scope
preserves the identity, integrity, and operational relevance of
every methodology.
