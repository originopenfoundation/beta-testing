Methodology Consistency

Preserving Internal Coherence

Every methodology should remain internally consistent. Without
consistency, principles, definitions, processes, structural
components, and expected outcomes may gradually contradict one
another, reducing reliability, reproducibility, and long-term
confidence in the methodology.

Methodology Consistency establishes the principle that every
element of a methodology should support a common purpose and
operate as part of a coherent methodological system. Definitions
should preserve the same meaning throughout the methodology,
principles should reinforce rather than conflict with one another,
and structural components should remain logically connected across
the methodology's entire lifecycle.

Consistency enables methodologies to grow without becoming
fragmented. As new concepts, capabilities, processes, or
structural components are introduced, they should strengthen the
methodology while remaining compatible with its original purpose,
scope, governing principles, and methodological identity.

A consistent methodology is easier to understand, communicate,
validate, govern, compare, adopt, and continuously improve. It
provides confidence that every part of the methodology contributes
to the same objective and that its evolution preserves coherence
rather than creating unnecessary complexity.

Canonical Closing Statement

Consistency preserves methodological integrity. A methodology
whose principles, structure, and purpose no longer support one
another cannot remain a coherent methodology.
