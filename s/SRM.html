Canonical Definition System

Canonical Definition

Scope Restriction Module (SRM) defines the structural conditions under which governance-scope restrictions
remain legitimate, attributable, traceable, reviewable, governable, enforceable,
auditable, and operationally valid throughout the governance lifecycle.

SRM governs governance scope restriction.

The module establishes the foundational conditions required to determine when governance scope must be reduced,
limited, constrained, narrowed, or removed, and whether governance-scope restrictions
remain reconstructable throughout operational reality.

Governance scope may expand.

Governance scope may also require restriction.

SRM governs that restriction.

Module Operational Role

SRM defines the scope-restriction governance layer of GSS.

Its role is to establish governance-valid scope limitation throughout governance environments.

Module Operational Space

SRM governs:

  • governance scope restriction
  • governance-domain exclusion
  • governance-coverage reduction
  • governance-scope limitation
  • governance-scope narrowing
  • governance-scope auditability
  • governance-scope traceability
  • governance-restriction reconstruction


The module applies wherever governance scope must be reduced or constrained.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • legitimate governance-scope restrictions
  • traceable governance reductions
  • reconstructable restriction history
  • attributable restriction decisions
  • governance-valid restriction records
  • operational governance visibility


Its function is to ensure that governance can reconstruct how governance scope was restricted
throughout operational reality.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Scope Restriction Object

The organization must define which governance environments require governance-scope restriction governance.
This may include:

  • organizations
  • institutions
  • governments
  • regulatory environments
  • corporate governance structures
  • AI governance systems
  • autonomous-agent ecosystems
  • Human-AI governance environments


2. Define Scope Restriction Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which governance-scope restrictions remain valid.
This includes:

  • restriction requirements
  • legitimacy requirements
  • authorization requirements
  • validation requirements
  • traceability requirements
  • governance-valid restriction conditions


3. Define Restriction Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how governance-scope restriction failures are identified.
This may include:

  • unauthorized scope reductions
  • unjustified governance exclusions
  • hidden governance removals
  • governance blind-spot creation
  • unverifiable restriction records
  • governance-invalid restriction activities


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for governance-scope restriction failures.
Governance response may include:

  • restriction review
  • restriction validation
  • governance intervention
  • corrective actions
  • restriction reconstruction
  • legitimacy verification
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • governance-scope restrictions
  • restriction decisions
  • governance reviews
  • intervention procedures
  • validation activities
  • resulting scope states


A governance environment must not remain restriction-valid if materially significant governance-scope restrictions
cannot be reconstructed, reviewed, justified, validated, preserved, or governed.

Use Case 2 — Human-AI Governance Environment

Scenario

Result

The organization gains controlled governance reduction, reduced governance ambiguity, and improved Human-AI governance clarity.

Canonical Closing Statement

Scope Restriction Module (SRM) defines the structural conditions under which governance-scope restrictions
remain legitimate, attributable, traceable, reviewable, governable, enforceable,
auditable, and operationally valid throughout the governance lifecycle.

Governance scope that cannot be legitimately restricted cannot remain governance-valid. Scope Restriction
therefore becomes a foundational condition of trustworthy governance scope governance.
