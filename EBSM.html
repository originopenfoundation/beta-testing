Canonical Definition

The Event Boundary Separation Module (EBSM) governs how
materially distinct event structures that are adjacent,
overlapping, interconnected, technically combined, operationally
continuous, commercially grouped, or otherwise at risk of being
represented as one event are identified and separated into
distinguishable governed Event Boundary structures.

EBSM establishes whether activity surrounding or intersecting a
developing Event Boundary represents: part of the same governed
event, structurally external activity, a materially distinct
event candidate, multiple event candidates requiring separation,
or an unresolved separation condition.

Its specific task is:

to prevent materially distinct events from being incorrectly
collapsed into one Event Boundary while also preventing one
coherent event from being artificially fragmented into multiple
event structures.

EBSM governs event separation at the boundary level.

It does not determine the later semantic, economic, causal,
contractual, tax, or other governed relationships among separated
events. ------------------------------

Operational Role

EBIM identifies where the Event Boundary may exist.

EBSCM establishes what provisionally belongs within its scope.

EBEM determines what should remain outside that scope.

EBSM asks:

Does the resulting boundary structure still contain, overlap
with, or become conflated with materially distinct event
structures that must be separated?

The governing problem is two-sided:

Over-Aggregation Risk

and

Over-Fragmentation Risk

Over-aggregation combines materially distinct events into one
governed event.

Over-fragmentation divides one coherent event into artificial
event units.

Therefore:

Operational Connection ≠ Same Event.

And:

Technical Separation ≠ Separate Event.

EBSM must govern both errors.

------------------------------ Operational Space

EBSM operates where one developing Event Boundary may intersect
with, contain, overlap, obscure, or become confused with other
materially distinct event structures.

Its Operational Space includes: Separation Candidate
identification event distinctness assessment adjacent-event
separation overlapping-event separation nested-event assessment
composite-structure separation distributed-event separation
repeated-event separation process-stage separation
technical-fragment consolidation assessment over-aggregation
detection over-fragmentation detection separation ambiguity
separation conflict separation uncertainty separation
traceability machine-generated event separation autonomous event
separation cross-system event separation

EBSM owns whether materially distinct event structures must be
separated at the Event Boundary level.

It does not own the later relationship governance among those
events. ------------------------------ Operational Scope

EBSM may operate across: commercial transactions financial
operations digital services platform activity logistics
manufacturing supply chains subscription environments
marketplaces banking distributed systems digital assets
autonomous systems AI-agent activity robotic operations
machine-to-machine transactions infrastructure operations hybrid
physical-digital activity future machine economies

The separation logic remains actor-neutral, technology-neutral,
organization-neutral, and jurisdiction-neutral.
------------------------------ Separation Candidate

A Separation Candidate is a structure whose relationship to the
developing Event Boundary creates a legitimate possibility that
materially distinct event structures have been combined or
insufficiently distinguished.

A Separation Candidate may arise from: excluded structures
identified by EBEM, internal scope analysis, overlapping
operational activity, repeated activities, composite processes,
multiple settlements, multiple deliveries, multiple executions,
distributed technical operations, autonomous sub-operations,
competing system representations, new information.

Candidate status does not mean that a separate event exists.

Therefore:

Separation Candidate ≠ Separate Event.
------------------------------ Material Event Distinctness

Separation must depend upon material structural distinctness
rather than superficial technical difference.

Potential distinctness indicators may include: independently
coherent operational structure materially independent execution
independent operational objective separately reconstructable
occurrence independently meaningful completion materially
independent value-affecting consequence distinct participant
configuration distinct operational root structural independence
from the candidate event ability to exist without destroying the
coherence of the other event

No single indicator automatically establishes separation.

EBSM evaluates the structure as a whole.
------------------------------ Same Process ≠ Same Event

A single business or operational process may contain multiple
governed events.

Examples may include:

Order

↓

Fulfilment

↓

Delivery

↓

Settlement

↓

Refund

The entire chain may belong to one commercial process.

That does not mean the chain must be represented as one event.

Therefore:

One Process ≠ One Event by Default.

EBSM prevents process-level aggregation from automatically
becoming Event Boundary logic. ------------------------------
Multiple Process Steps ≠ Multiple Events

The opposite error must also be prevented.

One event may require many technical or operational steps.

Therefore:

Multiple Process Steps ≠ Multiple Events by Default.

A manufacturing event may involve hundreds of machine actions.

A digital-service event may involve thousands of API calls.

A payment may involve multiple technical processing stages.

Technical multiplicity does not independently establish event
multiplicity. ------------------------------ Shared Identifier

Operational systems frequently group activity under: order ID
transaction ID contract ID customer ID project ID batch ID
invoice ID workflow ID

Such identifiers may help locate related activity.

They do not govern Event Boundary separation.

Therefore:

Shared Identifier ≠ Same Event Boundary.

Likewise:

Different Identifier ≠ Separate Event by Default.
------------------------------ Adjacent Events

Two events may occur directly beside one another within the same
operational process.

For example:

a sale may be followed immediately by a separate service.

A delivery may be followed by a return.

A resource allocation may be followed by an independently
initiated reallocation.

Temporal or process adjacency does not collapse those structures.

Therefore:

Event Adjacency ≠ Event Unity.

EBSM determines whether structural separation is required.
------------------------------ Overlapping Events

Event structures may overlap operationally.

They may share: participants infrastructure records assets
technical systems contractual environments operational stages

Shared elements do not automatically mean the events are
identical.

Therefore:

Operational Overlap ≠ Same Event.

EBSM may preserve overlapping references while maintaining
distinct Event Boundaries where materially justified.
------------------------------ Nested Event Structures

Some operational environments may contain event structures within
larger activity structures.

A nested structure may represent: a component of the same event,
a supporting operation, or an independently governable event.

The mere existence of a smaller coherent structure does not
automatically establish a separate event.

Therefore:

Nested Structure ≠ Separate Event by Default.

EBSM must determine whether separation is structurally justified.
------------------------------ Composite Activity

Composite commercial or operational arrangements may combine
several activities.

Examples include: bundled services marketplace transactions
multimodal logistics integrated manufacturing cloud-service
packages autonomous resource orchestration

A composite commercial presentation does not automatically
establish one Event Boundary.

Therefore:

Commercial Bundle ≠ Single Event by Default.

Conversely, a composite operational structure should not be
fragmented merely because several technical systems participated.
------------------------------ Repeated Activity

Repeated activity presents another boundary problem.

Examples include: recurring service delivery repeated machine
execution subscription consumption periodic resource allocation
continuous platform operations repeated digital transactions

Repeated activity may represent: repeated independent events, one
continuing event, or another governed structure requiring further
analysis.

Therefore:

Repeated Activity ≠ Repeated Event by Default.

EBSM governs the structural separation question without
determining later timing or tax-period treatment.
------------------------------ Distributed Systems

One event may be represented across many systems.

Multiple systems may also collectively represent several distinct
events.

Therefore:

Multiple System Representations ≠ Multiple Events.

And:

One Distributed Workflow ≠ One Event by Default.

EBSM evaluates governed operational structure rather than system
topology. ------------------------------ Machine and Autonomous
Systems

Autonomous systems can create extreme event-granularity problems.

A single autonomous objective may generate: planning operations
model inference API requests resource reservations execution
actions payment operations settlement operations corrective
actions

EBSM must prevent two opposite distortions:

every machine action becoming a separate event

and

every action generated under one autonomous objective becoming
one giant event.

Therefore:

Machine Action ≠ Independent Event by Default.

And:

Autonomous Objective ≠ Single Event by Default.

Event separation must remain grounded in material operational
structure. ------------------------------ Over-Aggregation

Over-Aggregation occurs when materially distinct event structures
are incorrectly combined within one Event Boundary.

Possible indicators include: independently coherent substructures
unrelated operational objectives materially distinct executions
independently meaningful outcomes independent operational roots
artificial grouping through system identifiers commercial
packaging masking distinct activity process-level aggregation

Over-aggregation can make downstream governance unable to
distinguish what actually happened.

EBSM must identify and correct this risk.
------------------------------ Over-Fragmentation

Over-Fragmentation occurs when one coherent event structure is
artificially divided into multiple event candidates.

Possible causes include: system boundaries database records API
calls technical processing stages machine actions accounting
entries infrastructure layers organizational departments

Therefore:

Technical Granularity ≠ Event Granularity.

EBSM must preserve coherent events even when their technical
implementation is highly fragmented.

That belongs to:

Therefore:

Temporal Separation Evidence ≠ Event Timing Determination.
------------------------------ Separation Does Not Determine
Event Location

Spatial or digital differences may support separation analysis.

But EBSM does not determine final Event Location.

That belongs to:

TRELS — Tax-Relevant Event Location Standard.

Therefore:

Location Difference ≠ Separate Event by Default.
------------------------------ Separation Does Not Determine Tax
Treatment

A jurisdiction may later: aggregate events, treat them
separately, apply special transaction rules, apply tax-period
rules, apply nexus rules, apply exemptions, treat multiple events
as one taxable supply, treat one operational structure as
multiple taxable consequences.

Those are downstream legal questions.

EBSM must remain independent.

Therefore:

Governed Event Separation ≠ Tax Separation.

And:

Separate Governed Events ≠ Separate Tax Liabilities by Default.
------------------------------ Separation Conflict

Different systems or stakeholders may represent event
multiplicity differently.

For example:

the commerce platform may represent one transaction.

The logistics platform may represent three deliveries.

The payment system may represent two settlements.

The accounting system may represent one invoice.

No system representation automatically governs the Event
Boundary.

Therefore:

System Representation ≠ Event Reality by Default.

Conflicting representations must remain explicit until a governed
separation determination can be supported.
------------------------------ Separation Uncertainty

Where material distinctness cannot yet be established, EBSM must
preserve uncertainty.

It must not force separation merely because downstream systems
require a discrete record.

Therefore:

Data Model Requirement ≠ Event Separation Authority.

Likewise, it must not suppress legitimate separation because one
source system cannot represent multiple events.
------------------------------ Separation Traceability

Every material separation determination should preserve: original
boundary structure Separation Candidates separation basis
retained common elements separated elements unresolved overlaps
supporting information qualification conflict uncertainty
resulting boundary structures downstream revision requirements

This allows the separation decision to remain reconstructable.

------------------------------ Governance Logic

EBSM applies the following logic:

Does the current Event Boundary contain or intersect with a
materially distinct event structure?

If no:

→ confirm the single-event separation structure.

If yes:

→ establish distinguishable Event Boundary structures.

If technical fragmentation exists but material event distinctness
does not:

→ preserve the coherent event and reject artificial
fragmentation.

If operational aggregation exists but materially independent
event structures can be established:

→ separate those structures.

If substantial overlap exists:

→ preserve shared references while establishing distinct
boundaries where justified.

If the separation basis conflicts:

→ record Separation Conflict.

If material distinctness remains unresolved:

→ preserve Separation Unresolved.

If separation materially changes the developing Event Boundary:

→ transfer the resulting structure to EBRM for final boundary
revision and confirmation. ------------------------------

Enterprise Application

Modern enterprise systems rarely agree on what constitutes one
event.

A single operational reality may appear as:

1 order in Commerce

3 shipments in Logistics

2 settlements in Payments

1 invoice in ERP

14 technical transactions in Infrastructure

hundreds of API operations in Platform Systems

Without governed separation logic, organizations may either
collapse materially different events into one record or treat
every technical operation as an independent event.

EBSM creates a system-independent event separation layer.

It allows downstream systems to consume event structures grounded
in governed operational reality rather than database
architecture, application boundaries, commercial packaging, or
tax-specific assumptions. ------------------------------ Use Case
1 — One Marketplace Order Produces Multiple Operationally
Distinct Outcomes

A marketplace receives one customer order containing goods from
three independent sellers.

The platform assigns: one customer order ID, three seller
fulfilments, three shipments, two payment settlements, one later
refund.

The commerce system represents the activity as one order.

The logistics system represents three delivery structures.

The payment system represents multiple financial operations.

The existence of one order ID cannot decide whether one or
several governed events exist.

EBSM evaluates the operational structures independently of those
system representations.

Where materially distinct event structures are established, it
separates their Event Boundaries while preserving the common
order reference.

The result avoids both:

one oversized event created by the commercial order ID

and

dozens of artificial events created by technical system records.

The downstream tax system can subsequently evaluate the separated
governed events under applicable law without TREGA™ deciding
their tax treatment. ------------------------------ Use Case 2 —
Autonomous Cloud Agent Executes One Objective Through Multiple
Independent Operations

An autonomous AI agent receives a high-level objective:

Acquire and deploy computational resources required to complete a
complex workload.

The agent independently: purchases compute capacity, reserves
storage, acquires specialized model access, initiates network
resources, executes workloads across multiple providers, releases
unused capacity, performs settlement operations.

Thousands of API calls occur.

One interpretation would treat the entire autonomous objective as
one event.

Another would treat every API operation as a separate event.

Both may distort operational reality.

EBSM evaluates whether materially independent event structures
exist within the autonomous activity.

It may distinguish independently coherent resource acquisitions
and executions while preserving technical sub-operations as
components or supporting activity rather than artificial
independent events.

Autonomous complexity is therefore converted into governable
event structure without allowing machine granularity to dictate
event granularity. ------------------------------ Canonical
Boundary

EBSM governs the identification, assessment, differentiation,
separation, consolidation protection, overlap handling,
over-aggregation control, over-fragmentation control, conflict,
uncertainty, and traceability of materially distinct event
structures at the Event Boundary level. It does not identify the
original Candidate Event Boundary, establish the initial Event
Boundary Scope, perform Boundary Exclusion, determine semantic or
economic relationships among separated events, establish final
Event Timing, determine Event Location, govern Event
Participation, determine Event-to-Value Association, determine
legal taxability, identify the taxpayer, determine Tax
Jurisdiction, establish legal tax point, calculate tax, or
determine jurisdiction-specific tax consequences.

Canonical Closing Statement

The Event Boundary Separation Module prevents operational reality
from being distorted by either excessive aggregation or excessive
fragmentation.

It separates materially distinct event structures where justified
while preserving a coherent event where differences arise only
from technical systems, records, infrastructure, or
implementation granularity.

EBSM does not determine what separated events legally mean or how
they are taxed.

EBSM determines when operationally connected activity must remain
structurally distinguishable as separate governed events —
without allowing systems, identifiers, commercial packaging, or
tax rules to define event reality.
