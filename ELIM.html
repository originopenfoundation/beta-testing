Canonical Definition

The Event Location Identification Module (ELIM) governs how
spatial information potentially associated with a Governed
Temporally Situated Event is discovered, identified,
contextualized, differentiated, correlated, and qualified before
any specific physical, digital, distributed, or composite Event
Location is established.

ELIM creates the governed spatial-input layer required for
subsequent Event Location governance.

Its canonical task is:

to establish a coherent and traceable Governed Event Location
Signal Set representing the materially relevant spatial signals
associated with the governed event without prematurely
determining Physical Event Location, Digital Event Location,
Distributed Event Location, Tax Jurisdiction, tax nexus, place of
supply, source jurisdiction, or any other jurisdiction-specific
tax consequence.

ELIM therefore answers:

What location information legitimately belongs to the event?

It does not yet answer:

What is the final governed location of the event?

And it never answers:

Which jurisdiction has authority to tax the event?

Operational Role

A modern event may generate numerous location-bearing signals.

These may originate from physical facilities, devices, users,
machines, networks, servers, cloud regions, delivery points,
payment infrastructure, operational systems, autonomous agents,
transaction records, digital platforms, sensors, logistics
systems, or other sources.

The existence of such information does not automatically
establish Event Location.

ELIM determines which spatial signals are materially associated
with the bounded event and creates the governed location evidence
structure that subsequent TRELS modules can evaluate.

Its operational transition is:

Governed Temporally Situated Event

↓

Governed Event Location Signal Set

The module prevents downstream systems from selecting the
easiest, earliest, most commercially convenient, or legally
advantageous location signal and treating it as operational
truth.

ELIM therefore establishes the spatial signal foundation.

It does not perform the downstream determinations owned by ELPM,
ELDM, ELDSM, or ELCM.

Operational Space

ELIM governs the identification layer between an event that has
already been bounded and temporally situated and the subsequent
determination of its operational spatial characteristics.

Its operational space includes: physical location signals;
digital location signals; infrastructure location signals;
device-derived location signals; machine-generated location
signals; platform-recorded location signals; network-derived
location signals; transaction-associated location signals;
service-execution location signals; delivery and fulfillment
location signals; participant-associated spatial signals where
materially relevant to the event; distributed-system location
signals; autonomous-system location signals; spatial metadata;
location-source relationships; location-signal relevance;
location-signal precision; location-signal uncertainty;
location-signal ambiguity; conflicting location signals; spatial
traceability.

The module governs these signals as potential descriptors of
operational reality.

It does not assume that any one signal represents the Event
Location.

Operational Scope

ELIM shall support the identification and governance of spatial
signals associated with physical, digital, hybrid, distributed,
transactional, service-based, machine-generated, autonomous, and
value-affecting events.

The module shall be capable of distinguishing between: where an
actor is located; where an object is located; where
infrastructure is located; where a device is located; where a
system records an event; where computation occurs; where a
service executes; where goods move; where goods are delivered;
where instructions originate; where an autonomous action
executes; where payment processing occurs; where records are
stored; and where the governed event itself may operationally
occur.

These locations may coincide.

They must not be assumed to coincide. 1.
Spatial Signal Discovery

ELIM shall identify available spatial signals materially
associated with the Governed Temporally Situated Event.

A spatial signal may include a coordinate, address, facility
identifier, device position, infrastructure region,
network-derived location, system location attribute, delivery
point, service execution environment, machine location, or
another spatial descriptor.

Signal discovery shall not itself establish Event Location.

Available Location Data ≠ Governed Event Location.

The purpose of discovery is to identify the spatial evidence
environment surrounding the event. 2. Spatial Signal
Identification

Each materially relevant spatial signal shall be identifiable as
a distinct governed object within the location-identification
process.

Where feasible, the signal shall retain information concerning:
source; type; spatial representation; associated event component;
temporal relevance; precision; acquisition method; confidence or
qualification state; transformation history; and traceability
reference.

Spatial signals shall not be collapsed merely because they appear
to reference the same geographic area.

Two signals may describe different operational dimensions of the
same event. 3. Spatial Signal Source Governance

ELIM shall distinguish the source of location information from
the operational meaning of that information.

Potential sources include: human-provided information;
organizational records; sensors; mobile devices; machines;
autonomous systems; platform records; logistics systems; payment
systems; network infrastructure; cloud infrastructure;
geolocation services; digital identity systems; transaction
systems; or external evidence sources.

A source may provide useful spatial information without
possessing authority to define Event Location.

Location Source ≠ Event-Location Authority by Default. 4.
Physical and Digital Signal Differentiation

ELIM shall preserve the distinction between physical and digital
spatial signals.

Physical signals may describe facilities, devices, machines,
persons, goods, infrastructure, delivery destinations, or other
physically situated components.

Digital signals may describe logical infrastructure, service
execution environments, cloud regions, network endpoints,
platform environments, or other digitally represented operational
spaces.

The presence of a digital signal shall not automatically
establish physical location.

The presence of a physical signal shall not automatically
establish the location of a digital operation.

Digital Location Signal ≠ Physical Event Location.

Physical Location Signal ≠ Digital Event Location.

Final governance of these dimensions belongs to subsequent TRELS
modules. 5. Participant-Associated Location Signals

Participants may produce location information materially relevant
to an event.

Examples include customer location, provider location, employee
location, machine operator location, autonomous-agent
infrastructure location, organization address, or recipient
location.

ELIM may identify these signals where they materially describe
the event environment.

It shall not infer that Participant Location is automatically
Event Location.

Participant Location ≠ Event Location by Default.

Participant identity and participation governance remain outside
ELIM. 6. Infrastructure Location Signals

Digital and machine-mediated events may rely upon infrastructure
distributed across multiple places.

ELIM may identify locations associated with: servers; data
centers; cloud regions; edge infrastructure; telecommunications
infrastructure; payment infrastructure; machine controllers;
industrial facilities; storage infrastructure; or computational
environments.

Infrastructure location may be operationally relevant.

It shall not automatically define the location of the event.

Infrastructure Location ≠ Event Location by Default. 7. Device
and Machine Location Signals

Devices, robots, industrial machines, vehicles, sensors,
autonomous systems, and connected equipment may generate precise
spatial information.

ELIM shall distinguish the location of the device or machine from
the spatial meaning of the event.

A machine may participate in an event without its physical
location being sufficient to represent the complete event.

Similarly, a mobile device may generate geolocation data without
proving that the relevant economic or operational event occurred
at that exact position.

Device Location ≠ Event Location by Default.

Machine Location ≠ Event Location by Default. 8. Network-Derived
Location Signals

IP addresses, network endpoints, routing information,
telecommunications records, and similar technical signals may
provide spatial indicators.

ELIM may identify these signals where materially relevant.

Network-derived location shall be qualified according to its
evidential characteristics and operational meaning.

IP Location ≠ Event Location by Default.

Technical geolocation may contribute to Event Location governance
without becoming operational truth merely because it is
machine-readable. 9. Transaction and Payment Location Signals

Transaction systems may associate events with merchant locations,
terminal locations, bank locations, payment-processor locations,
account addresses, settlement locations, or other spatial
attributes.

ELIM shall distinguish such signals from the location of the
underlying governed event.

Payment Processing Location ≠ Event Location by Default.

Settlement Location ≠ Event Location by Default.

Financial infrastructure may describe part of the event
environment without defining where the event itself occurred. 10.
Delivery and Fulfillment Location Signals

Events involving physical goods may contain origin, dispatch,
transit, warehouse, transfer, delivery, collection, and
destination locations.

ELIM shall identify materially relevant signals without assuming
that any one logistics point constitutes the Event Location.

The meaning of each location depends upon the bounded event under
governance.

Delivery Location ≠ Complete Event Location by Default. 11.
Service Execution Location Signals

A service may involve several operational locations.

The customer, service provider, personnel, platform,
infrastructure, data processing, and actual service execution may
all be spatially separated.

ELIM shall identify the relevant signals while preserving their
distinct operational meanings.

For digital services, a server or cloud region shall not
automatically become the service's governed Event Location.

For human-delivered services, corporate registration address
shall not automatically become the operational location of
service performance. 12. Distributed Location Signal Sets

An event may legitimately contain materially relevant spatial
signals from several locations.

ELIM shall not treat multiplicity as an error requiring
artificial reduction.

Multiple Location Signals ≠ Location Conflict by Default.

Several signals may accurately describe different spatial
dimensions of one governed event.

Determining whether the event is genuinely distributed belongs
downstream within TRELS. 13. Spatial Signal Correlation

ELIM shall establish relevant associations between identified
spatial signals and the bounded event.

Correlation may consider: event component; activity; object;
infrastructure; participant role; machine action; transaction
stage; service stage; temporal position; or other legitimate
event relationships.

Correlation shall preserve distinctions among signals.

Spatial Correlation ≠ Spatial Equivalence.

Two signals associated with the same event need not describe the
same location function. 14. Spatial Relevance Assessment

Not every location appearing in surrounding systems materially
describes the governed event.

ELIM shall support differentiation among: materially relevant
location signals; contextual location signals; supporting
location signals; technically generated but operationally weak
signals; administratively associated signals; redundant signals;
and unrelated spatial information.

The purpose is not to eliminate complexity but to prevent
irrelevant location data from acquiring false governance
significance. 15. Location Precision

Spatial signals may exist at different levels of precision.

Examples include: exact coordinates; facility; building; site;
municipality; region; country; infrastructure zone; cloud region;
network region; or unknown / qualified spatial range.

ELIM shall preserve the precision legitimately supported by the
underlying information.

Recorded Spatial Precision ≠ Proven Spatial Accuracy.

A coordinate with many decimal places is not automatically more
operationally reliable than a qualified facility-level location.
16. Spatial Ambiguity

Where available information supports more than one legitimate
spatial interpretation, ELIM shall preserve that ambiguity.

Systems shall not manufacture false precision solely because
downstream data structures require a single location field.

A governed result may legitimately indicate: approximate
location; bounded location; multiple candidate locations;
distributed spatial signals; conflicting location information; or
unresolved location.

Required Location Field ≠ Permission to Invent Location
Certainty. 17. Conflicting Location Signals

Conflicting signals shall be identifiable and traceable.

Conflict may arise because: systems use different location
sources; devices report inconsistent positions; network-derived
location differs from physical location; participant information
is outdated; infrastructure is distributed; timestamps correspond
to different operational stages; records were transformed; or
systems apply different location conventions.

ELIM shall not resolve such conflict by arbitrary source
preference.

System Preference ≠ Governed Spatial Resolution. 18. Machine and
Autonomous Event Environments

Autonomous systems may generate large volumes of location-bearing
information.

An autonomous economic event may involve: an AI agent operating
through one infrastructure environment; a robot acting in another
location; a cloud service executing elsewhere; a payment system
in another region; and a recipient in another location.

ELIM shall identify the materially relevant spatial signals
without equating infrastructure topology with Event Location.

Machine Location Data Volume ≠ Event Spatial Granularity.

One Machine Location ≠ One Event Location.
19. Location Normalization

Spatial data may require normalization for interoperability.

Normalization may include standardized coordinates, address
structures, facility identifiers, region identifiers, or other
spatial representations.

The original location information shall remain traceable where
material.

Normalized Location ≠ Original Location Evidence.

Normalization shall improve interoperability without rewriting
the spatial evidence history of the event. 20. Location Signal
Correction

Where a spatial signal is later shown to be incorrect,
incomplete, or improperly associated, correction shall remain
traceable.

Correction shall not silently erase the previous state where
historical reconstruction is required.

Location Correction ≠ Historical Erasure.

Correction of a signal also does not automatically change the
Event Boundary or create a new event. 21. Governance Requirements

ELIM implementations shall: identify materially relevant spatial
signals; preserve signal-source traceability; distinguish signal
type from Event Location; distinguish physical, digital,
infrastructure, participant, device, machine, transaction, and
other location dimensions; preserve legitimate spatial
multiplicity; identify ambiguity and conflict; maintain
appropriate spatial precision; avoid false geographic certainty;
preserve original spatial evidence where normalization occurs;
support distributed and autonomous environments; prevent
downstream legal assumptions from redefining operational spatial
information; and produce a governed location signal set suitable
for subsequent TRELS processing.

No individual spatial signal shall become authoritative Event
Location solely because it is technically available, commercially
convenient, administratively preferred, or required by a
downstream tax system. 22. Governance Logic

ELIM follows the canonical logic:

The module deliberately stops before final Event Location

A minimum ELIM implementation should support:

Governed Event Reference Reference to the event received from
TRETS.

Spatial Signal Identifier Unique reference for each materially
relevant spatial signal.

Spatial Signal Type Physical, digital, infrastructure, device,
machine, participant-associated, network-derived, transactional,
logistics, service-execution, or other legitimate category.

Spatial Signal Source System, device, actor, infrastructure,
record, or evidence source producing the signal.

Spatial Representation The location information itself at the
legitimately supported precision.

Event Association Relationship between the signal and the
governed event.

Operational Function The spatial role represented by the signal.

Temporal Association Relevant relationship between the location
signal and governed Event Timing where necessary.

Precision State Level of spatial precision legitimately
supported.

Relevance State Material, contextual, supporting, weak,
redundant, excluded, or unresolved.

Ambiguity State Known ambiguity associated with the signal.

Conflict State Known conflict with other spatial signals.

Traceability Reference Evidence or governance reference
supporting reconstruction.

Governance State Current state of the signal within ELIM.

Handoff State Readiness for downstream Physical, Digital,
Distributed, and Correlation governance. 24. Governance Outputs

Governed Event Location Signal Set

This output becomes the spatial governance input for ELPM. 25.

27. Use Case 1 — Distributed Digital Marketplace

A customer initiates a digital purchase from a mobile device in
Country A.

The platform operator is established in Country B.

Authentication infrastructure operates in Country C.

The application executes through cloud regions in Countries D and
E.

Payment authorization is processed through Country F.

The customer's account contains an address in Country G.

The platform therefore contains several technically valid
location signals.

A conventional implementation may select the customer's IP
address, billing address, merchant address, or server region and
immediately treat it as transaction location.

ELIM instead identifies each materially relevant spatial signal,
records its source and operational function, preserves its
precision, and establishes its relationship to the governed
event.

It does not decide which location is the final Event Location.

It does not decide which country may tax the transaction.

The output is a Governed Event Location Signal Set that
subsequent TRELS modules can evaluate.

Complex spatial reality must be governed before it is compressed
into a legal jurisdictional conclusion. 28. Use Case 2 —
Autonomous Industrial Event

An AI-controlled production network receives a manufacturing
requirement.

Planning occurs through cloud infrastructure in one country.

A robotic production cell operates in another.

Components arrive from a separate facility.

Machine coordination uses edge infrastructure in another region.

Quality verification occurs through remote computational
infrastructure.

The finished component is transferred to an autonomous logistics
system serving another country.

Thousands of machine, sensor, infrastructure, network, and
logistics records contain location information.

ELIM identifies which spatial signals materially belong to the
governed event and distinguishes machine location, infrastructure
location, computational location, logistics location, and other
relevant spatial dimensions.

It does not allow the physical position of the AI infrastructure,
the robot, or a server to become Event Location automatically.

The location of the machine performing an action is evidence
about the event — not automatically the complete spatial identity
of the event. 29. Canonical Boundary

ELIM owns the identification, contextualization, differentiation,
relevance assessment, correlation, precision qualification,
ambiguity recognition, conflict recognition, normalization
traceability, and governance of spatial signals materially
associated with the Tax-Relevant Event.

ELIM does not determine final Physical Event Location, Digital
Event Location, Distributed Event Location, or final Event
Location Correlation.

It does not redefine Event Context, Event Recognition, Event
Formation, Event Boundary, Event Timing, Event Participation,
Participant Identity, Value Association, Event Relationships,
Event Determination, or Event Record Governance.

It does not determine Tax Jurisdiction, tax nexus, tax residence,
permanent establishment, place of supply, source jurisdiction,
customs jurisdiction, taxpayer status, legal taxability, Tax
Liability, Tax Period, Legal Tax Point, tax rate, tax
calculation, filing obligation, or any other
jurisdiction-specific tax consequence.

Canonical Closing Statement

ELIM establishes which spatial signals legitimately describe the
governed event before any individual address, device, machine,
server, infrastructure region, or participant location is
permitted to define where that event occurred.

ELIM identifies the locations that may describe the event — it
does not yet decide the final Event Location, and it never
decides the Tax Jurisdiction.
