Canonical Definition

The Event Boundary Revision Module (EBRM) governs how an
established or developing Event Boundary is reconsidered,
revised, confirmed, or preserved when new information, corrected
information, structural change, resolved uncertainty, separation
results, conflicting representations, or other materially
relevant boundary evidence affects the legitimacy of the existing
boundary structure.

EBRM establishes the governance conditions under which Event
Boundary revision may occur while preserving traceability between
the previous boundary state and the revised boundary state.

Its specific task is:

to ensure that an Event Boundary can change when materially
justified without allowing revision to become uncontrolled
reconstruction, retrospective convenience, historical erasure, or
downstream tax-driven redefinition of the event.

EBRM is the final Core Module of the Tax-Relevant Event Boundary
Standard.

It converts the boundary structures established through EBIM,
EBSCM, EBEM, and EBSM into a sufficiently governed and
revision-controlled Event Boundary capable of proceeding into
Event Timing governance. ------------------------------

Operational Role

The preceding TREBS modules establish:

EBIM — where a candidate Event Boundary may exist.

EBSCM — what belongs within its provisional scope.

EBEM — what must remain outside that scope.

EBSM — whether materially distinct event structures must be
separated.

EBRM asks:

Does the resulting Event Boundary remain legitimate in light of
the complete governed boundary structure, and how must any
justified change to that boundary be controlled and preserved?

EBRM therefore governs the transition:

Developing Event Boundary

↓

Revision-Controlled Event Boundary

↓

Governed Bounded Event

Its narrow task is:

to govern material revision, confirmation, preservation, and
traceability of the Event Boundary before the event proceeds into
subsequent TREGA™ lifecycle stages.

------------------------------ Boundary Revision Principle

An Event Boundary must not be treated as permanently immutable
merely because it was previously established.

Operational reality may later become clearer.

New information may reveal that: relevant activity was omitted,
external activity was incorrectly included, one event was
incorrectly aggregated, one event was incorrectly fragmented, an
assumed separation was unsupported, an unresolved boundary
condition has become resolvable, a source representation was
incorrect, or a material structural change occurred.

A governed architecture must therefore support revision.

However:

Revisability ≠ Arbitrary Mutability.

Boundary revision requires a governed basis.
------------------------------ Revision Readiness

Before revision begins, EBRM must establish whether sufficient
reason exists to reopen the current Event Boundary.

A boundary may be:

Revision Not Indicated

Revision Candidate

Revision Required

Revision Unresolved

Revision readiness prevents every new technical record or minor
informational difference from reopening an otherwise legitimate
Event Boundary.

Therefore:

New Information ≠ Revision Requirement by Default.
------------------------------ Revision Trigger

A Revision Trigger is a materially relevant condition that
creates a legitimate basis for reconsidering an existing Event
Boundary.

Potential triggers may include: newly discovered operational
information corrected source information previously unavailable
evidence resolved boundary uncertainty resolved boundary conflict
incorrect inclusion incorrect exclusion incorrect separation
newly established event distinctness newly established event
unity structural transformation system reconciliation material
inconsistency downstream discovery of a boundary defect
autonomous-system correction machine-generated correction prior
governance error

A trigger initiates reconsideration.

It does not predetermine the result.

Therefore:

Revision Trigger ≠ Boundary Change.
------------------------------ Revision Materiality

Not every difference justifies revision.

EBRM must determine whether the identified issue is materially
capable of affecting the governed Event Boundary.

A difference may be:

Non-Material

Potentially Material

Material

Materiality Unresolved

Non-material differences may be recorded without changing the
Event Boundary.

Material differences require governed reconsideration.

This prevents unnecessary boundary instability.
------------------------------ Affected Boundary Elements

Where revision is justified, EBRM must identify which part of the
Event Boundary is affected.

Affected elements may include: included activity excluded
activity separation structure overlapping structure nested
structure composite structure distributed structure
machine-generated structure autonomous structure boundary
qualification unresolved boundary condition

Revision should remain proportionate to the affected boundary
element.

Therefore:

Local Boundary Defect ≠ Full Event Reconstruction by Default.
------------------------------ Boundary Preservation

EBRM must explicitly allow the existing Event Boundary to remain
unchanged where reconsideration does not establish a material
reason for revision.

Possible outcome:

Boundary Reviewed — Existing Boundary
Preserved

This is important because revision governance is not a mechanism
for producing change.

It is a mechanism for governing whether change is legitimate.

Therefore:

Boundary Review ≠ Boundary Revision.
------------------------------ Boundary Expansion

A boundary may require expansion when materially relevant
activity was incorrectly excluded or previously unavailable
information establishes legitimate inclusion.

Boundary expansion must identify: what is being added, why it is
being added, the prior state, the revised state, supporting
basis, downstream implications.

However:

Additional Related Activity ≠ Automatic Boundary Expansion.

The activity must satisfy the governed boundary basis.
------------------------------ Boundary Contraction

A boundary may require contraction where activity previously
included is later established as outside the legitimate
structural extent of the event.

Boundary contraction must preserve the historical fact that the
activity was previously included.

Therefore:

Boundary Contraction ≠ Historical Deletion.

The previous boundary state remains reconstructable.
------------------------------ Boundary Restructuring

Some revisions cannot be represented merely as expansion or
contraction.

New information may establish that: one boundary should become
multiple boundaries, multiple boundaries should be consolidated,
overlapping structures require reorganization, nested structures
were incorrectly represented, separation logic must be
reconsidered.

Such cases may require Boundary Restructuring.

Where restructuring creates materially distinct event structures,
EBRM must preserve linkage to the EBSM separation basis.

EBRM must not independently absorb EBSM's separation function.
------------------------------ Revision of Inclusion

Where previous inclusion becomes materially questionable, EBRM
may reopen the relevant inclusion result established through
EBSCM.

The revision record must identify: previous inclusion state,
reason for reconsideration, new information, revised state,
resulting boundary effect.

EBRM governs the revision.

It does not replace EBSCM's underlying scope methodology.
------------------------------ Revision of Exclusion

Where previous exclusion becomes materially questionable, EBRM
may reopen the relevant exclusion result established through
EBEM.

The revision must preserve: previous exclusion basis, revision
trigger, revised determination, resulting Event Boundary impact.

Therefore:

Previously Excluded ≠ Permanently Excluded.

But equally:

New Association ≠ Automatic Inclusion.
------------------------------ Revision of Separation

Where later information materially challenges an existing
separation structure, EBRM may trigger reconsideration of the
EBSM result.

Possible outcomes include: separation preserved, separation
modified, separation removed, additional separation required,
separation remains unresolved.

EBRM governs revision control.

EBSM remains the owner of separation logic.
------------------------------ Boundary Version Continuity

Every material Event Boundary revision must preserve continuity
between boundary states.

A conceptual version sequence may be represented as:

Boundary State B1

↓

Revision Trigger

↓

Boundary Review

↓

Boundary State B2

↓

Subsequent Revision

↓

Boundary State B3

Each state must remain distinguishable and traceable.

Therefore:

Current Boundary ≠ Only Boundary State That Matters.
------------------------------ Historical Preservation

Revision must never silently overwrite prior governed reality.

The architecture must preserve: previous Event Boundary previous
governance basis revision trigger changed elements unchanged
elements revision rationale revised Event Boundary time of
governance revision linkage between states

This supports later reconstruction of what the system knew and
how the Event Boundary evolved.

Therefore:

Event Boundary Revision ≠ Historical Erasure.
------------------------------ Correction ≠ Revision

A purely technical correction may not materially change the Event
Boundary.

For example: spelling correction identifier formatting correction
duplicate metadata removal non-material system-field correction

Such corrections should not automatically create a new material
boundary version.

Therefore:

Technical Correction ≠ Material Boundary Revision by Default.

Materiality governs whether EBRM revision logic is required.
------------------------------ New Event ≠ Boundary Revision

Not every later development should modify an existing Event
Boundary.

New operational activity may represent a new event rather than an
extension of the existing event.

Therefore:

New Activity ≠ Existing Boundary Expansion by Default.

Where the later activity represents a materially new occurrence,
it may require new TREGA™ Event Recognition and Event Formation
rather than revision of the previous boundary.

EBRM must prevent revision from becoming unlimited event
absorption. ------------------------------ Material
Transformation

In exceptional cases, new information may establish that the
event has transformed so materially that revision of the existing
boundary would no longer preserve event identity coherently.

Such a condition may require escalation back into appropriate
TREGA™ lifecycle governance.

Therefore:

Material Event Transformation may require New Event Formation.

EBRM must not force continuity where operational continuity no
longer exists. ------------------------------ Boundary Revision
and Event Timing

The time at which a boundary is revised is not necessarily the
operational time of the event.

Likewise, discovering an omitted component later does not mean
that component occurred at the time of revision.

Therefore:

Boundary Revision Time ≠ Event Time.

Final Event Timing belongs to:

TRETS — Tax-Relevant Event Timing Standard.
------------------------------ Boundary Revision and Tax Rules

Tax law may change after an Event Boundary has already been
governed.

A jurisdiction may later classify or tax the event differently.

This does not automatically justify reconstructing the Event
Boundary.

Therefore:

Tax Rule Change ≠ Event Boundary Change by Default.

TREGA™ must preserve operational event representation
independently of downstream tax-rule evolution.
------------------------------ Boundary Revision and Tax Outcome

A tax authority, tax engine, or compliance system may identify
that a different Event Boundary would produce a different tax
result.

That fact alone does not authorize TREGA™ to revise operational
reality.

Therefore:

Different Tax Outcome ≠ Boundary Revision Basis by Default.

Revision must remain grounded in governed event structure.
------------------------------ Boundary Revision and Jurisdiction

A jurisdiction may legally aggregate or divide operational events
differently for taxation.

Such legal treatment does not automatically alter TREGA's
governed Event Boundary.

Therefore:

Jurisdictional Aggregation ≠ Operational Event Aggregation.

Jurisdictional Separation ≠ Operational Event Separation.

The same governed event layer may support multiple jurisdictional
evaluations. ------------------------------ Autonomous Boundary
Revision

Autonomous systems may discover inconsistencies or new
information without direct human intervention.

Examples include: reconciliation agents machine monitoring
distributed ledgers automated settlement systems AI anomaly
detection autonomous logistics systems

An autonomous system may generate a Revision Trigger.

It must not silently rewrite the Event Boundary.

Therefore:

Autonomous Detection ≠ Autonomous Authority to Rewrite Governed
Event History.

The same governed revision requirements apply regardless of
whether the trigger originates from a human, organization,
machine, or autonomous agent. ------------------------------
Revision Conflict

Different sources may propose incompatible boundary revisions.

For example: one system indicates expansion, another indicates
contraction, one participant disputes separation, an autonomous
system detects conflicting structure, later records contradict
earlier operational representations.

EBRM must preserve the conflict.

It must not select a revision merely because one source has
technical priority.

Possible state:

Boundary Revision Conflict

The boundary may remain qualified or unresolved until sufficient
governance basis exists. ------------------------------ Revision
Uncertainty

A Revision Trigger may be legitimate while the correct revised
boundary remains uncertain.

EBRM must distinguish:

Reason to Reconsider

from

Sufficient Basis to Change

Therefore:

Revision Readiness ≠ Revision Determination.

Where uncertainty remains material, EBRM preserves:

Boundary Revision Unresolved

rather than creating artificial certainty.
------------------------------ Boundary Confirmation

Once revision analysis is complete, EBRM must establish whether
the resulting Event Boundary is sufficiently governed to proceed.

Boundary Confirmation may result from: preservation of the
existing boundary, legitimate expansion, legitimate contraction,
legitimate restructuring, resolution of separation, qualified
acceptance of non-material uncertainty.

The confirmation does not establish taxability.

It establishes only that the structural Event Boundary is
sufficiently governed for subsequent TREGA™ lifecycle stages.
------------------------------ Governed Bounded Event

The principal final output of EBRM and TREBS is:

Governed Bounded Event

A Governed Bounded Event is an event whose structural extent has
been sufficiently: identified, scoped, excluded, separated where
necessary, revised where materially justified, preserved where
revision was not justified, traceably maintained, and confirmed
for progression.

It does not yet establish final Event Timing, Event Location,
Event Participation, Event-to-Value Association, Event
Relationships, Event Determination, or legal taxability.

Therefore:

Governed Bounded Event ≠ Determined Tax-Relevant Event.

EBRM requires that:

1.

revision begins from a governed Event Boundary structure. 2.

a legitimate Revision Trigger is identified before material
revision occurs. 3.

Revision Trigger and revision outcome remain distinct. 4.

revision materiality is evaluated. 5.

non-material differences do not automatically reopen the Event
Boundary. 6.

affected boundary elements are explicitly identified. 7.

revision remains proportionate to the affected boundary
condition. 8.

preservation of the existing boundary remains a legitimate
outcome. 9.

expansion requires a governed inclusion basis. 10.

contraction preserves historical inclusion state. 11.

restructuring preserves linkage to prior boundary states. 12.

inclusion revision remains traceable to EBSCM. 13.

exclusion revision remains traceable to EBEM. 14.

separation revision remains traceable to EBSM. 15.

previous Event Boundary states remain reconstructable. 16.

material revisions maintain Boundary Version Continuity. 17.

technical corrections do not automatically become material
revisions. 18.

new activity is not automatically absorbed into an existing Event
Boundary. 19.

material transformation may require new Event Formation rather
than boundary revision. 20.

revision time remains distinct from Event Timing. 21.

tax-rule change does not automatically alter Event Boundary. 22.

tax outcome does not determine revision legitimacy. 23.

jurisdictional aggregation or separation does not redefine
operational Event Boundary. 24.

autonomous systems may identify revision triggers but may not
silently rewrite governed history. 25.

revision conflict and uncertainty remain explicit. 26.

confirmed Event Boundary is sufficiently governed before transfer
to TRETS.

------------------------------ Governance Logic

EBRM applies the following logic:

Has a legitimate Boundary Revision Trigger been identified?

If no:

→ preserve the existing Event Boundary.

If yes:

→ assess materiality.

Is the identified difference materially relevant to the Event
Boundary?

If no:

→ record the information and preserve the boundary.

If yes:

→ identify affected boundary elements.

Does the new governance basis support a boundary change?

If no:

→ preserve the existing boundary and record the review.

If yes:

→ determine whether expansion, contraction, restructuring, or
reconsideration of prior scope, exclusion, or separation is
required.

Would the proposed change destroy coherent continuity with the
existing event?

If yes:

→ evaluate whether the condition requires new Event Formation
rather than Event Boundary revision.

If no:

→ construct the revised boundary.

Can the previous and revised states remain reconstructable and
traceably linked?

If no:

→ Boundary Revision Unresolved.

If yes:

→ preserve Boundary Version Continuity.

Is the resulting Event Boundary sufficiently governed for
subsequent lifecycle processing?

If yes:

→ confirm Governed Bounded Event.

If no:

→ preserve qualified, conflicting, or unresolved state.

------------------------------ Enterprise
Application

Enterprise event records evolve.

Initial representations are frequently incomplete because
information arrives asynchronously from: commerce systems payment
systems logistics platforms banks suppliers customers autonomous
systems distributed infrastructure reconciliation processes

Without controlled revision governance, organizations commonly
face two bad options:

freeze an incorrect historical event representation

or

silently overwrite the original representation whenever new
information appears.

EBRM provides a third model:

governed event evolution with preserved historical continuity.

A boundary may legitimately change while every material state
remains reconstructable.

This enables ERP systems, digital platforms, tax infrastructure,
AI systems, auditors, regulators, and downstream jurisdictional
engines to understand not only the current Event Boundary, but
also how and why that boundary became legitimate.
------------------------------ Use Case 1 — Cross-Border
Marketplace Event Changes After Partial Refund and Fulfilment
Reconciliation

A marketplace initially records one customer order containing
products from several sellers.

TREBS establishes several governed Event Boundaries based on the
operational structures available at the time.

Weeks later, reconciliation reveals that: one shipment never
occurred, one product was fulfilled by a different seller, one
settlement was incorrectly associated with the original
fulfilment, and a partial refund relates only to one previously
bounded event.

The platform cannot simply overwrite the original Event
Boundaries.

EBRM identifies the new reconciliation information as potential
Revision Triggers.

It evaluates materiality and determines which Event Boundaries
are affected.

One boundary is preserved.

Another is contracted.

A third requires reconsideration of its separation structure.

Every previous boundary state remains linked to the revised
state.

The resulting Governed Bounded Events can then proceed through
Event Timing and later TREGA™ governance.

The tax engines of different jurisdictions may subsequently
interpret the events differently, but those tax interpretations
do not rewrite the underlying operational boundary history.
------------------------------ Use Case 2 — Autonomous Industrial
Network Discovers That Previously Unified Machine Activity
Contained a Distinct Event

An autonomous manufacturing network coordinates robots,
procurement agents, inventory systems, production equipment, and
settlement services.

Initial operational information indicates that thousands of
machine actions belong to one governed production event.

After reconciliation, an autonomous monitoring system discovers
that part of the resource consumption and machine execution was
independently initiated by another production objective.

The new information creates a legitimate Revision Trigger.

EBSM separation logic is reconsidered through the governed
revision process.

The previously unified boundary is restructured into two
materially distinct event structures.

EBRM preserves: the original Event Boundary, the new information,
the revision trigger, the separation reconsideration, the revised
Event Boundaries, and the linkage between all boundary states.

The autonomous monitoring system therefore contributes new
information without gaining uncontrolled authority to rewrite
historical event reality.

Machine-discovered reality can change a governed Event Boundary —
but only through governed, traceable revision.
------------------------------ Canonical Boundary

EBRM governs the readiness, triggering, materiality,
reconsideration, preservation, expansion, contraction,
restructuring, version continuity, conflict, uncertainty,
traceability, and confirmation of Event Boundary revisions. It

Event Boundary Scope determination, original Event Boundary
Exclusion, original Event Boundary Separation, Event Timing,
Event Location, Event Participation, Event-to-Value Association,
Event Relationship determination, final Event Determination,
legal taxability determination, Tax Jurisdiction determination,
taxpayer determination, tax-base calculation, tax-rate
application, exemptions, deductions, tax liability, reporting
obligations, payment, collection, or enforcement.

Canonical Closing Statement

The Event Boundary Revision Module ensures that a governed Event
Boundary can respond to better information without losing
methodological control over its history.

It allows legitimate correction of the governed structural extent
of an event while preserving previous boundary states, revision
reasons, material changes, unresolved conditions, and continuity
between representations.

It prevents both rigid preservation of a materially incorrect
boundary and uncontrolled rewriting of operational reality.

An Event Boundary may change when reality justifies the change —
but governed history must never disappear merely because the
current representation has changed.
