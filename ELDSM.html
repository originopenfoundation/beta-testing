Canonical Definition

The Event Distributed Location Module (ELDSM) governs how a
Tax-Relevant Event is spatially represented when its materially
constitutive activity occurs across multiple physical, digital,
hybrid, machine, autonomous, or infrastructural locations and
cannot legitimately be reduced to one operational location
without distorting the reality of the event.

ELDSM operates on the Governed Event Physical Location
established by ELPM and the Governed Event Digital Location
established by ELDM.

Its canonical task is:

to establish a coherent, traceable, and operationally justified
Governed Event Distributed Location where multiple locations
materially constitute the same governed event, without forcing
that event into an artificial single-location representation or
converting distributed operational reality into
jurisdiction-specific tax allocation.

The principal transition is:

Governed Event Physical Location +
Governed Event Digital Location

↓

Governed Event Distributed Location

ELDSM governs spatial distribution of the event.

It does not determine how taxation rights, tax bases, revenues,
profits, obligations, or liabilities are allocated among
jurisdictions.

Operational Role

Modern economic activity increasingly occurs across distributed
operational environments.

A single governed event may involve: physical activity in several
places; digital execution across several cloud regions; edge and
central infrastructure; machines operating at different sites;
autonomous agents invoking services across multiple systems;
platform activity spanning several operational environments;
distributed ledger infrastructure; cross-border logistics; remote
control and physical execution; or simultaneous physical and
digital activity.

Traditional systems often attempt to reduce such events to one
location.

They may select: customer country; seller country; server
country; billing address; headquarters; IP location;
transaction-processing location; device location; or another
convenient geographic proxy.

Such reduction may simplify downstream processing while
materially misrepresenting the event.

ELDSM exists to prevent that distortion.

Where the event is genuinely distributed, its governed location
may also be distributed.

Distributed Operational Reality ≠ Governance Failure.

A legitimate governance result does not require artificial
spatial simplicity.

1. Distributed Location Candidate Identification

ELDSM shall identify whether the governed physical and digital
locations indicate that materially constitutive parts of the same
event occur across more than one location.

A Distributed Location Candidate exists where multiple locations
may legitimately belong to the same governed event.

However:

Multiple Location Signals ≠ Distributed Event by Default.

The locations must materially relate to the bounded event itself.
2. Constitutive Location Assessment

ELDSM shall distinguish locations constituting the event from
locations that merely support, observe, record, route, finance,
administer, or provide infrastructure to it.

A location may be relevant without being constitutive.

Therefore:

Relevant Location ≠ Constitutive Event Location by Default.

Only locations with a materially justified operational
relationship to the event shall form the Governed Event
Distributed Location. 3. Distributed Event Determination

ELDSM shall determine whether the event is legitimately
represented as distributed.

A distributed representation is appropriate where: no single
location sufficiently represents the event; multiple locations
contain constitutive activity; the event exists through
coordinated activity across locations; physical and digital
components materially interact across locations; execution
migrates between locations; or simultaneous distributed execution
forms part of the event.

Multiple Locations ≠ Multiple Events by Default.

The Event Boundary remains governed by TREBS.

ELDSM shall not fragment one bounded event merely because its
spatial reality is complex. 4. Physical Distribution

An event may contain constitutive physical activity across
multiple locations.

Examples include: goods moving through multiple operational
stages; coordinated manufacturing across facilities;
infrastructure operations across sites; distributed energy
systems; multi-site industrial processes; logistics networks; or
machines performing coordinated operations in different
locations.

ELDSM shall preserve those locations where they materially form
part of the same event.

Multiple Physical Locations ≠ Multiple Events by Default. 5.
Digital Distribution

Digital events may execute across multiple computational
environments.

Relevant locations may include: cloud regions; edge nodes;
execution clusters; platform environments; API services;
autonomous-agent environments; distributed databases; or
networked machine systems.

Where these environments materially constitute the event:

Distributed Digital Execution ≠ Single Digital Location by
Default.

ELDSM shall preserve the distributed structure rather than
selecting one infrastructure component as the authoritative
location. 6. Hybrid Physical-Digital Distribution

Many modern events combine physical and digital activity.

For example:

↓

Edge Controller

↓

↓

External
API

↓

Machine Execution

The event may therefore possess both physical and digital spatial
dimensions.

ELDSM shall permit a hybrid distributed representation.

Physical Location + Digital Location ≠ Requirement to Select One
Dominant Location.

Both may legitimately belong to the governed event. 7.
Simultaneous Distribution

Constitutive activity may occur in several locations at the same
time.

ELDSM shall support simultaneous multi-location representation.

Examples include: distributed computing; synchronized industrial
systems; multi-node autonomous operations; replicated digital
execution; coordinated machine networks; or concurrent platform
processes.

Simultaneous Multi-Location Activity ≠ Spatial Conflict.

Several locations can legitimately describe one event
simultaneously. 8. Sequential Distribution

An event may move through locations over time.

For example:

Location A

↓

Location B

↓

Location C

ELDSM shall preserve this spatial progression where all stages
remain within the same governed Event Boundary.

Location Transition ≠ New Event by Default.

TRETS governs the temporal structure.

ELDSM governs the spatial progression. 9.
Dynamic Location

Some events possess locations that change dynamically.

Examples include: mobile machines; vehicles; aircraft; vessels;
mobile devices; migrating cloud workloads; autonomous robots;
mobile edge systems; or dynamic digital services.

ELDSM shall support dynamic location without requiring every
location change to create a new event.

Changing Location ≠ Changing Event Identity by Default. 10.
Location Contribution

Not every constitutive location contributes equally to the event.

ELDSM may represent the operational contribution of locations
where supported by evidence.

Possible states may include: primary constitutive; secondary
constitutive; supporting constitutive; transitional;
simultaneous; sequential; conditional; or indeterminate.

However:

Operational Location Contribution ≠ Tax Allocation Weight.

A location's operational significance shall not automatically
become a percentage of taxation rights, profit allocation, or tax
base. 11. Primary Location

Some distributed events may possess an operationally dominant
location.

ELDSM may identify such a location where evidence supports it.

However:

Primary Operational Location ≠ Exclusive Event Location.

Primary Operational Location ≠ Primary Tax Jurisdiction.

Dominance shall not erase other constitutive locations. 12. No
Primary Location

A distributed event may legitimately have no single primary
location.

This is a valid governance outcome.

No Single Primary Location ≠ Unresolved Event Location.

Where operational reality is genuinely distributed, the correct
representation may be:

Governed Distributed Location — No Single
Dominant Location

rather than an artificial geographic assignment. 13. Distributed
Cloud Execution

Cloud services may distribute event execution dynamically across
multiple regions.

ELDSM shall distinguish: deliberate multi-region execution;
dynamic workload migration; redundancy; replication; failover;
storage distribution; and constitutive processing.

Only materially constitutive locations shall form the distributed
event representation.

Cloud Replication ≠ Distributed Event Execution by Default.

Redundant Infrastructure ≠ Constitutive Event Location by
Default. 14. Edge-Cloud Distribution

An event may begin at an edge device, undergo local processing,
invoke cloud services, and return a result to physical
infrastructure.

ELDSM shall preserve this structure where the components form one
bounded event.

Edge Location ≠ Complete Event Location.

Cloud Location ≠ Complete Event Location.

The legitimate governed representation may be their operationally
justified combination. 15. AI Agent Distributed Location

AI agents may operate through: orchestration environments; model
providers; external tools; APIs; databases; payment systems;
digital platforms; physical machines; and other agents.

The agent's activity may therefore have no stable single digital
location.

ELDSM shall govern the distributed operational environments
materially constituting the event.

AI Agent Distributed Execution ≠ Agent Legal Residence.

AI Agent Execution Geography ≠ Owner Tax Residence.

AI Agent Infrastructure Distribution ≠ Tax Jurisdiction
Allocation. 16. Autonomous-System Distribution

Autonomous systems may coordinate decisions and actions across
multiple physical and digital locations without contemporaneous
human control.

ELDSM shall preserve this spatial reality independently from the
location of: system owner; operator; developer; supervising
organization; manufacturer; or human beneficiary.

Autonomous Event Location ≠ Human Controller Location by Default.
17. Machine-to-Machine Distribution

Machine-to-machine events may involve machines physically located
in different places while their communications and decision
processes operate through distributed digital infrastructure.

ELDSM shall support representation such as:

Machine A Physical Location + Machine B Physical Location + Edge
Environment + Cloud Execution Environment

↓

Governed Event
Distributed Location

without prematurely assigning the event to one jurisdiction. 18.
Platform Distribution

Platforms may coordinate events across: buyers; sellers; service
providers; infrastructure; payment systems; logistics providers;
and digital services.

ELDSM shall govern only locations materially constituting the
bounded event.

Platform Global Presence ≠ Event Distributed Location by Default.

A platform's worldwide infrastructure shall not automatically
become the spatial footprint of every event processed through it.
19. Distributed Ledger Environments

Events involving distributed ledgers may be technically
replicated across many nodes.

Node distribution alone shall not establish the distributed
location of the event.

Ledger Node Distribution ≠ Event Location Distribution by
Default.

ELDSM shall determine whether specific distributed components
materially constitute the governed event rather than merely
maintaining replicated infrastructure. 20. Network Distribution

Network traffic may traverse numerous jurisdictions.

Transit does not automatically constitute event distribution.

Network Path ≠ Distributed Event Location by Default.

Routing infrastructure shall be included only where its
operational role materially forms part of the event. 21. Data
Distribution

Event-related data may be stored or replicated globally.

Such distribution shall not automatically create distributed
Event Location.

Distributed Data Storage ≠ Distributed Event Location by Default.

Storage becomes spatially constitutive only where its role forms
part of the governed event itself. 22. Distributed Service
Delivery

Services may be delivered through multiple locations.

For example, a digital service may involve: customer interaction
in one place; computational execution elsewhere; specialized
processing elsewhere; and physical fulfillment elsewhere.

ELDSM shall preserve materially constitutive locations without
forcing them into one geographic point.

Distributed Service Delivery ≠ Single Place of Supply by Default.

Legal place-of-supply determination remains outside TREGA™. 23.
Distributed Production

Production may occur across several facilities or machine
environments while remaining part of one governed event.

ELDSM shall preserve the spatial structure established by
operational evidence.

It shall not infer: origin law; customs origin; tax situs;
permanent establishment; transfer-pricing allocation; or taxation
rights.

Operational Production Location ≠ Legal Origin Determination. 24.
Distributed Logistics

Logistics events may involve movement through many locations.

ELDSM shall distinguish spatial progression belonging to one
event from separate events formed under TREFS and bounded under
TREBS.

Route Complexity ≠ Event Multiplicity by Default.

A multi-location journey can remain one governed event where its
Event Boundary supports that representation. 25. Cross-Border
Distribution

A Tax-Relevant Event may operationally cross national borders.

ELDSM shall record such distribution where materially relevant.

However:

Cross-Border Event ≠ Multi-Jurisdiction Tax Liability by Default.

Operational border crossing may become an input to
jurisdiction-specific tax or customs analysis.

It is not itself a legal conclusion. 26.
Distribution and Event Boundary

ELDSM shall not redefine the Event Boundary merely because the
event spans multiple locations.

TREBS remains authoritative for Event Boundary.

Spatial Distribution ≠ Boundary Expansion by Default.

A location belongs to the distributed representation only where
the activity at that location is already legitimately associated
with the bounded event. 27. Distribution and Event Timing

Distributed locations may correspond to different points or
intervals in Event Timing.

and Participation

Participants may operate from locations different from the
locations constituting the event.

Therefore:

Participant Distribution ≠ Event Location Distribution by
Default.

TREPS governs Event Participation.

ELDSM shall not infer taxpayer status, participant legal
residence, identity, authority, or responsibility from spatial
distribution. 29. Distribution and Value

Value may originate, move, change state, develop, degrade, or be
recognized across different locations.

VFM® governs the Value Lifecycle.

ELDSM may associate governed spatial information with an event
involving value.

It shall not determine value ownership or value lifecycle state.

Distributed Value Activity ≠ Distributed Event Location by
Default. 30. Distribution and Event Relationships

Multiple events may occur in different locations and remain
related.

Conversely, one event may occur across multiple locations.

These are distinct governance questions.

Multiple Locations Within One Event ≠ Multiple Related Events.

Multiple Related Events ≠ One Distributed Event.

TRERLS governs Event Relationships.

ELDSM governs spatial distribution within the bounded event. 31.
Distribution Precision

ELDSM shall represent distributed locations only at precision
supported by evidence and operational relevance.

Possible levels may include: exact point; facility; city; region;
country; cloud region; logical digital environment; multi-region
environment; network zone; distributed physical network;
distributed digital environment; or qualified unknown location.

More Location Data ≠ Greater Governance Precision by Default. 32.
Distribution Uncertainty

Some constitutive locations may be known while others remain
uncertain.

ELDSM shall permit partial spatial governance.

For example:

Location A — Established

Location B — Established

Location C — Regional Only

Digital Environment D — Distributed /
Exact Infrastructure Unknown

The event may still possess a legitimate Governed Event
Distributed Location.

Partial Location Uncertainty ≠ Total Location Failure. 33.
Distribution Conflict

Conflicting evidence concerning one spatial component shall not
invalidate correctly established components.

ELDSM shall isolate the conflict.

One Conflicted Location ≠ Entire Distributed Location Invalid.

Likewise:

Multiple Different Locations ≠ Conflict by Default.

Different locations may all legitimately constitute the event.
34. Distribution Continuity

Distributed spatial representation shall preserve continuity as
locations enter, leave, migrate, fail, recover, or change during
the event.

A constitutive location may cease participation while the event
continues elsewhere.

Location Departure ≠ Event Completion.

Location Addition ≠ New Event by Default.

Infrastructure Failover ≠ Event Replacement by Default. 35.
Distributed Location Traceability

Every constitutive location shall remain traceable to: source
evidence; operational role; Event Boundary; relevant Event
Timing; location type; confidence; uncertainty; conflicts;
corrections; and governance decisions.

A downstream system shall be able to reconstruct why a location
was included in or excluded from the distributed representation.
36. Distributed Location Correction

Distributed Event Location may require correction when evidence
changes.

Corrections shall preserve governance history.

Location Correction ≠ Historical Erasure.

A corrected location record shall preserve the previous state,
reason for correction, supporting evidence, and resulting state
where required. 37. Distributed Location and Tax Jurisdiction

ELDSM shall maintain a strict separation between distributed
operational reality and legal jurisdiction allocation.

It shall not determine: Tax Jurisdiction; tax nexus; permanent
establishment; significant economic presence; place of supply;
source jurisdiction; residence; legal situs; customs origin;
taxable presence; taxing rights; tax-base allocation; profit
allocation; revenue allocation; transfer-pricing outcome; or
jurisdiction-specific tax liability.

Distributed Event Location ≠ Distributed Tax Liability.

Multiple Event Locations ≠ Multiple Tax Jurisdictions by Default.

Operational Location Contribution ≠ Taxing Rights Allocation.

Cross-Border Distribution ≠ Legal Nexus Determination.

Distributed Digital Infrastructure ≠ Permanent Establishment by
Default. 38. Jurisdiction-Neutral Representation

ELDSM shall preserve the event independently from the legal rules

This allows the same governed event to remain operationally
coherent even when several jurisdictions later evaluate it
differently. 46. Use Case 1 — Autonomous Cross-Border
Manufacturing Network

A multinational industrial network uses autonomous production
systems across several facilities.

A machine in one country identifies a production requirement.

An AI orchestration service running through distributed cloud
infrastructure generates a production plan.

A specialized component is manufactured at a second facility.

An autonomous logistics system transfers the component to a third
facility.

Final machine processing occurs there.

Digital coordination continues across edge systems, cloud
services, APIs, and machine controllers throughout the process.

If TREBS establishes that the relevant activity forms one bounded
Tax-Relevant Event, ELDSM does not force that event into the
country containing the first machine, the AI server, corporate
headquarters, or final facility.

It governs the materially constitutive spatial structure of the
event.

The output may legitimately contain multiple physical and digital
locations.

It does not decide which country possesses taxation rights.

The event may be operationally distributed before any
jurisdiction decides how that distribution matters under law. 47.
Use Case 2 — Autonomous Global Digital Service

An AI agent autonomously acquires and coordinates a digital
service.

The agent executes through one cloud environment.

Model inference occurs through an external provider in another
region.

Data processing occurs across several regions.

A specialized API is invoked through another provider.

Payment infrastructure operates elsewhere.

The service is delivered to a machine-controlled system in
another country.

No single technical location adequately represents the event.

Selecting the server location, customer device, corporate
headquarters, billing country, or one cloud region would erase
material parts of the operational reality.

ELDSM establishes a Governed Event Distributed Location
containing only those physical and digital environments that
materially constitute the event.

Downstream jurisdictions may then independently apply their own
nexus, place-of-supply, source, residence, or other tax rules.

Distributed digital reality is governed once; legal
jurisdictional interpretation may occur many times. 48. Canonical
Boundary

ELDSM owns the governance of distributed spatial representation
within the bounded Tax-Relevant Event, including physical
distribution, digital distribution, hybrid distribution,
simultaneous distribution, sequential distribution, dynamic
location, constitutive-location assessment, operational location
contribution, primary/no-primary location states,
temporal-spatial association, distributed-location precision,
uncertainty, conflict, continuity, correction, and traceability.

ELDSM does not own Event Location Signal Identification, which
belongs to ELIM.

It does not own Physical Event Location, which belongs to ELPM.

It does not own Digital Event Location, which belongs to ELDM.

It does not own final Event Location Correlation, which belongs
to ELCM.

It does not redefine Event Context, Event Recognition, Event
Formation, Event Boundary, Event Timing, Event Participation,
Participant Identity, Value Association, Event Relationships,
Event Determination, or Event Record Governance.

It does not determine Tax Jurisdiction, tax nexus, permanent
establishment, significant economic presence, tax residence,
source jurisdiction, place of supply, legal situs, customs
origin, taxable presence, taxing rights, tax-base allocation,
revenue allocation, profit allocation, transfer-pricing outcome,
Legal Tax Point, Tax Period, taxability, Tax Liability, tax rate,
reporting obligation, or any jurisdiction-specific tax
consequence.

Canonical Closing Statement

ELDSM preserves the spatial reality of events that genuinely
exist across multiple physical, digital, machine, autonomous, or
infrastructural locations without forcing operational complexity
into an artificial single-location model.

A distributed event does not become geographically simple merely
because a downstream system requires one country field.

ELDSM governs where the event is distributed — not how taxation
rights are distributed among jurisdictions.

TREGA™ governs the event. The jurisdiction governs the tax.
