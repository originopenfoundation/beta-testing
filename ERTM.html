Canonical Definition

The Event Record Traceability Module (ERTM) governs how a linked
Tax-Relevant Event Record remains reconstructable across its
materially relevant governance lineage, source references, state
transitions, corrections, reconsiderations, transformations, and
downstream representations.

Its principal transition is:

Governed Event Record Linkage State

↓

Event Record Traceability
Governance

↓

Governed Event Record Traceability State

ERTM establishes whether the Record's governed history can be
reconstructed sufficiently to determine: what Record is being
examined; which Event it represents; which Determination applies;
which material governance states contributed to it; what changed;
why a materially relevant change occurred; and which Record state
is currently effective.

Its foundational distinction is:

Traceability ≠ Audit.

Operational Role

A Record may be correctly identified, structurally complete, and
richly linked while still being difficult to reconstruct over
time.

ERTM addresses this problem.

It answers:

Can the governed Record and its materially relevant history be
reconstructed from its present state back through the governance
states and transitions that produced it?

ERTM prevents: orphaned Record states; undocumented Record
transformation; loss of prior material states; broken
Determination lineage; invisible correction; silent supersession;
unexplained Record changes; and historical state collapse.

Traceability Object

The traceability object is the governed lineage through which the
Event Record and its materially relevant states can be
reconstructed.

Traceability is not merely the presence of links.

It is the governed ability to reconstruct meaningful sequence and
state history.

Link Network ≠ Traceability by itself.

Trace Scope

ERTM shall define the materially required scope of
reconstruction.

Trace scope may include: Record identity; underlying Event; TREDS
Determination; material upstream governance states; Record
structure state; Record linkage state; material qualifications;
uncertainty; reconsideration; corrections; supersession;
transformations; current effective Record state.

Traceability should remain proportional.

Maximum Historical Data ≠ Optimal Traceability.

Trace Gap

A Trace Gap exists where a materially necessary part of Record
history cannot be reconstructed.

Possible causes include: missing prior state; broken link;
unknown transformation; deleted technical source; identifier
loss; incomplete migration; unresolved lineage.

Trace Gap Materiality

Not every missing historical detail is material.

ERTM shall evaluate whether the gap affects: Record
interpretation; Determination reliance; lifecycle continuity;
downstream use; reconstruction.

Missing Data ≠ Material Trace Gap by default.

Trace Ambiguity

Trace ambiguity exists where multiple plausible histories exist
and one cannot be sufficiently established.

Ambiguity should remain explicit.

Plausible History ≠ Established Trace.

Trace Conflict

Trace conflict exists where material sources support incompatible
Record histories.

Conflict may involve: inconsistent state sequence; incompatible
Determination references; contradictory correction histories;

ERTM should not silently select one history without governance
basis.

Trace Reconstruction Sufficiency

A Record is sufficiently traceable where materially relevant
lineage can be reconstructed with sufficient confidence and
governance clarity for its intended downstream use.

Complete reproduction of every technical interaction is not
required.

Governance Requirements

ERTM implementations shall ensure that: a valid Governed Event
Record Linkage State exists; materially required trace scope is
defined; Record identity lineage remains reconstructable;
Event-to-Record association remains traceable; Determination
lineage remains reconstructable; materially relevant
governance-state references are preservable; material state
transitions remain traceable; transition basis is recorded or
referenced where necessary; prior material states are not
silently erased; current effective state remains distinguishable
from historical state; qualification history remains
reconstructable where material; uncertainty history remains
traceable where relevant; reconsideration does not erase prior
Determination history; corrections preserve materially relevant
prior states; supersession preserves predecessor/successor
lineage; technical version history remains distinguishable from
Event history; transformations remain traceable where they could
affect meaning; cross-system representations can be associated
where material; technical logs are not treated as sufficient
traceability by default; immutable records are not treated as
complete traceability by default; trace gaps are assessed for
materiality; ambiguity and conflict remain visible;
machine-generated changes remain traceable; Record Traceability
remains distinct from Audit, Linkage, Interoperability, and Event
Lifecycle Governance; and the resulting Traceability State is
suitable for ERINM.

Governance Logic

IF no valid Governed Event Record Linkage State exists, THEN ERTM
shall not establish complete Record Traceability.

IF a materially relevant Record state changed, THEN sufficient
transition basis shall be preserved or referenced.

IF a prior Record state materially affects interpretation of the
current Record, THEN that state shall remain reconstructable.

IF a Determination was reconsidered, THEN prior and current
effective Determination states shall remain distinguishable.

IF a Record was corrected, THEN the correction shall not silently
erase materially relevant prior representation.

IF a Record was superseded, THEN predecessor and successor
lineage shall remain reconstructable.

IF technical transformation occurs while canonical Record
identity remains continuous, THEN transformation lineage should
preserve that continuity.

IF a trace gap exists but does not materially affect
reconstruction, THEN the Record may remain traceable with
appropriate qualification.

IF a material trace gap, ambiguity, or conflict prevents reliable
reconstruction, THEN Traceability shall remain qualified or
insufficient.

IF materially relevant Record lineage can be reconstructed
sufficiently, THEN ERTM shall establish the Governed Event Record
Traceability State and hand it to ERINM.

Enterprise Application

ERTM provides the reconstructability layer required before a
governed Event Record can be safely exchanged across systems and
organizations.

The canonical enterprise path is:

ERIDM — Governed Event Record Identity

→ ERSTM — Governed Event Record Structure

→ ERLKM — Governed Event Record Linkage State

→ ERTM — Governed Event Record Traceability State

→ ERINM — Governed Event Record Interoperability

→ Governed Tax-Relevant Event Record

This allows organizations to determine not only what the current
Record says, but how that governed Record reached its present
state. Use Case 1 — Record Reconsidered After New Participation
Information

A cross-border digital Event has already produced a Governed
Tax-Relevant Event Record.

Later, new governed Participation information causes TREDS to
reconsider the original Determination.

EDREM produces a new effective Determination.

ERTM preserves: original Determination; reconsideration
reference; prior Record state; resulting Determination; updated
Record state; and the transition basis.

A downstream tax engine can consume the current state without
losing the history of how that state was reached. Use Case 2 —
Autonomous Platform Migrated Across Three Technical Systems

An autonomous platform creates one governed Event Record.

Over several years, the Record is migrated from:

System A

↓

System B

↓

System C

The technical schema, local identifiers, and storage model
change.

The canonical Record identity remains continuous.

ERTM preserves the relevant transformation lineage and allows the
Record to be reconstructed across all three environments.

The system can therefore demonstrate that the current Record is
the continued governed representation of the original Determined
Event rather than an unrelated new Event.

Canonical Boundary

ERTM owns the governance of Record lineage, Event-to-Record
lineage, Determination lineage, governance-state lineage,
materially relevant state transitions, prior-state preservation,
historical reconstruction,
qualification/uncertainty/reconsideration/correction/supersession
traceability, transformation lineage, version lineage, trace
gaps, ambiguity, conflict, trace continuity, reconstruction
sufficiency, and establishment of the Governed Event Record
Traceability State.

ERTM does not establish Record identity; ERIDM owns
identification.

It does not define Record Structure; ERSTM owns structure.

It does not establish material Record links; ERLKM owns linkage.

It does not govern semantic preservation across systems; ERINM
owns interoperability.

It does not perform general evidence or audit governance; ADIT®
remains authoritative.

It does not govern the substantive Event lifecycle; TRECLS owns
lifecycle and continuity governance.

It does not determine legal taxability, Tax Jurisdiction,
taxpayer status, Tax Base, Tax Rate, legal tax point, Tax Period,
Tax Liability, filing obligation, or other jurisdiction-specific
tax consequences.

Its governance begins when a valid Governed Event Record Linkage
State exists.

Its governance ends when the materially relevant history and
present state of the Record are sufficiently reconstructable and
the Governed Event Record Traceability State is ready for
Interoperability Governance.
