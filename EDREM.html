Canonical Definition

The Event Determination Reconsideration Module (EDREM) governs
when and how an existing Tax-Relevant Event Determination is
reopened for formal review after materially relevant new
information, corrected governance, changed Event structure,
resolved uncertainty, new conflict, superseding upstream output,
or another legitimate reconsideration trigger emerges.

EDREM governs the transition from an existing Determination state
to a reviewed Determination state while preserving the complete
history of the prior Decision.

Its principal transition is:

Governed Event Determination Decision + Reconsideration Trigger

↓

Reconsideration Governance

↓

Confirmed, Amended, Replaced,
Deferred, Rejected, or Superseded Determination State

EDREM does not silently rewrite the prior Decision.

Its foundational distinction is:

Determination Reconsideration ≠ Historical Erasure.

Operational Role

Tax-Relevant Event Determination occurs at a specific governance
state and time.

Operational reality may later change or become better understood.

Examples include: corrected Event Formation; revised Event
Boundary; new participant information; corrected Value
Association; resolved Event Relationship conflict; new Merge or
Split result; newly available evidence; resolved uncertainty;
discovery of duplicate Event representation; or material error in
the prior Determination basis.

EDREM provides the controlled mechanism for reopening the
existing Determination without destroying its historical validity
as a prior governance state.

The core question is:

Does new or changed governance materially affect the existing
Determination, and if so, what governed Determination state
should replace or qualify it?

Operational Scope

EDREM applies to reconsideration of Determination Decisions
arising from: commerce; marketplaces; payments; banking;
accounting; ERP systems; logistics; supply chains; digital
platforms; digital assets; blockchain systems; smart contracts;

Its governance logic remains technology-neutral and
jurisdiction-neutral.

Reconsideration Object

The Reconsideration Object is an existing Governed Event
Determination Decision whose continued validity has become
materially reviewable.

The Reconsideration Object shall include: the existing
Determination Decision; its Decision basis; relevant
qualifications; relevant uncertainty state; the new or changed
governance information; the reconsideration trigger.

Existing Decision ≠ Immutable Decision.

Reconsideration Trigger

A Reconsideration Trigger is a materially relevant change or
discovery capable of affecting the validity, qualification, or
status of an existing Determination.

Potential triggers include: corrected upstream governance; newly
available information; resolved uncertainty; newly discovered
uncertainty; resolved conflict; newly discovered conflict; Event
Merge; Event Split; Event correction; duplicate-event discovery;
superseded source; materially changed Event Relationship;
materially changed Value Association; materially changed
Participation; or identified Decision error.

New Information ≠ Automatic Reconsideration Trigger.

The information must be materially relevant to the existing
Determination.

Trigger Legitimacy

EDREM shall determine whether the reconsideration trigger is
methodologically legitimate.

A Decision shall not be reopened solely because: a different tax
result is desired; reporting becomes inconvenient; commercial
preference changes; a jurisdiction produces an unfavorable legal
interpretation; a technical system wishes to rewrite history.

Preferred Tax Outcome ≠ Reconsideration Basis.

Trigger Materiality

A trigger is material where it could alter: Event Identity; Event
Formation; Event Boundary; Determination Conditions;
Determination Decision; Determination qualification; Event count;
Participation structure; Value Association; Event Relationships;
or downstream usability.

Changed Information ≠ Material Change by default.

Reconsideration Initiation

Reconsideration shall begin only after a legitimate material
trigger is established.

The prior Decision remains the historical Decision until the
reconsideration process produces a governed result.

Reconsideration Initiation ≠ Prior Decision Cancellation.

Reconsideration Scope

EDREM shall define the scope of the reconsideration.

The review may affect: one Decision qualification; one governance
dependency; one Decision basis element; the complete
Determination Decision; or several related Event Determinations.

Scope should be no broader than materially necessary.

Local Change ≠ Full Determination Reopening by default.

Decision-Basis Change

Where the basis supporting a prior Decision changes materially,
EDREM shall assess whether that change affects the Decision.

A changed basis does not automatically require a changed outcome.

Changed Basis ≠ Changed Decision by default.

Upstream Governance Change

An upstream Parent Standard may produce a corrected or superseded
governance result.

Examples include: revised Formation; revised Boundary; corrected
Timing; corrected Location; changed Participation; corrected
Value Association; revised Relationship structure.

EDREM evaluates the effect on Determination.

It does not redo the upstream governance.

Formation Reconsideration Impact

A materially changed Event Formation may require complete
Determination reconsideration.

Changed Formation ≠ Automatic Continuity of Prior Determination.

TREFS remains authoritative for Formation.

Boundary Reconsideration Impact

If Event Boundary changes materially, the prior Determination may
no longer apply to the same Event structure.

Changed Boundary ≠ Same Determination by default.

TREBS remains authoritative.

Timing Reconsideration Impact

A corrected Event Time may or may not affect the Determination.

EDREM evaluates material impact.

It does not determine legal tax timing.

Corrected Event Time ≠ Legal Tax Point Reconsideration.

Location Reconsideration Impact

Changed Location information may affect Event Determination
qualification.

EDREM does not determine Tax Jurisdiction.

Location Reconsideration ≠ Jurisdiction Reconsideration.

Participation Reconsideration Impact

A corrected Participant structure may materially affect the
Determination.

EDREM evaluates Determination impact only.

Participant Reconsideration ≠ Taxpayer Reconsideration.

Value Association Reconsideration Impact

Corrected Value Association may require Determination
reconsideration.

EDREM does not determine taxable value or Tax Base.

Value Reconsideration ≠ Tax Base Recalculation.

Relationship Reconsideration Impact

Corrected Event Relationships may affect whether prior
Determination remains valid.

Examples include: newly established dependency; changed sequence;
revised grouping; Event Merge; Event Split.

TRERLS remains authoritative for the relationship structure.

Merge Impact

Where multiple previously determined Events are governed into one
Event through ERLMM, the prior Determination states shall not
simply collapse into the new Event.

The resulting Event requires appropriate reconsideration.

Merged Determinations ≠ One Determination by automatic
inheritance.

Split Impact

Where one previously determined Event is split into multiple
governed Events, the prior Decision does not automatically
transfer to each resulting Event.

One Prior Determination ≠ Multiple Valid Determinations after
Split.

Duplicate-Event Discovery

A previously determined Event may later be identified as a
duplicate representation of another governed Event.

EDREM may supersede or reject the duplicate Determination while
preserving its historical record.

Duplicate Determination Correction ≠ Historical Deletion.

Uncertainty Resolution Impact

EDUM may resolve uncertainty that previously qualified or
deferred a Decision.

EDREM evaluates whether that resolution justifies: confirmation;
removal of qualification; amendment; or replacement.

Resolved Uncertainty ≠ Automatic Positive Determination.

New Uncertainty Impact

New material uncertainty may emerge after Determination.

EDREM may reopen the Decision where continued reliance is no
longer methodologically justified.

Conflict Resolution Impact

A conflict may later be resolved.

EDREM assesses whether the resolution changes the prior
Determination.

Conflict Resolution ≠ Automatic Decision Change.

New Conflict Impact

A new materially authoritative conflict may undermine a
previously stable Decision.

EDREM may require reconsideration, qualification, deferral, or
upstream referral.

Known Error

If the prior Determination is known to contain a substantive
error, EDREM shall distinguish simple correction from full
reconsideration.

A minor clerical error may not require reopening the Decision.

A material governance error may.

Clerical Correction ≠ Full Reconsideration by default.

Reconsideration Qualification

Reconsideration may produce a Determination that remains valid
but now carries a new qualification.

Any qualification shall remain explicit and traceable.

Determination Confirmation

The prior Determination may be Confirmed where the new
information does not materially alter its basis or outcome.

Confirmation should be explicit.

Reconsidered and Confirmed ≠ Never Reconsidered.

Confirmation with Qualification

The prior Determination may remain valid while requiring a new or
revised qualification.

This creates:

Determination Confirmed with Qualification

rather than silently altering the original Decision.

Determination Amendment

A Determination may be Amended where part of the Decision state
or qualification changes without replacing the entire
Determination.

The amendment shall preserve the prior state.

Amendment ≠ Historical Replacement.

Determination Replacement

A Determination may require replacement where the new governance
state materially changes the correct Determination outcome.

The new Decision shall reference the replaced Decision.

Replacement ≠ Erasure.

Determination Deferral after
Reconsideration

A previously positive or conditional Decision may become Deferred
where new information creates a material unresolved governance
issue.

Prior Positive Determination ≠ Permanent Positive Determination.

Determination Rejection after
Reconsideration

A previously determined Event may ultimately be rejected if new
governance establishes that the Event representation was invalid.

For example: duplicate Event; invalid Formation; invalid
standalone Event after Merge; technical operation incorrectly
treated as independent Event.

Reconsidered Rejection ≠ Legal Non-Taxability.

Determination Supersession

A new Determination may supersede a prior Decision.

Supersession shall preserve: prior Decision; effective sequence;
reason for supersession; new Decision; traceability between them.

Supersession ≠ Deletion.

Reconsideration Inconclusive

Where reconsideration cannot establish a sufficiently governed
outcome, the state may remain Inconclusive.

Further upstream governance or uncertainty resolution may be
required.

Reconsideration ≠ Mandatory Definitive Outcome.

Prior-State Preservation

EDREM shall preserve every materially relevant prior
Determination state.

This enables reconstruction of:

What was determined

Effective-State Management

EDREM shall distinguish: historical Decision state; current
effective Determination; pending reconsideration state;
superseded Determination.

Historical State ≠ Current Effective State.

Reconsideration Timing

The time of reconsideration shall be distinct from: Event Time;
original Decision Time; correction time; legal tax point.

Reconsideration Time ≠ Event Time.

Repeated reconsiderations may occur.

For example:

First Reconsideration

↓

Amended
Determination

Second Reconsideration

↓

Each material state shall remain traceable.

Reconsideration Frequency

EDREM shall not reopen Determination unnecessarily.

Repeated reconsideration without material trigger may create
governance instability.

Frequent Review ≠ Stronger Governance by default.

Reconsideration Stability

After reconsideration, the new Determination should achieve
sufficient operational stability for downstream use.

Operational stability does not eliminate the possibility of later
reconsideration.

Downstream Reliance Impact

Where downstream systems have already consumed the prior
Determination, EDREM should produce a state indicating whether
downstream reliance may be affected.

Possible states include: No Downstream Impact; Qualification
Update Required; Determination Update Required; Prior
Determination Withdrawal Required; Downstream Re-evaluation
Required.

EDREM does not perform jurisdiction-specific tax recalculation.

Downstream Notification

A material reconsideration outcome should be capable of notifying
downstream consumers that the effective TREGA™ Determination has
changed.

The handoff should identify: prior Decision; new Decision;
effective state; reason for change; relevant qualification.

Cross-System Reconsideration

The same Event may exist across multiple technical systems.

A reconsideration shall govern the Event Determination itself
rather than create independent contradictory Determinations per
system.

System Record Update ≠ Event Determination Reconsideration by
default.

Cross-Organization Reconsideration

Several organizations may consume the same governed Event.

EDREM may support distribution of the revised Determination state
without deciding each organization's legal tax treatment.

Cross-Border Reconsideration

New cross-border information may affect Location, Participation,
Value Association, or Event Relationships.

EDREM may reconsider the Event Determination.

It does not reconsider Tax Jurisdiction itself.

Cross-Border Determination Reconsideration ≠ Tax Jurisdiction
Reallocation.

Multi-Jurisdiction Continuity

A reconsidered TREGA™ Event may continue to support multiple
jurisdictional evaluations.

The model becomes:

↓

Multiple Updated Jurisdictional
Evaluations

Each jurisdiction remains responsible for its own legal

Blockchain Reconsideration

Immutable ledger records do not make Event Determinations
immutable.

Off-chain information may reveal: incorrect participant
attribution; invalid Event Formation; duplicate Event
representation; changed relationship interpretation.

Immutable Ledger ≠ Immutable Event Determination.

Smart-Contract Reconsideration

A smart contract may execute exactly as coded while the governed
Event interpretation later changes.

Immutable Execution ≠ Immutable Determination.

AI-Agent Reconsideration

AI-agent Events may require reconsideration where: agent identity
mapping changes; execution role is corrected; newly available
logs alter Event Formation; Value Association changes;
relationship governance is revised.

Agent Action History ≠ Determination Finality.

Automated Reconsideration

EDREM may be automated.

Automation shall not permit silent rewriting of prior
Determinations.

An automated implementation shall preserve: trigger; prior
Decision; new governance basis; scope; outcome; effective state;
traceability.

Automated Reconsideration ≠ Automatic Historical Rewrite.

Reconsideration and Readiness

A material reconsideration may require return to EDRDM where the
revised Event no longer satisfies readiness requirements.

EDREM shall not perform EDRDM functions itself.

Reconsideration ≠ Readiness Reassessment by default.

Reconsideration and Conditions

Changed Event governance may require EDCM conditions to be
reevaluated.

EDREM may trigger that process but does not own condition
evaluation.

Reconsideration ≠ Condition Re-evaluation Authority.

Reconsideration and Decision

EDDM owns the formal Determination Decision logic.

EDREM owns governance of reopening and transitioning from a prior
Decision to a revised governed Decision state.

Initial Decision ≠ Reconsideration Decision Process.

Reconsideration and Uncertainty

EDUM may produce a Reconsideration Required state.

EDREM then governs the reconsideration process.

Uncertainty Trigger ≠ Reconsideration Outcome.

Reconsideration and Lifecycle Governance

EDREM governs reconsideration within TREDS.

Once the Determination is finalized, TREGS governs the Event
Record and TRECLS governs longer-term Event continuity,
amendment, correction, supersession, and closure.

Determination Reconsideration ≠ Event Lifecycle Governance.

Reconsideration and Taxability

A tax authority or tax engine may later reinterpret the legal
treatment of an Event.

That does not automatically require TREGA™ reconsideration unless
the underlying Event governance changes.

Changed Tax Treatment ≠ Event Determination Reconsideration by
default.

Reconsideration and Tax Jurisdiction

A jurisdiction may change its legal nexus or sourcing rules.

That legal change does not itself alter TREGA™ Event
Determination.

Tax Jurisdiction Change ≠ Event Determination Change by default.

Reconsideration and Taxpayer

A legal conclusion about taxpayer identity does not redefine
TREGA™ Participation unless new operational facts emerge.

Taxpayer Reclassification ≠ Participation Reconsideration by
default.

Reconsideration and Tax Base

A later Tax Base adjustment does not automatically alter Event
Determination.

Tax Base Adjustment ≠ Event Determination Reconsideration by
default.

Established Governance Requirements

EDREM implementations shall ensure that: reconsideration begins
only from an existing Determination; triggers are distinguishable
from ordinary new information; triggers are evaluated for
legitimacy and materiality; preferred tax outcome is not a valid
reconsideration basis; reconsideration initiation does not cancel
the prior historical Decision; reconsideration scope is bounded
to materially affected areas; changed upstream governance remains
governed by its authoritative Parent Standard; Merge and Split do
not automatically transfer prior Determinations; duplicate-event
correction preserves history; resolved uncertainty does not
automatically produce Positive Determination; newly material
uncertainty can reopen a Decision; conflict resolution does not
automatically require Decision change; clerical correction
remains distinct from full reconsideration; confirmation,
amendment, replacement, deferral, rejection, and supersession
remain distinct states; prior Decision history is preserved;
current effective state remains distinguishable from historical
state; reconsideration time remains distinct from Event Time;
repeated reconsideration requires material basis; downstream
impact can be represented; downstream consumers can receive
updated Determination state; immutable technical infrastructure
does not make Event Determination immutable; automated
reconsideration does not silently rewrite history; EDRDM, EDCM,
EDDM, and EDUM ownership boundaries remain preserved; tax-law
changes do not automatically trigger TREGA™ reconsideration; and
the final reconsidered Determination is suitable for handoff to
TREGS.

Governance Logic

IF no existing Governed Event Determination Decision exists, THEN
EDREM shall not initiate reconsideration.

IF new information is not materially relevant to the existing
Determination, THEN reconsideration shall not be required.

IF a proposed trigger exists only because a different tax outcome
is preferred, THEN the trigger shall be rejected.

IF a material upstream governance change could alter the
Determination basis, THEN EDREM shall initiate or require
reconsideration.

IF the new information confirms the original Decision basis, THEN
the Determination may be Confirmed.

IF the original Decision remains valid but requires a bounded
qualification, THEN the Determination may be Confirmed with
Qualification.

IF part of the Decision must change while the Determination
remains substantially continuous, THEN the Determination may be
Amended.

IF the original Decision no longer represents the governed Event
correctly, THEN a Replacement Determination shall be established.

IF material governance remains unresolved, THEN the reconsidered
Determination may become Deferred.

IF new governance establishes that the apparent Event should not
have been determined as a standalone Tax-Relevant Event, THEN the
Determination may be Rejected or Superseded as appropriate.

IF a Merge or Split materially changes Event structure, THEN
prior Determinations shall not transfer automatically.

IF reconsideration cannot establish a sufficiently governed
outcome, THEN the state shall remain Inconclusive or Further
Governance Required.

IF a new Determination supersedes a prior Decision, THEN the
historical Decision shall remain traceable.

IF downstream systems relied on the prior Decision and the
reconsidered outcome materially changes it, THEN Downstream
Update Required shall be established.

IF reconsideration is completed, THEN EDREM shall establish the
Final TREDS Determination State for TREGS.

Enterprise Application

EDREM provides the controlled mechanism for maintaining
Determination integrity when reality, evidence, or upstream
governance changes after a Decision has already been issued.

The canonical operational path is:

EDDM — Governed Event Determination
Decision

→ EDUM — Governed Determination Uncertainty State

→ EDREM — Final TREDS Determination State

→ TREGS — Governed Tax-Relevant Event Record

This allows organizations to correct Determination without
destroying historical governance or confusing Event
reconsideration with retroactive tax-law reinterpretation. Use
Case 1 — Marketplace Event Reconsidered After Event Merge

A marketplace originally governs two separate Events and EDDM
issues a Positive Determination for each.

Later, additional operational evidence establishes that the two
apparent Events were technical fragments of one economic Event.

TRERLS and ERLMM govern the Merge and create a revised Event
structure.

EDREM identifies the Merge as a material reconsideration trigger.

The two earlier Determinations are preserved historically and
superseded.

The merged Event is routed through the necessary TREDS governance
and receives a new effective Determination state.

The prior Determinations are not deleted and are not
automatically inherited by the merged Event. Use Case 2 —
Autonomous Service Determination Confirmed After Uncertainty
Resolution

An autonomous service Event received a Conditional Determination
because the identity of one machine participant remained
uncertain.

EDUM preserved the uncertainty and triggered reconsideration once
new authenticated execution records became available.

TREPS updates the governed Participation state.

EDREM evaluates the change and determines that the new
information confirms the original Event structure while removing
the prior qualification.

The Determination becomes Confirmed without the previous
Participation qualification.

The earlier Conditional Determination remains preserved in the
Event's governance history.

Canonical Boundary

EDREM owns the governance of Determination reconsideration
triggers, trigger legitimacy, materiality, reconsideration
initiation, scope, upstream-change impact, uncertainty-resolution
impact, conflict-resolution impact, correction impact,
Merge/Split impact, confirmation, qualification, amendment,
replacement, deferral, rejection, supersession, prior-state
preservation, effective-state management, downstream impact,
downstream update requirements, reconsideration traceability, and
establishment of the Final TREDS Determination State.

EDREM does not perform Event Context, Recognition, Formation,
Boundary, Timing, Location, Participation, Value Association, or
Relationship governance.

It does not independently perform Determination Readiness,
Determination Conditions, initial Determination Decision, or
Determination Uncertainty governance.

It does not govern the broader Event lifecycle after TREDS; TREGS
and TRECLS remain authoritative for Event Record Governance and
Event Lifecycle & Continuity.

It does not determine legal taxability, Tax Jurisdiction,
taxpayer status, Tax Base, Tax Rate, legal tax point, Tax Period,
exemption, deduction, Tax Liability, filing obligation,
collection, enforcement, or any other jurisdiction-specific legal
tax consequence.

Its governance begins when a materially legitimate trigger places
an existing Determination under reconsideration.

Its governance ends when the effective Final TREDS Determination
State is established and ready for handoff to TREGS.

Canonical Closing Statement

EDREM ensures that a Tax-Relevant Event Determination can evolve
when governed reality changes without allowing the history of
prior decisions to disappear.

A Determination may be confirmed, amended, replaced, deferred,
rejected, or superseded — but every material transition must
remain reconstructable.

Determination Reconsideration ≠ Historical Erasure.

Supersession ≠ Deletion.

Changed Tax Treatment ≠ Event Determination Reconsideration by
default.
