Location What This Standard Governs

The Tax-Relevant Event Location Standard (TRELS) governs how a
Tax-Relevant Event is spatially situated after its operational
timing has been governed.

Modern events do not always occur at one obvious place. A
physical transaction may involve several operational locations. A
digital service may involve a user, platform, infrastructure,
processing environment, and service execution distributed across
different places. Autonomous systems, cloud infrastructure,
digital assets, machine-to-machine operations, and distributed
platforms can create events whose relevant operational location
cannot legitimately be represented by a single address, server
location, or participant location.

TRELS establishes the governance structure required to identify,
distinguish, correlate, qualify, and preserve the operational
locations materially associated with the event.

Its purpose is not to decide which country, territory,
municipality, or other legal jurisdiction has taxation authority
over the event.

Event Location ≠ Tax Jurisdiction.

TRELS governs the operational spatial reality of the event.
Jurisdiction-specific tax systems subsequently determine what
that spatial reality means under applicable law.

Position Within TREGA™

TRELS is the sixth Parent Standard within TREGA™.

It receives a Governed Temporally Situated Event from the
Tax-Relevant Event Timing Standard (TRETS).

TRELS then establishes the event's governed spatial
representation before the event proceeds to the Tax-Relevant
Event Participation Standard (TREPS).

TRELS therefore operates before jurisdiction-specific taxation
rules are applied.

It answers:

Where did the governed event occur operationally?

It does not answer:

Which jurisdiction has the legal right to tax the event?

Why Event Location Requires Governance

Traditional systems often represent location through a single
available data point: billing address, IP address, merchant
address, device location, company registration address, server
location, delivery destination, or infrastructure location.

In complex economic and digital environments, none of these
should automatically become the authoritative location of the
event.

A governed event may contain multiple legitimate spatial signals
representing different operational dimensions.

TRELS preserves those distinctions rather than forcing them
prematurely into a single jurisdictional conclusion.

Available Location Data ≠ Governed Event Location by Default.

Participant Location ≠ Event Location by Default.

Server Location ≠ Event Location by Default.

Event Location ≠ Tax Jurisdiction.
Physical, Digital, and Distributed Events

TRELS is designed for both conventional and emerging economic
environments.

It can govern spatial representation for physical commerce,
logistics, manufacturing, services, digital platforms, cloud
infrastructure, distributed systems, autonomous agents, robotics,
digital assets, machine-to-machine transactions, and other
environments in which economic activity may span multiple
operational locations.

Where an event genuinely spans several locations, TRELS does not
require artificial compression into one location.

A distributed event may legitimately remain distributed.

Distributed Event ≠ Single Location by Default.

This allows downstream systems to receive a governed
representation of operational reality rather than a location
selected primarily to satisfy a later legal or administrative
rule.

Separation From Tax Jurisdiction

The distinction between operational location and legal
jurisdiction is foundational to TREGA™.

TRELS may establish that an event involves operationally relevant
locations in several countries, infrastructure environments,
digital environments, or physical facilities.

It does not determine whether any of those locations establishes
tax residence, permanent establishment, source jurisdiction,
place of supply, taxable nexus, customs jurisdiction, VAT
jurisdiction, corporate tax jurisdiction, or another legally
defined connection.

Those determinations belong downstream.

Relationship to Other OOF® Architectures

TRELS governs Event Location only within the Tax-Relevant Event
lifecycle.

ORA™ may establish what occurred in operational reality.

OBIDENITY® may govern the identity, origin, ownership, authority,
and provenance of relevant actors or objects.

ADIT® may govern evidence and auditability associated with
location information.

VALIDOS™ may govern validation of relevant location claims or
evidence.

INTEGROS® may govern the integrity of location information.

TREGA™, through TRELS, owns the governed spatial representation
of the Tax-Relevant Event itself.

These architecture boundaries prevent Event Location from
silently absorbing identity, evidence, validation, integrity, or
jurisdictional tax functions. Use Case 1 — Cross-Border Digital
Service

A customer located in one country purchases a digital service
from a platform operator established in another country.

The service executes through distributed cloud infrastructure
spanning several technical regions. Payment processing occurs
through another country, account registration contains a separate
address, and the user's device produces an additional location
signal.

A conventional system may attempt to select one of these
locations immediately as the location of the transaction.

TRELS instead governs the operational location structure.

It distinguishes the customer location, platform location,
service execution location, infrastructure location,
payment-processing location, and other materially relevant
spatial signals and preserves their relationships to the event.

The resulting event can then be passed downstream for
jurisdiction-specific evaluation.

TRELS does not decide which country may impose VAT, sales tax,
corporate tax, or another tax.

Case 2 — Autonomous Cross-Border Machine Economy

An autonomous industrial system detects demand, initiates
production, coordinates machinery, obtains digital services,
exchanges machine-generated instructions, and transfers output
through infrastructure distributed across several locations.

The AI scheduler may operate in one environment, production
machinery in another, cloud computation elsewhere, the
controlling organization in another country, and the receiving
machine or customer in another location.

Thousands of location-bearing technical signals may exist.

TRELS prevents arbitrary selection of a server address, corporate
address, device location, or machine location as the
authoritative event location.

Instead, it establishes which spatial signals materially describe
the bounded event, how they relate to one another, and whether
the event should legitimately be represented as physically
situated, digitally situated, distributed, or spatially
qualified.

The governed result can subsequently be evaluated by multiple
taxation systems without requiring TREGA™ to determine which
jurisdiction has taxation authority.

Machine-generated location data must describe operational reality
before tax law determines jurisdictional consequence.

Canonical Boundary

TRELS governs where the Tax-Relevant Event occurred
operationally.

It does not determine Tax Jurisdiction, tax nexus, tax residence,
place of supply, permanent establishment, source rules, legal
taxability, tax liability, tax rates, tax periods, filing
obligations, or other jurisdiction-specific tax consequences.

Its output is a governed spatial representation of the event
suitable for subsequent Event Participation governance and
eventual jurisdiction-specific taxation analysis.

TRELS governs the location of the event — not the legal authority
that may arise from that location.

TREGA™ governs the event. The jurisdiction governs the tax.
