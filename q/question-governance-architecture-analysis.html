Governance Architecture Analysis™ Question Index

OOF Problem Space Intelligence Index™ (PSII™)

Analyzing Governance Structures, Dependencies, Gaps, Readiness, and
Operational Governance Conditions

Organizations often invest heavily in technology, processes,
compliance, and operations.

However, many failures originate not from technology itself, but
from governance weaknesses.

Governance Architecture Analysis™ exists to help organizations
understand how governance operates, where governance gaps exist, how
authority flows, where accountability resides, and whether
governance structures are capable of supporting
operational objectives.

This Question Index™ provides a structured collection of questions
designed to support governance assessment, governance mapping,
governance readiness evaluation, and governance
architecture analysis.

The objective is not merely to evaluate governance.

The objective is to understand whether governance is capable of
producing reliable outcomes.

Governance Structure

documented? Is governance consistently applied? Is governance
understandable? Can governance relationships be identified? Are
governance layers visible? Are governance boundaries defined? Is
governance architecture coherent? Does governance support
operational objectives? Can governance be independently reviewed?

Does governance align with organizational goals? Are governance
outcomes measurable? Does governance reduce risk? Does governance
improve accountability? Does governance support decision-making?
Does governance support operational stability? Is governance purpose
clearly communicated? Is governance fulfilling its intended role?

delegated? Is authority traceable? Are authority conflicts present?
Are authority limitations defined? Can authority decisions be

About the OOF Problem Space Intelligence Index™

The OOF Problem Space Intelligence Index™ (PSII™) is a growing
collection of structured questions designed to help identify,
understand, classify, and investigate problems before searching
for solutions.

Questions are organized into categories, clusters, and problem
environments to improve clarity, analysis, governance,
and decision-making.

The objective is not merely to provide answers.

The objective is to ensure that the right questions are being asked.
