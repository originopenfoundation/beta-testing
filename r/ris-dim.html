Canonical Definition

Delegation Integrity Module (DIM) defines the governance conditions
under which authority, responsibility, execution, and operational
control remain preserved whenever activities are delegated between
humans, AI systems, autonomous agents, software components,
organizations, or operational environments.

DIM governs delegation integrity.

The module establishes the methodological conditions required to ensure
that delegated execution never results in the loss of governance,
accountability, operational visibility, or traceability.

Delegation does not transfer governance.

Delegation extends governance.

DIM governs that continuity.

Module Operational Role

DIM defines the delegation governance layer of Runtime Integrity
Standard™.

Its role is to preserve governance continuity whenever operational
authority or execution is delegated between operational actors.

Module Operational Space

DIM governs:
  • delegation integrity
  • delegated execution
  • delegated authority
  • delegated responsibility
  • delegation traceability
  • delegation governance
  • execution continuity
  • governance continuity


The module applies wherever execution passes from one operational actor,
system, agent, organization, or component to another.

Module Function

The module applies wherever organizations must preserve:
  • governed delegation
  • continuous accountability
  • operational visibility
  • execution traceability
  • governance continuity
  • reconstructable delegation history


Its function is to ensure that every delegated activity remains
independently reconstructable, attributable, and governance-valid
throughout the complete delegation lifecycle.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Delegation Object

Identify which authorities, responsibilities, decisions, actions, or
operational activities may be delegated.

2. Define Delegation Integrity Conditions

The organization shall define:
  • delegation authority
  • delegation boundaries
  • responsibility ownership
  • execution conditions
  • governance evidence
  • delegation traceability


3. Define Delegation Degradation Detection Logic

The governance methodology shall identify:
  • unauthorized delegation
  • undefined responsibility
  • broken delegation chains
  • missing delegation evidence
  • uncontrolled delegated execution
  • governance-invalid delegation


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

Governance response may include:
  • delegation validation
  • execution suspension
  • authority reassessment
  • governance escalation
  • operational correction
  • delegation recovery


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The organization shall preserve reconstructable traceability of:
  • delegation origin
  • delegated authority
  • responsibility ownership
  • execution history
  • governance interventions
  • resulting operational outcomes


Operational integrity shall not be considered valid if materially
significant delegation activities cannot be reconstructed, reviewed,
attributed, or governed.

Use Case 1 — Multi-Agent AI System

Scenario
A primary AI agent delegates complex operational tasks to multiple
specialized AI agents.

Application
DIM ensures that delegated authority, execution, responsibility, and
operational outcomes remain continuously traceable and governance-valid.

Result
The organization preserves accountability, governance continuity, and
operational trust across the complete multi-agent execution chain.

Use Case 2 — Enterprise Workflow Delegation

Scenario
A critical operational process is delegated across multiple departments,
software systems, and external service providers.

Application
DIM provides the methodological governance framework required to
preserve continuous responsibility, execution visibility, and delegation
traceability.

Result
The organization reduces governance gaps, strengthens accountability,
and improves confidence in delegated operational execution.

Canonical Closing Statement

Governance is never transferred through delegation; it survives only
when every delegated action remains visible, attributable, and
continuously governed.
