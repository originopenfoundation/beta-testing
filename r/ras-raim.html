Canonical Definition

Relationship Accountability Integrity Module (RAIM) defines the
structural conditions under which relationships remain attributable,
accountable, evidence-supported, governable, and operationally valid
throughout their lifecycle.

RAIM governs relationship accountability.

The module establishes the conditions required to determine who
created, maintained, modified, governed, supervised, benefited from,
or remained accountable for a relationship throughout
its operational existence.

Module Operational Role

RAIM serves as the accountability layer of the
Relationship Accountability Standard.

Its role is to connect relationships to accountability.

RAIM answers the question:

Who remains accountable for the existence, governance, and
consequences of the relationship?

Module Operational Space

RAIM governs:

  • relationship accountability
  • relationship ownership
  • relationship governance accountability
  • relationship supervision accountability
  • relationship consequence attribution
  • accountability traceability
  • accountability preservation


The module applies wherever governance must determine accountability
connected to relationships.

Module Function

The module ensures that governance can determine:

  • who established the relationship
  • who approved the relationship
  • who maintained the relationship
  • who supervised the relationship
  • who benefited from the relationship
  • who remained accountable for the relationship
  • who remains accountable for consequences arising from the relationship


Its function is to prevent accountability gaps at the
relationship layer.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Relationship Accountability Scope

Determine which relationships require accountability analysis.

Examples:

  • employment relationships
  • contractor relationships
  • subcontractor relationships
  • supplier relationships
  • Human-AI relationships
  • multi-agent relationships


2. Identify Relationship Governance Actors

Identify actors responsible for creating, maintaining, governing,
supervising, or approving the relationship.

Examples:

  • employers
  • managers
  • contractors
  • organizational leaders
  • AI system owners
  • governance authorities


3. Map Relationship Accountability

Determine how accountability is distributed across the
relationship structure.

Governance must identify:

  • primary accountability
  • shared accountability
  • delegated accountability
  • retained accountability
  • unresolved accountability


4. Validate Accountability Integrity

Determine whether accountability remains identifiable
and operationally valid.

Governance must identify:

  • accountability gaps
  • accountability fragmentation
  • hidden accountability
  • disputed accountability
  • abandoned accountability


5. Preserve Accountability Traceability

Maintain sufficient evidence to reconstruct accountability
throughout the relationship lifecycle.

Use Case 1 — Agency Employment Structure

Scenario

A worker is formally employed by an agency but performs work under
the operational direction of another company.

Application

RAIM determines who remains accountable for maintaining and
governing the relationship structure.

Result

Governance gains visibility into accountability beyond
formal contractual arrangements.

Use Case 2 — Orchestrated Agent Environment

Scenario

Multiple AI agents operate through a centralized orchestration layer
under organizational supervision.

Application

RAIM determines accountability for creating, governing, and
maintaining operational relationships between participating systems.

Result

Governance gains visibility into accountability across
the relationship structure.

Canonical Closing Statement

Relationship Accountability Integrity Module (RAIM) defines the
structural conditions under which relationships remain attributable,
accountable, evidence-supported, governable, and operationally valid
throughout their lifecycle.

A relationship may create authority, responsibility, control, and
consequence. Governance therefore requires accountability not only
for actions, but also for the relationships that make those
actions possible.
