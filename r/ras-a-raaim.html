Canonical Definition

Responsibility Assignment Accountability Integrity Module (RAAIM)
defines the structural conditions under which responsibility
assignments, assignment decisions, assignment transfers, assignment
approvals, assignment acceptances, assignment outcomes, and
assignment-related governance activities remain attributable,
accountable, traceable, governable, reconstructable, enforceable,
and operationally valid throughout the
responsibility-assignment lifecycle.

RAAIM governs responsibility-assignment accountability.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine who assigned responsibility, who approved the assignment,
who accepted the assignment, who modified the assignment, and who
remains accountable when responsibility-assignment failures occur.

Module Operational Role

RAAIM defines the assignment-accountability governance layer
of RAS-A.

Its role is to ensure that responsibility assignments remain
connected to accountable governance actors throughout the
assignment lifecycle.

Module Operational Space

RAAIM governs:

  • assignment accountability
  • assignment ownership
  • assignment-decision accountability
  • assignment-transfer accountability
  • assignment-failure accountability
  • governance accountability
  • assignment-governance accountability
  • consequence attribution preparation


The module applies wherever governance must determine accountability
arising from responsibility assignments.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • accountable assignment structures
  • attributable assignment decisions
  • reconstructable accountability history
  • traceable assignment outcomes
  • governance-valid accountability relationships
  • operational assignment enforcement


Its function is to ensure that governance can determine who remains
accountable for responsibility assignments and
assignment-related outcomes.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Assignment Accountability Object

The organization must define which assignment environments
require accountability governance.

This may include:

  • organizational structures
  • contractor networks
  • project environments
  • management systems
  • compliance environments
  • AI governance systems
  • Human-AI operational environments
  • governance frameworks


2. Define Assignment Accountability Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which assignment
accountability remains valid.

This includes:

  • attribution requirements
  • accountability requirements
  • enforcement requirements
  • consequence requirements
  • governance requirements
  • accountability-valid assignment conditions


3. Define Accountability Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how assignment-accountability failures
are identified.

This may include:

  • anonymous assignment decisions
  • undocumented assignment approvals
  • accountability gaps
  • assignment ownership ambiguity
  • governance-blind assignment activities
  • unverifiable assignment accountability


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic
for assignment-accountability failures.

Governance response may include:

  • accountability review
  • attribution verification
  • governance intervention
  • corrective actions
  • assignment reassessment
  • enforcement procedures
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • assignment decisions
  • assignment approvals
  • assignment acceptances
  • governance reviews
  • accountability relationships
  • resulting assignment outcomes


A responsibility-assignment environment must not remain
accountability-valid if materially significant assignment activities
cannot be attributed, reconstructed, reviewed, enforced,
validated, or governed.

Use Case 1 — Organizational Assignment Failure

Scenario

A critical responsibility is assigned to an unsuitable actor,
resulting in operational failure, safety issues, or
governance breakdown.

Application

RAAIM reconstructs who assigned the responsibility, who approved the
assignment, and who remains accountable for the assignment decision.

Result

Governance gains visibility into accountability
for responsibility-assignment failures.

Use Case 2 — Human-AI Assignment

Environment

Scenario

An orchestration platform allocates responsibilities across AI
agents and human supervisors, resulting in significant
operational outcomes.

Application

RAAIM reconstructs assignment authority, assignment approvals,
assignment ownership, and assignment-accountability relationships.

Result

Governance gains visibility into accountability for assignment
decisions across intelligent operational ecosystems.
