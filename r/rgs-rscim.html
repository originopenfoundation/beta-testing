Canonical Definition

Responsibility Scope Integrity Module (RSCIM) defines the structural
conditions under which the boundaries, obligations, duties,
expectations, limitations, operational requirements, and governed
activities associated with responsibility remain identifiable,
traceable, governable, accountable, reconstructable, and
operationally valid throughout the responsibility lifecycle.

RSCIM governs responsibility scope.

The module establishes the integrity conditions required to
determine exactly what a responsible actor was obligated to do, what
was outside the responsibility boundary, and where responsibility
began and ended.

Module Operational Role

RSCIM defines the responsibility-scope governance layer of RGS.

Its role is to preserve visibility of the obligations and boundaries
attached to responsibility assignments.

Module Operational Space

RSCIM governs:

  • responsibility scope
  • responsibility boundaries
  • responsibility obligations
  • responsibility duties
  • responsibility limitations
  • responsibility requirements
  • responsibility expectations
  • scope accountability


The module applies wherever governance requires clarification
of responsibility boundaries.

Module Function

The module applies wherever systems must preserve:

  • clearly defined obligations
  • visible responsibility boundaries
  • reconstructable responsibility scope
  • accountable duty assignment
  • governance-valid responsibility requirements
  • operational responsibility clarity


Its function is to ensure that governance can determine exactly what
a responsible actor was expected or required to do.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define the Responsibility Scope Object

The organization must define which responsibility environments
require scope governance.

This may include:

  • management structures
  • contractor environments
  • subcontractor networks
  • regulatory frameworks
  • operational systems
  • quality-control environments
  • AI governance systems
  • Human-AI operational systems


2. Define Responsibility Scope Conditions

The system must define the conditions under which responsibility
scope remains valid.

This includes:

  • obligation requirements
  • boundary requirements
  • limitation requirements
  • accountability requirements
  • governance requirements
  • scope-valid responsibility conditions


3. Define Scope Degradation Detection Logic

The system must define how responsibility-scope failures
are identified.

This may include:

  • undefined obligations
  • overlapping responsibilities
  • responsibility gaps
  • undocumented duties
  • governance-blind responsibility assignments
  • unclear responsibility boundaries


4. Define Operational Response or Governance Logic

The system must define governance logic for
responsibility-scope failures.

Governance response may include:

  • scope review
  • responsibility clarification
  • governance intervention
  • corrective actions
  • reassignment procedures
  • boundary verification
  • operational invalidation where required


5. Preserve Traceability & Restrict Invalid Conditions

The system must preserve reconstructable traceability of:

  • responsibility assignments
  • responsibility obligations
  • governance reviews
  • clarification procedures
  • intervention activities
  • resulting responsibility states


A responsibility-governance environment must not remain scope-valid
if materially significant responsibility boundaries cannot be
reconstructed, reviewed, validated, or governed.

Use Case 1 — Construction Contractor

Environment

Scenario

A contractor, subcontractor, and temporary worker all participate in
a construction project involving multiple operational tasks.

Application

RSCIM determines exactly which obligations belonged to each actor
and where responsibility boundaries existed.

Result

Governance gains visibility into responsibility scope across
the operational chain.

Use Case 2 — Human-AI Operational

Environment

Scenario

A human supervisor, orchestration platform, and AI agents jointly
participate in operational decision-making and execution.

Application

RSCIM determines which obligations belong to each actor and where
responsibility boundaries begin and end.

Result

Governance gains visibility into responsibility allocation across
intelligent operational systems.

Canonical Closing Statement

Responsibility Scope Integrity Module (RSCIM) defines the structural
conditions under which the boundaries, obligations, duties,
expectations, limitations, operational requirements, and governed
activities associated with responsibility remain identifiable,
traceable, governable, accountable, reconstructable, and

Responsibility cannot be fulfilled if responsibility boundaries are
unclear. Responsibility scope integrity therefore becomes a
foundational condition of trustworthy responsibility governance.
