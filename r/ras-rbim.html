Canonical Definition

Relationship Boundary Integrity Module (RBIM) defines the structural
conditions under which the boundaries, limits, scope, and governance
extent of relationships remain identifiable, distinguishable,
traceable, governable, and operationally valid throughout
the relationship lifecycle.

RBIM governs relationship boundaries.

The module establishes the conditions required to determine where a
relationship begins, where it ends, what it includes, what it
excludes, and how far governance obligations extend
across participating actors.

Module Operational Role

RBIM serves as the boundary-governance layer of the
Relationship Accountability Standard.

Its role is to define the limits of relationships before authority,
responsibility, capability, control, oversight, evidence, and
accountability analysis proceeds.

RBIM answers the question:

Where do the boundaries of the relationship begin and end?

Module Operational Space

RBIM governs:

  • relationship boundaries
  • relationship scope
  • governance boundaries
  • operational limits
  • participation limits
  • inclusion criteria
  • exclusion criteria
  • boundary traceability


The module applies wherever governance requires clear
relationship limits.

Module Function

The module ensures that governance can determine:

  • who is inside the relationship
  • who is outside the relationship
  • what activities are covered
  • what activities are excluded
  • where governance obligations start
  • where governance obligations stop
  • where relationship influence begins
  • where relationship influence ends


Its function is to prevent governance ambiguity caused by
unclear relationship boundaries.

Minimum Implementation Framework

1. Define Relationship Scope

Determine the intended scope of the relationship.

Examples:

  • employment relationship
  • contractor relationship
  • supplier relationship
  • supervisory relationship
  • Human-AI relationship
  • multi-agent relationship


2. Identify Included Actors

Identify all actors operating within the relationship boundary.

Examples:

  • individuals
  • organizations
  • contractors
  • subcontractors
  • AI agents
  • robotic systems


3. Identify Excluded Actors

Determine which actors remain outside the relationship boundary.

Governance must distinguish between:

  • participating actors
  • non-participating actors
  • indirect actors
  • external actors


4. Validate Boundary Accuracy

Determine whether documented boundaries reflect operational reality.

Governance must identify:

  • hidden extensions
  • undeclared participation
  • informal influence
  • boundary expansion
  • boundary contraction


5. Preserve Boundary Traceability

Maintain sufficient evidence to reconstruct relationship boundaries
throughout governance analysis.

Use Case 1 — Multi-Layer Contractor Environment

Scenario

A company contracts a supplier, who contracts a subcontractor, who
contracts a staffing agency.

Application

RBIM determines which actors fall within the operational
relationship boundary and where governance obligations extend.

Result

Governance gains a clear understanding of relationship limits across
the operational chain.

Use Case 2 — Human-AI Operational Environment

Scenario

A human operator supervises an orchestration system controlling
multiple AI agents and robotic systems.

Application

RBIM determines the governance boundaries of participation,
supervision, and operational influence.

Result

Governance gains visibility into the limits of the
operational relationship structure.

Canonical Closing Statement

Relationship Boundary Integrity Module (RBIM) defines the structural
conditions under which the boundaries, limits, scope, and governance
extent of relationships remain identifiable, distinguishable,
traceable, governable, and operationally valid throughout
the relationship lifecycle.

Governance cannot determine obligations if relationship boundaries
remain unclear. Relationship boundary integrity therefore becomes a
foundational condition of accountability governance.
