Relationship Accountability Standard (RAS) defines the structural
conditions under which operational relationships remain
identifiable, classifiable, traceable, accountable, and governable
throughout their lifecycle.

RAS governs the relationship layer of accountability. It establishes

→ Every accountability chain begins with a relationship.

About Standard

Canonical Definition

Relationship Accountability Standard (RAS) defines the structural
conditions under which operational, contractual, organizational,
supervisory, delegated, collaborative, autonomous, Human-AI, and
multi-party relationships remain identifiable, classifiable,
traceable, accountable, governable, and operationally valid
throughout their lifecycle.

RAS governs relationships.

The standard establishes the conditions required to determine the
actual operational relationship between actors before authority,
responsibility, control, oversight, evidence, accountability, and
consequence can be evaluated.

What This Standard Is

RAS is the foundational parent standard of AGA™.

It governs:

  • relationship identification
  • relationship classification
  • relationship boundaries
  • relationship continuity
  • relationship traceability
  • relationship accountability


RAS answers the first governance question:

What is the actual operational relationship between the actors?

What This Standard Is Not

RAS is not:

  • an authority standard
  • a responsibility standard
  • a control standard
  • an oversight standard
  • an accountability standard


RAS governs the relationship itself.

The remaining governance layers are evaluated only after the
relationship is identified.

Why This Standard Exists

Most governance failures begin with relationship misunderstanding.

Organizations frequently attempt to determine:

  • responsibility
  • authority
  • accountability
  • liability
  • compliance


before determining the true operational relationship.

This creates governance blindness.

A governance chain cannot be reliably reconstructed if the
underlying relationship remains unknown.

RAS exists to solve that problem.

Core Insight

Before asking who was responsible, governance must first determine
who was related to whom and how.

Operational Architecture Space

RAS defines the operational architecture space for:

  • relationship governance
  • relationship identification
  • relationship classification
  • relationship continuity
  • relationship traceability
  • relationship accountability


The space exists because every governance structure begins with
a relationship.

Without a relationship there can be no authority, responsibility,
control, oversight, evidence chain, or accountability chain.

Architectural Position

Within AGA™, RAS serves as the first governance layer.

The architecture flow begins:

Relationship → Authority → Responsibility → Capability → Control →
Oversight → Evidence → Accountability → Consequence

RAS governs the first step.

Foundational Principle

No authority exists without a relationship. No responsibility exists
without a relationship. No accountability exists without
a relationship.
